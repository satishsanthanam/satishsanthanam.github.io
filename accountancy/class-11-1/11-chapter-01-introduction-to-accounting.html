Class 11 (Part 1) — Chapter 1: Introduction to Accounting

Meaning and Process of Accounting

Accounting has evolved from simple financial record-keeping to a sophisticated information system. It identifies, measures, records, and communicates economic information to help users make informed decisions.

Definitions of Accounting

  • AICPA (1941): Defined accounting as the art of recording, classifying, and summarising in a significant manner and in terms of money, transactions and events which are, in part at least, of financial character, and interpreting the results thereof.
  • AAA (1966): Defined accounting as the process of identifying, measuring and communicating economic information to permit informed judgments and decisions by users of information.

The Accounting Process

The accounting process forms a continuous link between economic activities and decision-makers:

  1. Economic Events: Happenings of consequence to a business organization consisting of transactions measurable in monetary terms. These can be external (with outsiders) or internal (within the enterprise).
  2. Identification: Determining which transactions to record by observing and selecting activities of a financial character.
  3. Measurement: Quantification of business transactions in financial terms using a monetary unit (e.g., Rupees and Paise).
  4. Recording: Entering identified and measured transactions in the books of account in a chronological order.
  5. Communication: Disseminating compiled financial information through accounting reports to internal and external users.
History and Development of Accounting

Accounting practices are as old as civilization, evolving alongside commerce and governance:

  • Babylonia & Egypt (4000 B.C.): Recorded wage and tax payments on clay tablets.
  • Greece: Used accounting to apportion revenues, maintain receipts, and balance government transactions.
  • Rome: Maintained daybooks (memorandums) posted monthly to ledgers.
  • India (23 centuries ago): Kautilya, a minister in Chandragupta's kingdom, wrote Arthashasthra, detailing how accounting records should be maintained.
  • Luca Pacioli (1494): A Franciscan friar who published Summa de Arithmetica... in Venice, which is considered the first book on the double entry book-keeping system. He introduced the terms Debit (Dr.) and Credit (Cr.).
Branches and Qualitative Characteristics of Accounting

Branches of Accounting

  • Financial Accounting: Focuses on keeping systematic records of financial transactions to prepare financial reports (Profit & Loss Account, Balance Sheet) showing the financial performance and position of the business.
  • Cost Accounting: Analyzes expenditures to ascertain and control the cost of products manufactured or services rendered.
  • Management Accounting: Provides quantitative and qualitative, financial and non-financial information to assist management in planning, budgeting, and decision-making.

Qualitative Characteristics of Accounting Information

To be useful for decision-making, accounting information must possess the following attributes:

  • Reliability: Information must be dependable, free from error and bias, and faithfully represent what it claims to represent. It must be verifiable and neutral.
  • Relevance: Information must be available in time, help in making predictions or providing feedback, and influence user decisions.
  • Understandability: Information must be presented in a clear and intelligible manner so that decision-makers interpret it in the same sense as it was prepared.
  • Comparability: Reports must belong to a common period and use consistent formats and measurement units, allowing users to compare performance across different periods or entities.
Objectives and Role of Accounting

Objectives of Accounting

  1. Maintenance of Records: Keeping systematic, verifiable records of all financial transactions.
  2. Calculation of Profit and Loss: Determining the net results of operations periodically.
    $$\text{Profit} = \text{Revenues} - \text{Expenses}$$
  3. Depiction of Financial Position: Ascertaining assets and liabilities at the end of an accounting period via the Balance Sheet.
    $$\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Capital}$$
  4. Providing Information to Users: Disseminating financial data to internal users (management) and external users (investors, creditors, tax authorities, etc.).

Role of Accounting

Accounting plays multiple roles in modern society:

  • As a Language of Business to communicate enterprise information.
  • As a Historical Record of chronological financial transactions.
  • As a measure of Current Economic Reality.
  • As an Information System linking sources to receivers.
  • As a Commodity providing specialized, in-demand services.
Basic Accounting Terms

Understanding the fundamental terminology of accounting is essential:

Key Definitions

  • Entity: A reality that has a definite individual existence (e.g., a business enterprise).
  • Transaction: An event involving some value between two or more entities (cash or credit).
  • Capital: The amount invested by the owner in the firm, representing an obligation/claim on assets.
  • Sales: Total revenues from goods or services sold or provided to customers.
  • Revenues: Amounts earned by selling products, providing services, or from investments (interest, dividends, commission).
  • Expenses: Costs incurred in the process of earning revenue (e.g., rent, wages, salaries, depreciation).
  • Expenditure: Spending money or incurring a liability for some benefit. It is classified as Capital Expenditure (benefit lasts > 1 year) or Revenue Expenditure (benefit exhausted within 1 year).
  • Profit: Excess of revenues over related expenses during an accounting year.
  • Gain: A profit arising from incidental transactions (e.g., sale of fixed assets, winning a court case).
  • Loss: Excess of expenses over related revenues, or money/worth lost without return (e.g., theft, fire).
  • Discount: Deduction in price. Can be Trade Discount (percentage off list price at sale) or Cash Discount (incentive for prompt payment).
  • Voucher: Documentary evidence in support of a transaction (e.g., cash memo, invoice, receipt).
  • Goods: Products in which the business unit deals.
  • Drawings: Withdrawal of money or goods by the owner for personal use.
  • Purchases: Total amount of goods procured for resale or manufacturing.
  • Stock (Inventory): Measure of goods, spares, and other items on hand (Opening and Closing Stock).
  • Debtors: Persons or entities who owe money to the enterprise for buying goods/services on credit.
  • Creditors: Persons or entities who must be paid by the enterprise for providing goods/services on credit.
