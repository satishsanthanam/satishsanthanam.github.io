Accountancy: Class 11 (Part 1) — Chapter 5: Bank Reconciliation Statement

Bank Reconciliation Statement

A Bank Reconciliation Statement is prepared to reconcile the difference between the bank balance shown by the Cash Book and the balance shown by the bank Passbook (or Bank Statement) on a particular date.

5.1 Need for Reconciliation

The balance of the bank column in the cash book must tally with the balance shown in the bank passbook. However, in practice, these balances often differ. Reconciling them is necessary to:

  • Ascertain the accuracy of entries in both books.
  • Identify any errors or omissions in the Cash Book or Passbook.
  • Prevent fraud and embezzlement.
  • Keep track of cheques sent for collection and cheques issued but not presented.

5.1.1 Timing Differences

Differences often arise due to the time gap in recording transactions. Key factors include:

  • Cheques issued but not yet presented for payment: Entered immediately in the Cash Book (credit side), but debited by the bank only when presented.
  • Cheques paid into the bank but not yet collected: Debited immediately in the Cash Book, but credited by the bank only after realization.
  • Direct debits made by the bank: Bank charges, interest on overdraft, or unpaid/dishonoured cheques debited by the bank without immediate entry in the Cash Book.
  • Amounts directly deposited in the bank account: Deposited directly by customers, credited in the Passbook but not recorded in the Cash Book until the statement is received.
  • Interest and dividends collected by the bank: Credited in the Passbook but not recorded in the Cash Book.
  • Direct payments made by the bank: Standing instructions (e.g., insurance premium, rent) debited by the bank but not recorded in the Cash Book.
  • Cheques deposited/bills discounted dishonoured: Debited by the bank upon dishonour, but no immediate entry in the Cash Book.

5.1.2 Differences Caused by Errors

Errors can be committed by either the business firm or the bank:

  • Errors by the firm: Omission or wrong recording of cheques issued/deposited, or wrong totaling in the Cash Book.
  • Errors by the bank: Omission or wrong recording of transactions, or wrong posting in the Passbook.

5.2 Proforma of Bank Reconciliation Statement

A Bank Reconciliation Statement can be prepared using two main formats:

Format 1: Add/Less Presentation

Particulars Amount (Rs.)
Balance as per Cash Book .......
Add: Cheques issued but not presented .......
Add: Interest credited by the bank .......
Less: Cheques deposited but not credited by the bank (.......)
Less: Bank charges not recorded in the cash book (.......)
Balance as per Passbook xxxx

Format 2: Plus (+) and Minus (-) Columns

Particulars Amount (+) (Rs.) Amount (-) (Rs.)
Balance as per Cash Book ......
Cheques issued but not presented ......
Interest credited by the bank ......
Cheque deposited but not credited by the bank ......
Bank charges not recorded in the cash book ......
Balance as per Passbook xxxx

5.2.1 Preparation of Statement under Different Situations

There are four distinct starting points for preparing a Bank Reconciliation Statement:

  1. Debit balance (favourable) as per Cash Book: Starting with a positive cash book balance to find the passbook balance.
  2. Credit balance (favourable) as per Passbook: Starting with a positive passbook balance to find the cash book balance.
  3. Credit balance (unfavourable/overdraft) as per Cash Book: Starting with a negative cash book balance.
  4. Debit balance (unfavourable/overdraft) as per Passbook: Starting with a negative passbook balance.

Note on Overdrafts: An Overdraft represents a negative bank balance where the business has borrowed funds from the bank. It is treated as a negative figure in the reconciliation statement.
