Accountancy: Class 12 (Part 2) — Chapter 1: Accounting for Share Capital

1.1 Features and Kinds of a Company

A company is an association of persons who contribute money or money's worth to a common inventory and use it for a common purpose. It is an artificial person having a corporate legal entity distinct from its members, with perpetual succession and a common seal.

Salient Features of a Company
  • Body Corporate: Formed and registered under the Companies Law.
  • Separate Legal Entity: Distinct from its members; can hold property and enter contracts in its own name.
  • Limited Liability: Liability of members is limited to the unpaid amount of shares held or the guarantee given.
  • Perpetual Succession: Continuous existence unaffected by death, insanity, or insolvency of members.
  • Common Seal: Acts as the official signature of the company.
  • Transferability of Shares: Shares of a public limited company are freely transferable.
  • May Sue or be Sued: Can enforce legal rights and be legally held liable in its own name.

Kinds of Companies
  1. On the Basis of Liability:
    • Companies Limited by Shares: Liability limited to the nominal value of shares held.
    • Companies Limited by Guarantee: Liability limited to the amount members undertake to contribute during winding up.
    • Unlimited Companies: No limit on the liability of members (rare in India).
  2. On the Basis of Number of Members:
    • Public Company: Not a private company; minimum 7 members, no maximum limit.
    • Private Company: Restricts share transfer; minimum 2 members, maximum 200 (excluding employees).
    • One Person Company (OPC): Only one person as a member; paid-up capital $\le$ Rs. 50 Lakhs, average annual turnover $\le$ Rs. 2 Crores.
1.2 Share Capital of a Company

The capital of a company is collected from several persons called shareholders, and the total amount contributed is merged into a common Share Capital Account.

Categories of Share Capital
  • Authorised/Nominal Capital: Maximum amount of share capital a company is authorised to issue by its Memorandum of Association.
  • Issued Capital: Part of authorised capital offered to the public for subscription.
  • Subscribed Capital: Part of issued capital actually subscribed by the public.
    • Subscribed and Fully Paid-up: Entire face value called and paid.
    • Subscribed but not Fully Paid-up: Called up but not fully paid, or face value not fully called.
  • Called-up Capital: Part of subscribed capital called by the company to pay.
  • Paid-up Capital: Actual amount received from shareholders.

Accounting Equation:

$$\text{Paid-up Capital} = \text{Called-up Capital} - \text{Calls in Arrears}$$
  • Reserve Capital: Portion of uncalled capital reserved to be called only in the event of winding up.

Classes of Shares
  • Preference Shares: Carry preferential rights to receive dividends at a fixed rate and repayment of capital on winding up before equity shares. Types include Cumulative/Non-cumulative, Participating/Non-participating, and Redeemable/Irredeemable.
  • Equity Shares: Shares that do not enjoy preferential rights; they receive dividends based on available profits and carry voting rights.
1.3 Issue and Allotment of Shares

The process of issuing shares involves three main steps: issuing a prospectus, receiving applications, and making the allotment of shares.

Minimum Subscription

According to SEBI guidelines, minimum subscription cannot be less than 90% of the issued amount. If this condition is not satisfied, the company must refund the entire subscription amount within the prescribed period (usually 8 days from closure of subscription), failing which interest at 15% p.a. is applicable.

1.4 Special Accounting Scenarios

Calls in Arrears and Calls in Advance
  • Calls in Arrears: Unpaid called-up capital. Interest is charged as per Table F at a rate not exceeding 10% p.a.
  • Calls in Advance: Amount paid by shareholders before it is called up. Interest is paid as per Table F at a rate not exceeding 12% p.a.

Over Subscription and Pro-rata Allotment

When applications exceed the shares offered, it is a case of Over Subscription. Directors have three alternatives:

  1. Reject excess applications and refund money.
  2. Make pro-rata allotment (proportionate allotment) to all applicants, adjusting excess application money towards allotment/calls.
  3. A combination of rejection and pro-rata allotment.

Issue of Shares at a Premium

Shares issued at a price higher than nominal value are issued at a premium. The excess is credited to the Securities Premium Account, which can only be used for:

  • Issuing fully paid bonus shares.
  • Writing off preliminary expenses.
  • Writing off commission paid or discount allowed on issue of securities.
  • Providing for premium payable on redemption of preference shares or debentures.
  • Buy-back of own shares.

Issue of Shares for Consideration Other than Cash

When assets are purchased and paid for by issuing shares:

$$\text{Number of Shares to be Issued} = \frac{\text{Amount Payable to Vendor}}{\text{Issue Price per Share}}$$
1.5 Forfeiture and Reissue of Shares

Forfeiture of Shares

Forfeiture refers to the cancellation of allotment and confiscation of the amount already paid due to non-payment of calls.

Journal Entry (Issued at Par):

Share Capital A/c (Called-up Amount) Dr.
  To Share Forfeiture A/c (Amount Paid)
  To Share Allotment A/c (Unpaid Allotment)
  To Share Call A/c (Unpaid Call)

Reissue of Forfeited Shares

Forfeited shares can be reissued at par, premium, or discount. The discount allowed on reissue cannot exceed the amount forfeited on those shares. Any remaining balance in the Share Forfeiture Account after reissue is transferred to the Capital Reserve Account.

Journal Entry for Transfer of Profit:

Share Forfeiture A/c Dr.
  To Capital Reserve A/c
