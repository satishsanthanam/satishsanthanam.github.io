Class 121 — Chapter 3: Use of Spreadsheet in Business Applications

Introduction & Learning Objectives

Spreadsheets are powerful tools for business applications. This chapter covers the application of spreadsheets (using Excel) to Payroll Accounting, Asset Management, and Loan Repayment.

Learning Objectives

  • Computing employee's gross and net salary for Payroll Accounting.
  • Calculation of Depreciation by various methods.
  • Loan repayment and interest calculation.
3.1 Payroll Accounting

Every employee is paid salary on a pre-determined date within the framework of an employee contract. The computation of salary is based on attendance, grade of pay, allowances, and deductions.

3.1.1 Payroll Components

Salary computation involves the following elements:

  • Earnings:
    • Basic Pay (BP): Core pay in the pay scale plus Grade Pay.
    • Grade Pay (GP): Pay added according to designation.
    • Dearness Pay (DP): Portion of Dearness Allowance merged with Basic Pay.
    • Dearness Allowance (DA): Compensation for cost-of-living increases.
    • House Rent Allowance (HRA): Allowance for housing.
    • Transport Allowance (TRA): Allowance for commuting.
  • Deductions:
    • Professional Tax (PT): State-level statutory deduction.
    • Provident Fund (PF): Social security contribution.
    • Tax Deduction at Source (TDS): Monthly income tax apportionment.
    • Recovery of Loan (LOAN): Installment recovery for loans taken.

3.1.2 Elements Used in Payroll Calculation

The mathematical formulas used for payroll calculations are:

Number of Effective Days Present (NOEDP):

$$NOEDP = NODM - \text{Leave Without Pay} - \text{Unauthorised Absence}$$

Basic Pay Earned (BPE):

$$BPE = BP \times \frac{NOEDP}{NODM}$$

Total Earnings (TE):

$$TE = BPE + DA + HRA + TRA$$

Total Deductions (TD):

$$TD = PF + TDS + LOAN$$

Net Salary (NS):

$$NS = TE - TD$$

3.2 Asset Accounting

Asset Accounting involves tracking fixed assets and computing Depreciation over their useful lives.

3.2.1 Introduction to Depreciation

Depreciation is the systematic allocation of the cost of a physical asset over its useful life.

$$\text{Total Depreciable Amount} = \text{Acquisition Cost} - \text{Salvage Value}$$

3.2.2.1 Straight Line Method (SLM)

Under the Straight Line Method, depreciation is constant across periods.

$$\text{Straight Line Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Total Depreciable Amount}}{\text{Expected Useful Life}}$$

Excel Built-in Function:

=SLN(cost, salvage, life)

3.2.2.2 Written Down Value (WDV) Method

The Written Down Value method (or Declining Balance method) computes depreciation at a decreasing rate based on the book value at the start of the period.

Excel Built-in Function:

=DB(cost, salvage, life, period, [month])
ParameterExplanation
CostInitial cost of the asset
SalvageValue at the end of the useful life
LifeNumber of periods over which the asset is depreciated
PeriodThe period for which you want to calculate the depreciation
MonthNumber of months in the first year (optional, defaults to 12)
3.3 Loan Repayment Schedule

A Loan Repayment Schedule details the periodic payments of principal and interest over the loan term.

3.3.1 Calculating Interest and Repayments

Excel's built-in function PMT is used to calculate the periodic payment for a loan based on constant payments and a constant interest rate.

=PMT(rate, nper, pv, [fv], [type])
ParameterExplanation
RateInterest rate per period for the loan
NperTotal number of payments for the loan
PvPresent value (the total amount that a series of future payments is worth now; principal)
FvFuture value, or a cash balance you want to attain after the last payment is made (defaults to 0)
TypeWhether payment is made at the beginning (1) or end (0) of the period
