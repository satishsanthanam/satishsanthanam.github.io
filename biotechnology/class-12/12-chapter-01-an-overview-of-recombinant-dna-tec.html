Biotechnology: Class 12 — Chapter 1: An Overview of Recombinant DNA Technology

1.1 Introduction to Recombinant DNA Technology

In the last century, the discovery of nucleic acid (DNA) as the principal molecule responsible for the expression of hereditary characters led to direct attempts to alter the genetic makeup of organisms. These methods are collectively referred to as recombinant DNA (rDNA) technology or genetic engineering.

Core Concepts & Foundations
  • rDNA Technology: The collection of techniques used to directly manipulate the genome/nucleic acids of an organism to alter its genetic makeup.
  • Gene Cloning: The fundamental theme of rDNA technology involving the isolation and propagation of a desired DNA molecule (gene) from a source to obtain its product in ample quantity.
  • Key Milestones: Isolation and purification of nucleic acids, determination of the double-helical structure of DNA, and understanding the central dogma processes (replication, transcription, and translation).
Pioneers and Key Enzymatic Discoveries

The development of genetic engineering was made possible by combining key discoveries in bacterial genetics and enzymatic manipulation of DNA.

The Cohen-Boyer Collaboration

Traditional genetic manipulation was limited by species barriers, preventing the precise transfer of individual functional genes between unrelated organisms.

Engineering Methodology & Mechanism: In 1973, Stanley Cohen (expert in introducing plasmid DNA into Escherichia coli) and Herbert Boyer (expert in cleaving double-stranded DNA with restriction enzymes to produce cohesive, single-stranded termini) combined their methodologies. They cut open a plasmid loop from one bacterial species, inserted a foreign gene from a different species, and sealed the loop to create the first functional recombinant plasmid, yielding the world's first genetically modified organism.

Essential Toolkits & Enzymes
  • Restriction Enzymes: Discovered by Werner Arber, Hamilton Smith, and Daniel Nathans (late 1960s–early 1970s). These act as molecular scissors to cut DNA molecules at specific target sequences.
  • DNA Ligase: Discovered by Gellert, Lehman, Richardson, and Hurwitz (1967). This enzyme acts as molecular glue to covalently join two DNA fragments together.
  • Plasmids: Discovered by Joshua Lederberg (1952). These are extrachromosomal, self-replicating circular DNA molecules used as vectors to carry foreign genetic material into host cells.
Applications of rDNA Technology

Recombinant DNA technology has revolutionized medicine, therapeutics, and agriculture by overcoming the limitations of natural resource extraction.

Therapeutic and Medical Advancements
  • Insulin (Humulin): Historically, obtaining insulin required processing tons of animal pancreatic glands, which was expensive, limited in yield, and caused immunogenic allergic reactions in humans. rDNA technology enabled the expression of pure human insulin in bacterial systems.
  • Human Growth Hormone: Previously extracted in minute quantities from human cadaver pituitary glands to treat dwarfism; now safely synthesized via recombinant bacterial systems.
  • Interferons: Produced recombinantly to assist in cancer therapies and viral infections.
  • Clot-Dissolving Enzymes: Recombinant plasminogen activator and urokinase are synthesized to dissolve coronary blood clots.

Agricultural Advancements
  • Genetically Modified (GM) Crops: Developed by introducing targeted modifications into plant genomes to confer resistance to pests, diseases, and environmental stresses.
  • Abiotic Stress Tolerance: Engineering crops capable of surviving drought, high salinity, and extreme temperatures to ensure food security in adverse environments.
  • Molecular Farming: The emerging field of using plants as bioreactors to produce therapeutic proteins, peptides, and hormones, offering cost-effective and contamination-free alternatives to animal-based production systems.
Chronological History of Biotechnology

The timeline below outlines the landmark discoveries and developments that shaped modern biotechnology:

Year Pioneer(s) / Discoverer(s) Landmark Development / Discovery
1917 Karl Ereky Coined the term 'Biotechnology'.
1944 Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty Demonstrated that DNA is the genetic material.
1952 Joshua Lederberg Discovered plasmids.
1953 Watson and Crick Proposed the double-helical structure of DNA.
1960s Werner Arber, Matthew Meselson Discovered Type I restriction enzymes.
1967 Gellert, Lehman, Richardson, and Hurwitz Discovered ligase enzymes.
1970 Hamilton O. Smith and Thomas J. Kelly Discovered Type II restriction enzymes.
1972 Paul Berg Assembled the first recombinant DNA molecule (bacterial DNA into a virus).
1973 Stanley N. Cohen and Herbert Boyer Developed DNA cloning and recombinant DNA technology.
1975 Georges J.F. Köhler and César Milstein Described hybridoma technology for monoclonal antibody production.
1982 Eli Lilly and Genentech FDA approved the world's first recombinant therapeutic product, Humulin (human insulin).
1983 Kary Mullis Developed the Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR).
1984 Sir Alec Jeffreys Invented DNA fingerprinting.
1986 Chiron / Merck First recombinant vaccine, Recombivax HB (for Hepatitis B), approved for human use.
1990 U.S. DOE and NIH Officially initiated the Human Genome Project (HGP).
1994 Calgene Introduced the first genetically engineered crop, the Flavr Savr tomato.
1996 Keith Campbell and Ian Wilmut Cloned the first mammal, Dolly the sheep, from a somatic cell using nuclear transfer.
1996 Monsanto Developed and commercially released Bt cotton.
2000 Ingo Potrykus and Peter Beyer Developed Golden Rice (provitamin A-enriched).
2003 International Consortium Completed the Human Genome Project.
2004 Genentech / Roche Developed Avastin (anti-VEGF monoclonal antibody) for cancer treatment.
2006 Merck Recombinant vaccine Gardasil (against HPV) received FDA approval.
2006 Andrew Fire and Craig Mello Nobel Prize awarded for the discovery of RNA interference (RNAi) / gene silencing.
2010 Robert Edwards Nobel Prize awarded for the development of in vitro fertilization (IVF) therapy.
2012 Shinya Yamanaka and John B. Gurdon Discovered that mature differentiated cells can be reprogrammed into induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs).
2019 Emmanuelle Charpentier and Jennifer Doudna Nobel Prize awarded for the discovery of the CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing tool.
2020 Various Developers Developed recombinant and mRNA vaccines against COVID-19.
2023 Katalin Karikó and Drew Weissman Nobel Prize awarded for nucleoside base modifications enabling effective mRNA vaccines against COVID-19.
