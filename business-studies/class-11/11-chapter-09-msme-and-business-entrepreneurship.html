Business Studies: Class 11 — Chapter 9: MSME and Business Entrepreneurship

Introduction to MSMEs

Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) contribute significantly to the economic development of India, acting as a vital link in industrialisation, employment generation, and export promotion.

Economic Contribution

  • Contribute approximately 29.7% of GDP.
  • Account for nearly 49.66% of exports.
  • Provide employment to over 60 million people across 28.5 million enterprises, making it the second-largest employer after agriculture.
  • Act as ancillary units complementary to large-scale industries.

Business Case Study: Romi Bags of Manipur

Business Case Title: Romi Bags of Manipur

Corporate Context/Problem: Khumbongmayum Dhanachandra Singh, the son of a poor tailor in Imphal, Manipur, faced severe financial constraints and lacked access to higher education or capital. He wanted to escape the cycle of low-income tailoring but had only one sewing machine and leftover fabric scraps.

Strategic Action Taken: He designed and stitched a unique purse from leftover fabric scraps. After positive feedback from friends, he identified a market demand, formulated a business plan, and launched "Romi Bags" in 1996, carefully calculating costs, expenses, and expected income.

Managerial Inference: Entrepreneurship does not require massive initial capital; rather, it relies on identifying niche market opportunities, utilizing local resources, and demonstrating grit, perseverance, and systematic planning.

Village and Cottage Industries

  • Village Industries: Located in rural areas, producing goods or rendering services with or without power, where fixed capital investment per head is specified by the central government.
  • Cottage Industries: Also known as traditional or rural industries, operated by family members using manual labor and local resources, not defined by rigid capital investment criteria.
Role and Significance of MSMEs

Key Contributions to the Economy

  • Balanced Regional Development: Small industries account for 95% of industrial units, allowing industrial benefits to reach rural and backward areas.
  • Employment Generation: Highly labor-intensive, generating more employment opportunities per unit of capital invested compared to large industries.
  • Product Variety: Produce a vast range of goods from mass consumption items (stationery, garments, soaps) to sophisticated electronic and electro-medical equipment.
  • Low Cost of Production: Benefit from locally available resources, lower overhead expenses, and low establishment costs.
  • Quick Decision Making: Small organizational size allows rapid, flexible, and timely decision-making.
Problems Faced by MSMEs

Major Operational Challenges

  • Finance: Severe shortage of working capital, low creditworthiness, lack of collateral security for bank loans, and exploitation by local money lenders.
  • Raw Materials: Low bargaining power due to small purchase quantities, high prices for quality materials, and lack of storage facilities.
  • Managerial Skills: Typically run by a single owner who may lack specialized functional expertise in marketing, finance, or operations.
  • Marketing: Excessive dependence on exploitative middlemen, lack of direct marketing infrastructure, and limited brand recognition.
  • Quality Standards: Inability to invest in research and development (R&D) or upgrade technology, leading to difficulties in meeting global quality standards.
  • Capacity Utilisation: Operating below full capacity due to weak demand or poor marketing, leading to business sickness and closure.
  • Global Competition: Intense competition from large-scale domestic industries and multinational corporations (MNCs).
Entrepreneurship Development & Startup India

Entrepreneurship is the systematic process of setting up one's own business enterprise.

The Entrepreneurship Triad

  • Entrepreneur: The person (the subject) who initiates and builds the business.
  • Entrepreneurship: The process (the verb) of starting and managing the venture.
  • Enterprise: The actual business unit (the object) created as a result of the process.

Characteristics of Entrepreneurship

  1. Systematic Activity: It is a structured, step-by-step process requiring specific skills and training, dispelling the myth that entrepreneurs are born, not made.
  2. Lawful and Purposeful Activity: Aimed at legitimate business operations to create personal profit and social value.
  3. Innovation: Involves introducing new products, discovering new markets, or implementing cost-saving production methods.
  4. Organisation of Production: Mobilizing and coordinating land, labor, capital, and technology to create a productive firm.
  5. Risk-taking: Assuming calculated risks rather than gambling, driven by confidence in one's capabilities.

Startup India Scheme

An initiative by the Government of India to build a strong ecosystem for nurturing innovation and startups. Key objectives include:

  • Triggering an entrepreneurial culture and shifting mindsets.
  • Creating awareness about entrepreneurship as a viable career option.
  • Encouraging youth, scientists, and technologists to launch startups.
  • Promoting inclusive development by supporting underrepresented groups (women, backward communities).
Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)

Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) are legal rights protecting creations of the human mind, allowing creators to control and monetize their intangible assets.

Types of Intellectual Property

  • Copyright: The exclusive right to "not copy" original literary, artistic, or dramatic works (e.g., books, music, films). Protection is automatic upon creation.
  • Trademark: A distinctive word, name, or symbol identifying and distinguishing products of one company from another. Can be conventional (logos, labels) or non-conventional (sound, smell).
  • Geographical Indication (GI): Identifies products originating from a specific territory possessing unique qualities or reputation (e.g., Darjeeling Tea, Kashmir Pashmina).
  • Patent: An exclusive right granted for scientific inventions (products or processes) that are new, non-obvious, and capable of industrial application. Valid for 20 years.
  • Design: Protects the aesthetic appearance, shape, pattern, or color combination applied to an article. Valid for 10 years (extendable by 5).
  • Plant Variety: Protection for newly bred plant varieties to encourage agricultural R&D.
  • Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout Design: Protects the layout designs of computer chips and transistors.
  • Traditional Knowledge (TK): Ancient community wisdom (e.g., Ayurveda, Yoga) protected from wrongful patenting via the Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL).
  • Trade Secrets: Confidential business information providing a competitive edge (e.g., Coca-Cola recipe), protected under the Indian Contract Act, 1872.
