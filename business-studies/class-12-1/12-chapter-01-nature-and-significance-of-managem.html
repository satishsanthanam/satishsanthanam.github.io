Class 12 (Part 1) — Chapter 1: Nature and Significance of Management

Introduction & Concept of Management

Management is essential for all organisations, whether big or small, profit or non-profit, services or manufacturing. It is the process of getting things done with the aim of achieving goals effectively and efficiently.

Business Case Title: Management at Tata Steel

Corporate Context/Problem: Establishing a massive global business conglomerate while maintaining strong values, employee welfare, and social responsibility.

Strategic Action Taken: Implemented pioneering employee benefits (gratuity, provident fund) before they were legally mandated, planned the city of Jamshedpur meticulously, and adopted the Tata Code of Conduct (TCOC).

Managerial Inference: Effective and efficient management at all levels is essential to build, sustain, and run large-scale enterprises profitably and responsibly.

Business Case Title: Namchi Designer Candles

Corporate Context/Problem: Smita Rai, an entrepreneur in South Sikkim, wanted to solve the problem of joblessness and poverty among local women by utilizing her candle-making skills.

Strategic Action Taken: Partnered with NEDFI for skill development and financial support to establish a 100% women-run candle manufacturing venture.

Managerial Inference: Successful entrepreneurship and organization require a series of continuous, interrelated management functions (planning, organizing, staffing, directing, and controlling).

Effectiveness versus Efficiency

For management, it is important to be both effective and efficient. They are two sides of the same coin, but they must be balanced.

Characteristics of Management

The basic characteristics of management are:

  • Goal-oriented process: Unites the efforts of different individuals in the organisation towards achieving common goals.
  • All pervasive: Activities involved in managing an enterprise are common to all organisations, whether economic, social, or political.
  • Multidimensional: Management is complex and has three main dimensions:
    • Management of work: Translates work in terms of goals to be achieved and assigns the means to achieve it.
    • Management of people: Managing employees as individuals with diverse needs and as a group of people.
    • Management of operations: Managing the production process that transforms inputs into desired outputs.
  • Continuous process: A series of continuous, composite, but separate functions performed simultaneously by all managers.
  • Group activity: Requires teamwork and coordination of individual effort in a common direction.
  • Dynamic function: Must adapt itself to the changing external environment (social, economic, and political factors).
  • Intangible force: Its presence cannot be seen but can be felt in the way the organisation functions (orderliness, met targets, happy employees).
Objectives of Management

Management seeks to achieve certain objectives, which can be classified into three categories:

1. Organisational Objectives

Aims at utilizing human and material resources to fulfill the economic objectives of a business:

  • Survival: Earning enough revenues to cover costs.
  • Profit: Providing a vital incentive for the continued successful operation of the enterprise.
  • Growth: Exploiting growth potential, measured by sales volume, number of employees, or capital investment.

2. Social Objectives

Involves the creation of benefit for society, such as using environmental-friendly production methods, giving employment to underprivileged sections, and providing basic amenities like schools and healthcare.

Business Case Title: ITC - Empowering Rural India

Corporate Context/Problem: Rural Indian farmers faced challenges due to unreliable electricity, lack of direct marketing channels, and high transaction costs.

Strategic Action Taken: ITC created the E-Choupal initiative, an IT-based intervention providing real-time information, customized knowledge, and direct marketing channels to farmers.

Managerial Inference: Business organisations can fulfill corporate social responsibility while enhancing operational efficiency and empowering marginalized communities.

3. Personal / Personnel Objectives

Aims to satisfy the diverse needs of employees, including financial needs (competitive salaries), social needs (peer recognition), and higher-level needs (personal growth and development).

Importance of Management

Management is important for the following reasons:

  • Helps in achieving group goals: Gives a common direction to individual efforts.
  • Increases efficiency: Reduces costs and increases productivity through better planning, organising, directing, staffing, and controlling.
  • Creates a dynamic organisation: Helps people adapt to changes so the organisation maintains its competitive edge.
  • Helps in achieving personal objectives: Motivates and leads teams so individuals achieve personal goals while contributing to organisational objectives.
  • Helps in the development of society: Provides quality products, creates employment, and adopts new technology.
Nature of Management

Management as an Art

Art is the skillful and personal application of existing knowledge to achieve desired results. Management is an art because it satisfies these criteria:

  • Existence of theoretical knowledge: Based on a lot of literature and theories in marketing, finance, and human resources.
  • Personalised application: Every manager applies scientific methods and knowledge in their own unique, personalized manner.
  • Based on practice and creativity: A manager achieves perfection through continuous practice, imagination, and innovation.

Management as a Science

Science is a systematised body of knowledge that explains general truths. Management has features of science but is considered an inexact science because:

  • Systematised body of knowledge: Has its own theory and principles, drawing from economics, sociology, and psychology.
  • Principles based on experimentation: Principles are developed through observation and repeated experimentation, though human behavior cannot be perfectly predicted.
  • Universal validity: Unlike pure science, management principles do not have universal validity and must be modified according to the situation.

Management as a Profession

A profession has specialized characteristics. Management does not meet the exact criteria of a full-fledged profession but is moving towards it:

  • Well-defined body of knowledge: Satisfied. Management is based on a systematic body of knowledge taught at specialized institutes like IIMs.
  • Restricted entry: Not strictly met. Anyone can be designated as a manager irrespective of educational qualifications, though professional degrees are preferred.
  • Professional association: Partially met. Associations like AIMA exist, but membership is not legally compulsory.
  • Ethical code of conduct: Partially met. AIMA has laid down a code of conduct, but it lacks statutory backing.
  • Service motive: Fast changing. While profit maximisation remains a goal, serving society by providing quality goods at reasonable prices is increasingly recognized.
Levels of Management

The authority-responsibility relationship binds individuals as superiors and subordinates, giving rise to three levels of management:

  1. Top Management: Consists of senior-most executives (Chairman, CEO, President). They integrate diverse elements, coordinate departments, formulate overall goals, and are responsible for the survival and welfare of the organisation.
  2. Middle Management: Consists of division heads (Production Manager, Marketing Manager). They act as a link between top and lower-level managers, interpret policies, ensure departmental personnel, assign duties, and motivate employees.
  3. Supervisory or Operational Management: Consists of foremen and supervisors. They directly oversee the workforce, ensure quality of output, minimize wastage, maintain safety standards, and pass on instructions to workers.
Functions of Management

The management process consists of five sequential, interrelated functions:

  1. Planning: Determining in advance what is to be done and who is to do it, setting goals, and developing a path to achieve them.
  2. Organising: Assigning duties, grouping tasks, establishing authority, and allocating resources required to carry out a specific plan.
  3. Staffing: Finding the right people for the right job, involving recruitment, selection, placement, and training.
  4. Directing: Leading, influencing, and motivating employees to perform the tasks assigned to them.
  5. Controlling: Monitoring organisational performance, measuring current performance against standards, and taking corrective action.
Coordination — The Essence of Management

Coordination is the process of synchronising the activities of different departments to ensure unity of action in the realization of common objectives. It is not a separate function, but the very essence of management.

Characteristics of Coordination

  • Integrates group efforts: Unifies diverse interests into purposeful work activity.
  • Ensures unity of action: Acts as the binding force between departments.
  • Continuous process: Begins at the planning stage and continues till controlling.
  • All pervasive function: Required at all levels and in all departments due to interdependence.
  • Responsibility of all managers: Top, middle, and operational managers must coordinate.
  • Deliberate function: A manager must coordinate efforts in a conscious and deliberate manner.

Importance of Coordination

  • Growth in size: As an organisation grows, coordination harmonizes individual goals with organisational goals.
  • Functional differentiation: Reconciles differences in approach and interest among separate departments (e.g., marketing vs. finance).
  • Specialisation: Reconciles differences in approach, interest, or opinion among highly specialized experts.

Business Case Title: Dabbawallas of Mumbai

Corporate Context/Problem: Delivering hot, home-cooked lunches to thousands of office workers daily across Mumbai with negligible technology and low capital.

Strategic Action Taken: Created a highly coordinated, multi-stage logistics system where different groups of dabbawallas independently yet network-dependently sort, transport, and deliver tiffins using cycles and local trains.

Managerial Inference: Flawless coordination, punctuality, and teamwork can achieve world-class operational excellence (Six Sigma) even without complex technological infrastructure.
