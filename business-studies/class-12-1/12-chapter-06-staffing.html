Business Studies: Class 12 (Part 1) — Chapter 6: Staffing

Introduction & Meaning of Staffing

The foundation of any organisation is its talented and hardworking people, who are its principal assets. Staffing has been described as the managerial function of filling and keeping filled the positions in the organisation structure.

Definition and Core Concepts

In the simplest terms, staffing is putting people to jobs. It begins with workforce planning and includes recruitment, selection, training, development, promotion, compensation, and performance appraisal.

  • Continuous Process: In an existing enterprise, staffing is a continuous process because new jobs are created and existing employees leave.
  • Individual Focus: Staffing recognises the importance of every single person employed, as the individual worker is the ultimate performer.
Business Case Title: Management of Human Resources at INFOSYS
Corporate Context/Problem: How to attract, retain, and develop human assets in a highly competitive, knowledge-intensive IT environment where competitors constantly covet talent.
Strategic Action Taken: Infosys includes human resources on its balance sheet to value their strategic importance. Under Narayana Murthy, they established a culture of closeness, wealth-sharing, and empowerment.
Managerial Inference: Long-term corporate success in knowledge-based industries depends on treating human resources as strategic assets rather than mere operational costs.
Importance of Staffing

Human resources are the foundation of any business. Proper staffing is a critical driver of organisational performance and provides several key benefits:

  • Discovering Competent Personnel: Helps in finding and obtaining talented workers for various jobs.
  • Higher Performance: Ensures the right person is placed on the right job, leading to improved efficiency.
  • Survival and Growth: Guarantees the continuous growth of the enterprise through succession planning for managers.
  • Optimum Utilisation of HR: Avoids overmanning, thereby preventing under-utilisation of personnel and reducing high labour costs.
  • Employee Satisfaction: Improves job satisfaction and morale through objective assessment and fair rewards.
Staffing as Part of Human Resource Management (HRM)

Staffing is a separate and specialised function that deals with the human element of management. As organisations grow, a dedicated Human Resource Department is established to manage people.

Evolution of HRM

HRM has evolved through several historical stages:

  1. Labour Welfare Officer: Early stage focused on bare minimum welfare activities for workers.
  2. Personnel Manager: Emerged with the factory system, handling recruitment, selection, and placement.
  3. Human Resource Manager: Modern approach treating people as valuable resources that can be continuously developed.

Specialised Duties of HR Personnel

  • Recruitment and searching for qualified people.
  • Analysing jobs and preparing job descriptions.
  • Developing compensation and incentive plans.
  • Training and development for career growth.
  • Maintaining labour and union relations.
  • Handling grievances and complaints.
  • Providing social security and welfare.
  • Defending the company in lawsuits.
The Staffing Process

The staffing function involves a logical sequence of steps designed to acquire and retain the right workforce:

  1. Estimating the Manpower Requirements: Determining the number and type of personnel needed. This involves Workload Analysis (assessing required human resources) and Workforce Analysis (assessing existing personnel).
  2. Recruitment: Searching for prospective employees and stimulating them to apply.
  3. Selection: Choosing the best candidate from the pool of applicants.
  4. Placement and Orientation: Introducing the selected employee to the workplace, rules, and colleagues, and placing them in their designated role.
  5. Training and Development: Providing opportunities to facilitate employee learning and career growth.
  6. Performance Appraisal: Evaluating an employee's current and past performance against predetermined standards.
  7. Promotion and Career Planning: Placing employees in positions of increased responsibility and pay.
  8. Compensation: Establishing wage and salary plans, including direct financial payments (time-based or performance-based) and indirect benefits.
Aspects of Staffing: Recruitment, Selection, & Training

1. Recruitment

Recruitment is the process of finding possible candidates for a job. It can be sourced internally or externally.

Internal Sources

  • Transfers: Shifting an employee from one job/department to another, representing a horizontal movement.
  • Promotions: Shifting an employee to a higher position with more responsibility, status, and pay, representing a vertical movement.

Merits: Motivates employees, simplifies selection, cheaper, and acts as a tool for training.
Limitations: Limits fresh talent (danger of 'inbreeding'), may make employees lethargic, and cannot be used by new enterprises.

External Sources

  • Direct Recruitment: Notice on the factory gate for casual/unskilled labor ('badli' workers).
  • Casual Callers: Utilizing a database of unsolicited applicants.
  • Advertisement: Wide reach via newspapers and professional journals.
  • Employment Exchanges: Government-run agencies matching job-seekers with employers.
  • Placement Agencies & Management Consultants: Private agencies providing specialized executive search.
  • Campus Recruitment: Direct recruitment from colleges and technical institutes.
  • Recommendations of Employees: Candidates introduced by current staff.
  • Labour Contractors: Recruiting unskilled workers through contractors.
  • Web Publishing: Utilizing dedicated job portals (e.g., internet recruitment).

Merits: Qualified personnel, wider choice, fresh talent, and competitive spirit.
Limitations: Dissatisfaction among existing staff, lengthy process, and high cost.

2. Selection

Selection is the process of choosing the best candidate from the pool of prospective applicants. It is often called a negative process because more candidates are rejected than selected.

The Selection Process

  1. Preliminary Screening: Eliminating unqualified or unfit job seekers based on application forms.
  2. Selection Tests: Using psychological and practical tests:
    • Intelligence Tests: Measures learning ability and decision-making.
    • Aptitude Tests: Measures potential for learning new skills.
    • Personality Tests: Probes emotions, reactions, and value systems.
    • Trade Tests: Measures existing professional/technical skills.
    • Interest Tests: Identifies a person's pattern of interests.
  3. Employment Interview: A formal, in-depth conversation to evaluate suitability.
  4. Reference and Background Checks: Verifying candidate details with previous employers, teachers, or references.
  5. Selection Decision: Final choice made from candidates who pass all tests and interviews.
  6. Medical Examination: Ensuring physical and medical fitness before the job offer.
  7. Job Offer: Issuing a letter of appointment with a reporting date.
  8. Contract of Employment: Executing formal documents, including the attestation form and terms of service.

3. Training and Development

Training is the process of increasing the knowledge and skills of employees to perform specific jobs, while development focuses on the overall growth of the individual.

BasisTrainingDevelopment
ConceptProcess of increasing knowledge and skills.Process of learning and growth.
ObjectiveTo enable the employee to do the job better.To enable the overall growth of the employee.
OrientationJob-oriented process.Career-oriented process.

Methods of Training

  • On-the-Job Methods (Learning while doing):
    • Apprenticeship Programmes: Trainee works under a master worker (e.g., plumbers, electricians).
    • Coaching: A superior guides and instructs the trainee.
    • Internship Training: Joint programme between educational institutions and business firms.
    • Job Rotation: Shifting trainees from one department to another to gain broader experience.
  • Off-the-Job Methods (Learning before doing):
    • Classroom Lectures/Conferences: Conveying specific information, rules, or procedures.
    • Films: Visual demonstrations of skills.
    • Case Study: Analysing real-life business problems to find solutions.
    • Computer Modelling: Simulating work environments via programming.
    • Vestibule Training: Training on actual equipment but conducted away from the work floor.
    • Programmed Instruction: Step-by-step logical learning packages.
