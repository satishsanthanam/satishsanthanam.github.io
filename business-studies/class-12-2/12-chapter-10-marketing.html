Business Studies: Class 12 (Part 2) — Chapter 10: Marketing

Introduction to Marketing & Market Concepts
Business Case Title: Procter & Gamble (P&G) Environmental Philosophy
Corporate Context/Problem: Modern companies must satisfy a diverse set of stakeholders (government, NGOs, media, social activists) beyond just consumers to ensure long-term survival and brand power.
Strategic Action Taken: P&G actively studied the environmental impact of consumer products and pioneered concentrated products, recycled plastic bottles, and refill packages to promote sustainable development.
Managerial Inference: Embracing deep social values and corporate social responsibility builds powerful brands and robust customer relationships.

Understanding the Concept of Market

In the traditional sense, a market refers to a physical place where buyers and sellers gather to conduct transactions. In modern marketing, it refers to a set of actual and potential buyers of a product or service.

What is Marketing?

Marketing is a social process by which individual groups obtain what they need and want through creating offerings and freely exchanging products and services of value with others.

Core Features of Marketing:

  • Needs and Wants: Needs are basic physiological/psychological deprivations (e.g., hunger), while wants are culturally shaped satisfiers (e.g., wanting a dosa or chapatti).
  • Creating a Market Offering: Developing a complete offer with specific features (size, quality, price) available at specific outlets.
  • Customer Value: Buyers make decisions based on their perception of the product's value relative to its cost. Marketers must add value to stand out from competitors.
  • Exchange Mechanism: The process of obtaining a desired product by offering something of value in return.

5 Conditions for Exchange:

  1. Involvement of at least two parties (buyer and seller).
  2. Each party must have something of value to offer the other.
  3. Each party must have the ability to communicate and deliver.
  4. Each party must have the freedom to accept or reject the offer.
  5. The parties must be willing to enter into transactions on a voluntary basis.

What can be Marketed?

Marketing is not limited to physical goods. The following entities can be marketed:

  • Physical Products: Motorcycles, mobile phones, footwear.
  • Services: Insurance, healthcare, online trading, education.
  • Ideas: Polio vaccination, blood donation, family planning.
  • Persons: Election candidates.
  • Places: Tourism campaigns (e.g., "Visit Agra", "Kerala Tourism").
  • Events: Sports events (Olympics), music concerts, film festivals.
  • Information: Technology information, market research data.

Functions of Marketing

Marketing involves a series of interrelated activities to ensure smooth flow of goods from producers to consumers:

  1. Gathering and Analysing Market Information: Conducting market research to identify customer needs, opportunities, and threats.
  2. Marketing Planning: Developing plans to achieve marketing objectives (e.g., increasing market share).
  3. Product Designing and Development: Designing products to make them attractive and competitive.
  4. Standardisation and Grading: Standardisation ensures uniformity in quality; grading classifies products based on characteristics like size or quality.
  5. Packaging and Labelling: Designing wrappers/containers (packaging) and labels to protect, identify, and promote products.
  6. Branding: Creating a distinct identity for the product to differentiate it from competitors.
  7. Customer Support Services: Providing after-sales service, handling complaints, and offering maintenance.
  8. Pricing of Product: Determining the amount of money customers must pay to obtain the product.
  9. Promotion: Informing and persuading customers through advertising, personal selling, sales promotion, and publicity.
  10. Physical Distribution: Managing channels of distribution and physical movement of goods.
  11. Transportation: Physical movement of goods from the place of production to the place of consumption.
  12. Storage or Warehousing: Storing goods to bridge the time gap between production and consumption.
The Marketing Mix (The 4 Ps)

The Marketing Mix is the set of marketing tools that a firm uses to pursue its marketing objectives in a target market. It consists of four elements: Product, Price, Place, and Promotion.

1. Product

A product is a bundle of utilities that provides functional, psychological, and social benefits to the buyer.

Classification of Products:

  • Consumer Products: Purchased by ultimate consumers for personal use.
    • On Basis of Shopping Effort:
      • Convenience Products: Purchased frequently, immediately, and with minimal effort (e.g., bread, medicine).
      • Shopping Products: Purchased after comparing quality, price, and style (e.g., clothes, television).
      • Speciality Products: Products with unique characteristics for which buyers make special purchasing efforts (e.g., art, antiques).
    • On Basis of Durability:
      • Non-durable Products: Consumed in one or a few uses (e.g., soap, toothpaste).
      • Durable Products: Tangible goods that survive many years of use (e.g., refrigerator, bicycle).
      • Services: Intangible activities or benefits offered for sale (e.g., dry cleaning, doctor's service).
  • Industrial Products: Used as inputs in producing other products (e.g., raw materials, engines, lubricants).
    • Materials and Parts: Goods that enter the product completely (e.g., raw materials, component parts).
    • Capital Items: Long-lasting goods used in production (e.g., installations, equipment).
    • Supplies and Business Services: Short-lasting goods and services that facilitate operations (e.g., lubricants, maintenance services).

Branding, Packaging, and Labelling:

  • Branding Terms:
    • Brand: A name, term, sign, symbol, or design used to identify and differentiate products.
    • Brand Name: The verbal component of a brand that can be spoken.
    • Brand Mark: The visual component of a brand (symbol, design) that cannot be spoken.
    • Trade Mark: A brand that is given legal protection.
  • Levels of Packaging:
    1. Primary Package: The immediate container (e.g., toothpaste tube).
    2. Secondary Package: Additional protection kept until use (e.g., cardboard box of toothpaste).
    3. Transportation Packaging: Necessary for storage and transport (e.g., corrugated boxes).
  • Functions of Labelling: Describe the product, identify the brand, grade the product, help in promotion, and provide information required by law.

2. Price

Price is the amount of money paid by a buyer in consideration of the purchase of a product or service.

Factors Affecting Price Determination:

  • Product Cost: Sets the minimum floor price. Includes fixed, variable, and semi-variable costs.
  • Utility and Demand: Sets the upper limit of the price based on buyer utility and demand elasticity.
  • Extent of Competition: High competition forces prices down toward the lower limit.
  • Government and Legal Regulations: Government can intervene to regulate prices of essential commodities.
  • Pricing Objectives: Objectives like profit maximisation, obtaining market share leadership, or surviving in a competitive market.
  • Marketing Methods Used: Costs of distribution, quality of sales force, and advertising affect final pricing.

3. Place (Physical Distribution)

Place mix covers activities that make a firm's products available to target customers at the right place and time.

Components of Physical Distribution:

  1. Order Processing: Accurate and speedy flow of instructions from customer to manufacturer.
  2. Transportation: Carrying goods from production points to sales points.
  3. Warehousing: Storing and assorting products to create time utility.
  4. Inventory Control: Balancing the cost of holding inventory against customer service levels.
Business Case Title: Word of Mouth in India
Corporate Context/Problem: Conventional advertising has varying effectiveness across different global markets.
Strategic Action Taken: Marketers in India leverage peer recommendations, family discussions, and local celebrity endorsements rather than relying solely on digital or conventional media.
Managerial Inference: Indian consumers are highly influenced by closely-knit social structures, making word-of-mouth a critical element of physical distribution and promotion success.

4. Promotion

Promotion refers to the use of communication to inform potential customers about a product and persuade them to buy it.

The Promotion Mix Elements:

  • Advertising: A paid, impersonal form of communication by an identified sponsor.
    • Objections: Adds to cost, undermines social values, confuses buyers, encourages sale of inferior products.
  • Personal Selling: Oral presentation of a message in the form of conversation with prospective buyers. Highly flexible with direct feedback.
  • Sales Promotion: Short-term incentives to encourage immediate purchase (e.g., rebates, discounts, refunds, lucky draws, sampling).
  • Public Relations (PR): Managing public opinion and building goodwill. Key tools include publicity, press releases, corporate communication, lobbying, and counselling.
