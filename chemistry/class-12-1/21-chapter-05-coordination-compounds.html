Chemistry: Class 121 — Chapter 5: Coordination Compounds

Introduction & Werner's Theory

In transition metals, metal atoms or ions are bound to a number of anions or neutral molecules by sharing of electrons, forming coordination compounds. These compounds play vital roles in biological systems (e.g., chlorophyll contains magnesium, haemoglobin contains iron, and vitamin B12 contains cobalt) and find extensive applications in metallurgy, catalysis, and medicine.

Werner's Postulates

Alfred Werner formulated his theory of coordination compounds based on the concepts of primary and secondary valencies:

  • Primary Valency: Ionisable, satisfied by negative ions, and corresponds to the oxidation state of the metal ion.
  • Secondary Valency: Non-ionisable, satisfied by neutral molecules or negative ions, and corresponds to the coordination number of the metal. It is fixed for a given metal.
  • The ions or groups bound by secondary linkages have characteristic spatial arrangements corresponding to different coordination numbers (coordination polyhedra).
Example: In $[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_3$, the six $NH_3$ molecules are directly bound to $Co^{3+}$ (secondary valency = 6), while the three $Cl^-$ ions satisfy the primary valency and are ionisable.

Double Salt vs. Complex Compound

  • Double Salts: Dissociate completely into simple constituent ions when dissolved in water. Examples include carnallite ($KCl \cdot MgCl_2 \cdot 6H_2O$), Mohr's salt ($FeSO_4 \cdot (NH_4)_2SO_4 \cdot 6H_2O$), and potash alum ($KAl(SO_4)_2 \cdot 12H_2O$).
  • Complex Compounds: Do not dissociate completely into simple constituent ions. The complex entity (e.g., $[Fe(CN)_6]^{4-}$) remains intact in solution.
Important Definitions

Coordination Entity & Central Atom

  • Coordination Entity: A central metal atom or ion bonded to a fixed number of ions or molecules. Example: $[CoCl_3(NH_3)_3]$.
  • Central Atom/Ion: The atom or ion to which a fixed number of ligands are bound in a definite geometrical arrangement. These act as Lewis acids.

Ligands & Their Classification

The ions or molecules bound to the central atom/ion are called ligands. They are classified based on the number of donor atoms:

  • Unidentate: Bound through a single donor atom. Examples: $Cl^-$, $H_2O$, $NH_3$.
  • Didentate: Bound through two donor atoms. Examples: ethane-1,2-diamine ($en$) and oxalate ($C_2O_4^{2-}$).
  • Polydentate: Bound through multiple donor atoms. Example: Ethylenediaminetetraacetate ion ($EDTA^{4-}$), which is a hexadentate ligand.
  • Chelate Ligand: A di- or polydentate ligand that uses two or more donor atoms simultaneously to bind to a single metal ion, forming a ring structure. Chelate complexes tend to be exceptionally stable (chelate effect).
  • Ambidentate Ligand: A ligand that can coordinate through more than one donor atom. Examples: $NO_2^-$ (can bind via N as nitrito-N or via O as nitrito-O) and $SCN^-$ (can bind via S as thiocyanato-S or via N as isothiocyanato-N).

Coordination Number & Sphere

  • Coordination Number (CN): The total number of ligand donor atoms directly bonded to the metal ion. Pi bonds are not counted.
  • Coordination Sphere: The central atom/ion and its attached ligands enclosed in square brackets. The ionisable species outside the brackets are called counter ions.
  • Coordination Polyhedron: The spatial arrangement of ligand atoms directly attached to the central atom/ion (e.g., octahedral, tetrahedral, square planar).

Homoleptic & Heteroleptic Complexes

  • Homoleptic Complexes: Complexes in which the metal is bound to only one kind of donor group. Example: $[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}$.
  • Heteroleptic Complexes: Complexes in which the metal is bound to more than one kind of donor group. Example: $[Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2]^+$.
Nomenclature of Coordination Compounds

Formulas of Mononuclear Coordination Entities

  1. The central atom is listed first.
  2. Ligands are listed in alphabetical order, irrespective of their charge.
  3. Polydentate ligands are also listed alphabetically. For abbreviated ligands, the first letter of the abbreviation is used.
  4. The entire coordination entity is enclosed in square brackets.
  5. There should be no space between ligands and the metal within the coordination sphere.

Naming Rules (IUPAC)

  1. The cation is named first in both positively and negatively charged coordination entities.
  2. Ligands are named in alphabetical order before the name of the central atom/ion.
  3. Anionic ligands end in -o (e.g., chlorido, cyanido). Neutral and cationic ligands keep their names, with exceptions: $H_2O$ (aqua), $NH_3$ (ammine), $CO$ (carbonyl), and $NO$ (nitrosyl).
  4. Prefixes di-, tri-, tetra- indicate the number of individual ligands. When the ligand name includes a numerical prefix, bis-, tris-, tetrakis- are used in parentheses.
  5. The oxidation state of the metal is indicated by a Roman numeral in parentheses.
  6. If the complex ion is an anion, the metal name ends with the suffix -ate (e.g., cobaltate, ferrate). For cationic/neutral complexes, the metal is named as the element.
Isomerism in Coordination Compounds

Isomers are compounds with the same chemical formula but different structural arrangements.

Stereoisomerism

  • Geometrical Isomerism: Occurs in heteroleptic complexes due to different spatial arrangements of ligands. Common in coordination numbers 4 (square planar) and 6 (octahedral). Examples include cis and trans isomers, as well as facial (fac) and meridional (mer) isomers in $[Ma_3b_3]$ complexes.
  • Optical Isomerism: Occurs when molecules are non-superimposable mirror images (chiral). The two forms are called dextro ($d$) and laevo ($l$). Common in octahedral complexes containing didentate ligands (e.g., $[Co(en)_3]^{3+}$).

Structural Isomerism

  • Linkage Isomerism: Arises in complexes containing ambidentate ligands. Example: $[Co(NH_3)_5(NO_2)]Cl_2$ and $[Co(NH_3)_5(ONO)]Cl_2$.
  • Coordination Isomerism: Arises from the interchange of ligands between cationic and anionic entities of different metal ions. Example: $[Co(NH_3)_6][Cr(CN)_6]$ and $[Cr(NH_3)_6][Co(CN)_6]$.
  • Ionisation Isomerism: Occurs when the counter ion in a complex salt is itself a potential ligand and can displace a ligand. Example: $[Co(NH_3)_5(SO_4)]Br$ and $[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4$.
  • Solvate (Hydrate) Isomerism: Differs by whether a solvent molecule (like water) is directly bonded to the metal ion or present as free solvent in the crystal lattice. Example: $[Cr(H_2O)_6]Cl_3$ (violet) and $[Cr(H_2O)_5Cl]Cl_2 \cdot H_2O$ (grey-green).
Bonding in Coordination Compounds

Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

According to VBT, the metal atom/ion under the influence of ligands uses its $(n-1)d$, $ns$, $np$, or $nd$ orbitals for hybridisation to yield equivalent orbitals of definite geometry:

  • $sp^3$ (Coordination Number 4): Tetrahedral geometry.
  • $dsp^2$ (Coordination Number 4): Square planar geometry.
  • $sp^3d$ (Coordination Number 5): Trigonal bipyramidal geometry.
  • $sp^3d^2$ (Coordination Number 6): Octahedral geometry (outer orbital or high-spin complex).
  • $d^2sp^3$ (Coordination Number 6): Octahedral geometry (inner orbital or low-spin complex).

Magnetic properties can be predicted: complexes with unpaired electrons are paramagnetic, while those with all paired electrons are diamagnetic.

Crystal Field Theory (CFT)

CFT is an electrostatic model that treats the metal-ligand bond as purely ionic, considering ligands as point charges. The electrostatic field of ligands lifts the degeneracy of the $d$ orbitals, causing them to split:

  • Octahedral Splitting: The $d$ orbitals split into a lower energy triplet ($t_{2g}$ set: $d_{xy}$, $d_{yz}$, $d_{xz}$) and a higher energy doublet ($e_g$ set: $d_{x^2-y^2}$, $d_{z^2}$). The energy separation is denoted as $\Delta_o$.
  • Spectrochemical Series: Ligands are arranged in order of increasing field strength:
    $I^- < Br^- < SCN^- < Cl^- < S^{2-} < F^- < OH^- < C_2O_4^{2-} < H_2O < NCS^- < edta^{4-} < NH_3 < en < CN^- < CO$.
  • Weak Field Ligands: Cause small splitting ($\Delta_o < P$, where $P$ is pairing energy), resulting in high-spin complexes.
  • Strong Field Ligands: Cause large splitting ($\Delta_o > P$), resulting in low-spin complexes.
  • Tetrahedral Splitting: The splitting is inverted and smaller compared to octahedral splitting: $\Delta_t = \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$. Low-spin configurations are rarely observed in tetrahedral complexes.

Colour in Coordination Compounds

The characteristic colours of coordination complexes are attributed to $d-d$ transitions. When light is absorbed, an electron is excited from the lower energy $d$ orbital set ($t_{2g}$) to the higher energy set ($e_g$). The observed colour is complementary to the absorbed wavelength. In the absence of ligands, crystal field splitting does not occur, and the substance remains colourless (e.g., anhydrous $CuSO_4$ is white, while $CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$ is blue).

Bonding in Metal Carbonyls

Metal carbonyls are homoleptic complexes containing carbon monoxide ($CO$) as ligands (e.g., $Ni(CO)_4$, $Fe(CO)_5$, $Cr(CO)_6$).

  • The metal-carbon bond possesses both $\sigma$ and $\pi$ character.
  • A $\sigma$ bond is formed by the donation of a lone pair of electrons from the carbonyl carbon into a vacant $d$ orbital of the metal.
  • A $\pi$ bond is formed by the back-donation of a pair of electrons from a filled $d$ orbital of the metal into the vacant antibonding $\pi^*$ orbital of $CO$.
  • This back-donation creates a synergic effect, which strengthens the bond between $CO$ and the metal.
Importance and Applications
  • Analytical Chemistry: Used in qualitative and quantitative analysis (e.g., complexation with EDTA, DMG).
  • Water Hardness: Estimated by titration with $Na_2EDTA$, which forms stable complexes with $Ca^{2+}$ and $Mg^{2+}$.
  • Metallurgy: Extraction of metals like gold and silver via cyanide complex formation, and purification of nickel by the Mond process ($Ni(CO)_4$).
  • Biological Systems: Essential components like chlorophyll (Mg), haemoglobin (Fe), vitamin B12 (Co), and enzymes like carboxypeptidase A.
  • Medicine: Use of chelating agents in treating metal poisoning (e.g., EDTA for lead poisoning) and coordination complexes like cis-platin as anti-tumour agents.
