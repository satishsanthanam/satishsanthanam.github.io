Chemistry: Class 12 — Chapter 4: Amines

Introduction & Structure of Amines

Amines constitute an important class of organic compounds derived by replacing one or more hydrogen atoms of ammonia ($NH_3$) molecule by alkyl/aryl group(s).

In nature, they occur among proteins, vitamins, alkaloids, and hormones. Biologically active examples include:

  • Adrenaline and Ephedrine: Contain secondary amino groups; used to increase blood pressure.
  • Novocain: A synthetic amino compound used as an anaesthetic in dentistry.
  • Benadryl: A well-known antihistaminic drug containing a tertiary amino group.
  • Quaternary ammonium salts: Used as surfactants.

9.1 Structure of Amines

Like ammonia, the nitrogen atom of amines is trivalent and carries an unshared pair of electrons. Nitrogen orbitals in amines are $sp^3$ hybridised, and the geometry of amines is pyramidal.

Each of the three $sp^3$ hybridised orbitals of nitrogen overlaps with orbitals of hydrogen or carbon. The fourth orbital contains an unshared pair of electrons. Due to the presence of this unshared pair, the angle $C-N-E$ (where $E$ is $C$ or $H$) is less than $109.5^\circ$ (e.g., $108^\circ$ in trimethylamine).

9.2 Classification of Amines

Amines are classified as primary ($1^\circ$), secondary ($2^\circ$), and tertiary ($3^\circ$) depending upon the number of hydrogen atoms replaced by alkyl or aryl groups in the ammonia molecule:

  • Primary ($1^\circ$): $R-NH_2$ or $Ar-NH_2$ (one hydrogen replaced).
  • Secondary ($2^\circ$): $R-NH-R'$ (two hydrogens replaced).
  • Tertiary ($3^\circ$): $R-N(R')-R''$ (three hydrogens replaced).

Amines are called 'simple' when all alkyl or aryl groups are the same, and 'mixed' when they are different.

9.3 Nomenclature

In the common system, aliphatic amines are named by prefixing the alkyl group to amine (e.g., methylamine). In the IUPAC system, they are named as alkanamines (replacing the 'e' of alkane with 'amine').

For secondary and tertiary amines, the locant $N$ is used to designate substituents attached to the nitrogen atom (e.g., $CH_3NHCH_2CH_3$ is named as N-methylethanamine).

Arylamines are named as derivatives of aniline (or benzenamine).

9.4 Preparation of Amines

1. Reduction of Nitro Compounds

Nitro compounds are reduced to amines by passing hydrogen gas in the presence of finely divided nickel, palladium, or platinum, or by reduction with metals in acidic medium.

$$R-NO_2 + 3H_2 \xrightarrow{Pd/Ethanol} R-NH_2 + 2H_2O$$

$$Ar-NO_2 \xrightarrow{Fe + HCl \text{ or } Sn + HCl} Ar-NH_2$$

Note: Reduction with iron scrap and hydrochloric acid is preferred because $FeCl_2$ formed gets hydrolysed to release hydrochloric acid, requiring only a small amount of $HCl$ to initiate the reaction.

2. Ammonolysis of Alkyl Halides

The carbon-halogen bond in alkyl or benzyl halides can be cleaved by a nucleophile like ammonia. This process is called ammonolysis.

$$NH_3 + R-X \rightarrow R-\overset{+}{N}H_3 \bar{X} \xrightarrow{NaOH} R-NH_2 + H_2O + NaX$$

The primary amine can further react with alkyl halide to form secondary and tertiary amines, and finally quaternary ammonium salts:

$$R-NH_2 \xrightarrow{RX} R_2NH \xrightarrow{RX} R_3N \xrightarrow{RX} R_4\overset{+}{N}\bar{X}$$

Order of reactivity of halides: $RI > RBr > RCl$.

3. Reduction of Nitriles

Nitriles on reduction with lithium aluminium hydride ($LiAlH_4$) or catalytic hydrogenation produce primary amines. This reaction is used for the ascent of the amine series (adding one carbon atom).

$$R-C\equiv N \xrightarrow{H_2/Ni \text{ or } Na(Hg)/C_2H_5OH} R-CH_2-NH_2$$

4. Reduction of Amides

Amides on reduction with lithium aluminium hydride yield amines.

$$R-CO-NH_2 \xrightarrow{(i) LiAlH_4, (ii) H_2O} R-CH_2-NH_2$$

5. Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis

Used for the preparation of primary aliphatic amines. Phthalimide on treatment with ethanolic $KOH$ forms potassium phthalimide, which on heating with an alkyl halide followed by alkaline hydrolysis produces a primary amine.

Note: Aromatic primary amines cannot be prepared by this method because aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with the phthalimide anion.

6. Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation Reaction

An amide is treated with bromine in an aqueous or ethanolic solution of sodium hydroxide, yielding a primary amine containing one carbon atom less than the starting amide.

$$R-CO-NH_2 + Br_2 + 4NaOH \rightarrow R-NH_2 + Na_2CO_3 + 2NaBr + 2H_2O$$

9.5 Physical Properties
  • Lower aliphatic amines are gases with a fishy odour. Primary amines with three or more carbons are liquids, and higher ones are solids.
  • Lower aliphatic amines are soluble in water because they can form hydrogen bonds with water molecules. Solubility decreases with an increase in molar mass due to the hydrophobic alkyl part.
  • Primary and secondary amines undergo intermolecular hydrogen bonding. The order of boiling points of isomeric amines is: Primary > Secondary > Tertiary.
9.6 Chemical Reactions

1. Basic Character of Amines

Amines are basic in nature and react with acids to form salts:

$$R-NH_2 + HX \rightleftharpoons R-\overset{+}{N}H_3 \bar{X}$$

The basic strength is expressed in terms of $K_b$ or $pK_b$ ($pK_b = -\log K_b$). A smaller $pK_b$ value indicates a stronger base.

  • Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than ammonia due to the $+I$ effect of alkyl groups.
  • Aromatic amines are weaker bases than ammonia because the unshared electron pair on nitrogen is conjugated with the benzene ring (resonance stabilization of aniline).
  • In the aqueous phase, basic strength is determined by a combination of inductive effect, solvation effect, and steric hindrance.
  • Order of basic strength in aqueous solution:
    • Ethyl substituted: $(C_2H_5)_2NH > (C_2H_5)_3N > C_2H_5NH_2 > NH_3$
    • Methyl substituted: $(CH_3)_2NH > CH_3NH_2 > (CH_3)_3N > NH_3$

2. Alkylation & Acylation

Alkylation: Amines react with alkyl halides to undergo alkylation.

Acylation: Aliphatic and aromatic primary and secondary amines react with acid chlorides, anhydrides, and esters by nucleophilic substitution to form amides. The reaction is carried out in the presence of a base stronger than the amine (like pyridine) to remove $HCl$.

$$R-NH_2 + CH_3COCl \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine}} R-NH-CO-CH_3 + HCl$$

3. Carbylamine Reaction

Aliphatic and aromatic primary amines on heating with chloroform and ethanolic potassium hydroxide form foul-smelling isocyanides (carbylamines). This is used as a test for primary amines.

$$R-NH_2 + CHCl_3 + 3KOH \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-NC + 3KCl + 3H_2O$$

4. Reaction with Nitrous Acid

  • Primary aliphatic amines: React with nitrous acid ($HNO_2$) to form unstable alkyldiazonium salts, which decompose to liberate nitrogen gas quantitatively and form alcohols.
  • Primary aromatic amines: React with nitrous acid at low temperatures ($273\text{--}278\text{ K}$) to form stable arenediazonium salts.

5. Reaction with Arylsulphonyl Chloride (Hinsberg's Reagent)

Benzenesulphonyl chloride ($C_6H_5SO_2Cl$) is used to distinguish primary, secondary, and tertiary amines:

  • Primary amines: Form a sulphonamide containing an acidic hydrogen on nitrogen, which is soluble in alkali.
  • Secondary amines: Form a sulphonamide with no acidic hydrogen, which is insoluble in alkali.
  • Tertiary amines: Do not react with benzenesulphonyl chloride.

6. Electrophilic Substitution

  • Bromination: Aniline reacts with bromine water at room temperature to give a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline. To get a monosubstituted product, the activating effect of $-NH_2$ is controlled by acetylation with acetic anhydride.
  • Nitration: Direct nitration of aniline yields tarry oxidation products and a significant amount of meta-derivative due to protonation to the anilinium ion. Acetylation is used to protect the amino group to obtain $p$-nitroaniline as the major product.
  • Sulphonation: Aniline reacts with concentrated sulphuric acid to form anilinium hydrogensulphate, which on heating at $453\text{--}473\text{ K}$ produces sulphanilic acid, existing as a zwitterion.
II. Diazonium Salts

Diazonium salts have the general formula $R-\overset{+}{N}_2 \bar{X}$ where $R$ is an aryl group and $X^-$ can be $Cl^-$, $Br^-$, $HSO_4^-$, $BF_4^-$, etc.

9.7 Method of Preparation

Prepared by the reaction of aniline with nitrous acid at $273\text{--}278\text{ K}$. This process is called diazotisation.

$$C_6H_5NH_2 + NaNO_2 + 2HCl \xrightarrow{273\text{--}278\text{ K}} C_6H_5\overset{+}{N}_2\bar{C}l + NaCl + 2H_2O$$

9.8 Physical Properties

Benzenediazonium chloride is a colourless crystalline solid, readily soluble in water, stable in the cold but reacts with water when warmed.

9.9 Chemical Reactions

Reactions are divided into two categories:

A. Reactions involving displacement of nitrogen:

  • Replacement by halide or cyanide (Sandmeyer Reaction):

    $$Ar\overset{+}{N}_2\bar{X} \xrightarrow{Cu_2Cl_2/HCl} ArCl + N_2$$

    $$Ar\overset{+}{N}_2\bar{X} \xrightarrow{Cu_2Br_2/HBr} ArBr + N_2$$

    $$Ar\overset{+}{N}_2\bar{X} \xrightarrow{CuCN/KCN} ArCN + N_2$$

  • Gatterman Reaction: Halogen is introduced using copper powder and halogen acid.
  • Replacement by Iodide: Treated with potassium iodide ($KI$).
  • Replacement by Fluoride: Treated with fluoroboric acid ($HBF_4$) to form diazonium fluoroborate, which on heating yields aryl fluoride.
  • Replacement by H: Reduced using mild reducing agents like hypophosphorous acid ($H_3PO_2$) or ethanol.
  • Replacement by Hydroxyl group: Hydrolysed to phenol by warming with water.
  • Replacement by Nitro group: Diazonium fluoroborate heated with aqueous $NaNO_2$ in the presence of copper.

B. Reactions involving retention of diazo group (Coupling Reactions):

Diazonium salts react with phenol or aniline to form brightly coloured azo compounds (dyes):

  • With phenol: Forms p-hydroxyazobenzene (orange dye).
  • With aniline: Forms p-aminoazobenzene (yellow dye).

9.10 Importance in Synthesis of Aromatic Compounds

Diazonium salts are excellent intermediates for introducing groups like $-F$, $-Cl$, $-Br$, $-I$, $-CN$, $-OH$, and $-NO_2$ into the aromatic ring at positions that cannot be easily substituted directly.
