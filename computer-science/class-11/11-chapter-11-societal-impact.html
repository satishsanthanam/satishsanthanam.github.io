11.1 & 11.2 Introduction & Digital Footprints

In recent years, the widespread adoption of Digital Technologies has dramatically impacted human lives, making communication, banking, aviation, and e-commerce faster and more efficient. However, this digital transition leaves behind a permanent trail of data.

Technical Profile: Digital Footprint

  • Architecture Layer / Tier: Application and Network Layer (Data Trail)
  • Operational Mechanics & Syntax: Created whenever a user surfs the internet, visits websites, sends emails, or submits online information. It includes IP addresses, location data, and device-specific details.
  • System Trade-offs & Failures: Once generated, digital footprints are stored on remote servers and are nearly impossible to fully erase, exposing users to targeted advertisements, profiling, or potential exploitation.
Active vs. Passive Digital Footprints
  • Active Digital Footprints : Data that a user intentionally submits online, such as sending emails, publishing blog posts, or uploading social media updates.
  • Passive Digital Footprints : Data collected unintentionally without the user's active intervention, such as web browsing history, cookies, IP addresses, and app usage statistics.
11.3 Digital Society and Netizen

A netizen (digital citizen) is anyone who uses digital technology and the internet. Being a responsible netizen requires practicing safe, ethical, and legal behaviors online by adhering to specific etiquettes.

Net Etiquettes
  • Be Ethical: Avoid copyright violation by not using copyrighted materials without permission. Share expertise and ensure shared information is accurate and non-redundant.
  • Be Respectful: Respect the privacy of others (do not share private files/images without consent) and respect diversity in public forums.
  • Be Responsible: Avoid cyber bullying (harassment, threats, or public ridicule online). Do not feed internet trolls who deliberately sow discord.
Communication & Social Media Etiquettes
  • Communication Etiquettes: Be precise (respect time and data limits by compressing large files) and be polite/credible in all digital interactions.
  • Social Media Etiquettes: Be secure (choose strong, frequently changed passwords; know who you befriend; beware of fake news) and be reliable (think carefully before uploading sensitive content).
Core Computer Science Concept Technical Definition / Operational State Structural Parameters & Specifications Computational Function & System Purpose
Netizen An active user of the internet and digital platforms. Governed by Net, Communication, and Social Media Etiquettes. Ensures safe, legal, and collaborative digital environments.
Cyber Bullying Insulting, degrading, or intimidating online behavior. Repeated targeting, posting rumors, or sharing private info. Traced via digital footprints; subject to legal penalties.
11.4 Data Protection & Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)

Data protection focuses on securing the privacy of digitally stored sensitive data (such as biometrics, financial records, and health information) using encryption and authentication.

Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)
  • Copyright : Automatically granted legal rights protecting original creative works (writing, music, software, photos) from unauthorized reproduction.
  • Patent : Legal protection for inventions, requiring an application. Grants exclusive rights to use/sell the invention for 20 years.
  • Trademark : Visual symbols, logos, slogans, or brand names that distinguish a commercial enterprise (e.g., Nike logo).
Violations of IPR
  • Plagiarism : Presenting someone else's work or ideas as one's own without proper citation.
  • Copyright Infringement : Unauthorized use of copyrighted material without permission or payment.
  • Trademark Infringement : Unauthorized use of a registered trademark on competing products or services.
Public Access and Open Source Software

Licenses define the rules for using, modifying, and distributing software and creative works.

Technical Profile: Public Licensing Models

  • Architecture Layer / Tier: Legal and Distribution Framework
  • Operational Mechanics & Syntax: Creative Commons (CC) licenses allow free distribution of creative works with custom conditions. GNU General Public License (GPL) ensures end-users have the freedom to run, study, share, and modify software.
  • System Trade-offs & Failures: Software Piracy (unauthorized distribution) degrades system performance via malware and harms the software industry's economy.
11.5 Cyber Crime

Cyber crime refers to criminal activities carried out in a digital environment where a computer is either the target or the tool used to commit the offense.

Types of Cyber Crimes
  • Hacking : Unauthorized access to digital systems. Ethical hacking ( White Hat ) identifies vulnerabilities to improve security, while non-ethical hacking ( Black Hat ) aims to steal data or damage systems.
  • Phishing : Fraudulent attempts to collect sensitive information (passwords, credit cards) using fake websites or spoofed emails.
  • Identity Theft : Stealing personal data (Aadhaar, PAN, email ID) to commit fraud. Types include financial, criminal, and medical identity theft.
  • Ransomware : Malicious software that encrypts user data and demands a ransom payment to restore access.
Combatting and Preventing Cyber Crime
  1. Take regular backups of important data.
  2. Use updated antivirus software.
  3. Avoid installing pirated software; download only from secure (HTTPS) sites.
  4. Use strong, unique passwords and change them periodically.
  5. Do not accept cookies from unknown sites or click on untrusted links.
11.6 Indian Information Technology Act (IT Act)

The Information Technology Act, 2000 (amended in 2008) provides the legal framework for electronic governance and cyber crime regulation in India.

Technical Profile: Digital Signature

  • Architecture Layer / Tier: Security and Authentication Layer
  • Operational Mechanics & Syntax: Digital equivalent of a paper certificate, issued by a licensed Certifying Authority (CA) under Section 24 of the IT Act. It binds a unique digital ID to a document to authenticate the signer's identity.
  • System Trade-offs & Failures: Highly secure but dependent on the integrity of the private key and the trust of the issuing CA.

The IT Act also established the Cyber Appellate Tribunal to resolve disputes arising from cyber crimes such as hacking, tampering with source documents, and unauthorized data publishing.

11.7 Impact on Health & Ergonomics

Excessive use of digital devices can lead to physical and psychological health issues, including eye strain, backaches, and stress.

Ergonomics is the science of designing and arranging workplaces, equipment, and systems so that they are safe and comfortable for the user.

Socio-Technical Analysis & Core Systemic Impact: Maintaining correct posture (viewing distance of 19 to 24 inches, viewing angle of 30 degrees, straight wrists, and 90-degree seat back angle) reduces physical fatigue and long-term injuries. Device safety practices, such as regular cleaning and proper ventilation, ensure the longevity of the computer system itself.
