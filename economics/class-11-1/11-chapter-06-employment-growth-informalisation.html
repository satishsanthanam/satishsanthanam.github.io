Class 11 (Part 1) — Chapter 6: Employment: Growth, Informalisation and Other Issues

6.1 Introduction

Work plays a vital role in individual lives and society, enabling people to earn a living, build self-worth, and contribute to the national income. Studying the workforce provides critical insights into human resource planning, sectoral contributions to GDP, and social issues like child labour and marginalisation.

Key Insight: Technology has transformed traditional work patterns, enabling factory-based production at home and accelerating trends like work-from-home during global disruptions.

6.2 Workers and Employment

Key Economic Definitions

  • Gross Domestic Product (GDP): The total money value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a given year.
  • Gross National Product (GNP): The GDP adjusted for net earnings from foreign transactions (exports minus imports).
  • Economic Activities: All activities that contribute to the Gross National Product.
  • Worker: An individual engaged in an economic activity, including self-employed persons, helpers, and those temporarily absent due to illness, injury, or social functions.

Workforce Size and Composition

During 2022-23, India had a workforce of approximately 545 million. The majority of this workforce resides in rural areas, with men constituting about 77% of the total workforce.

6.4 Self-Employed and Hired Workers

Classification of Workers by Status

  1. Self-Employed: Workers who own and operate an enterprise to earn their livelihood (e.g., shop owners, independent farmers). This is the largest employment category in India (~58%).
  2. Casual Wage Labourers: Workers casually engaged in others' enterprises or farms in return for daily or periodic wages (e.g., construction workers). They constitute ~20% of the workforce.
  3. Regular Salaried Employees: Workers engaged by an employer on a regular basis and paid regular wages or salaries (e.g., teachers, engineers). They account for ~22% of the workforce.

Employment Distribution by Gender and Region

  • Gender Distribution: Self-employment is the primary source of livelihood for both men (54%) and women (67%). Casual wage work is the second major source for women.
  • Regional Distribution: Self-employment is dominant in rural areas (65%) due to agriculture, whereas urban areas show a higher share of regular salaried jobs (48%) due to concentrated industrial and service enterprises.
6.5 Employment in Firms, Factories and Offices

Sectoral Classification

  • Primary Sector: Agriculture, Forestry, Fishing, Mining, and Quarrying.
  • Secondary Sector: Manufacturing, Construction, Electricity, Gas, and Water Supply.
  • Tertiary / Service Sector: Trade, Transport, Storage, and Services.

6.6 Growth and Changing Structure of Employment

Jobless Growth

During 1950-2010, India's GDP grew positively and consistently outpaced employment growth, which stagnated at under 2%. This widening gap where economic output expands without generating proportional employment is termed Jobless Growth.

Sectoral Shifts and Casualisation

  • Sectoral Shift: The share of the workforce in the primary sector declined from 74.3% (1972-73) to 46.1% (2023-24), while secondary and service sectors expanded.
  • Casualisation of Workforce: The process where workers shift from self-employment and regular salaried jobs to highly vulnerable casual wage labour.
6.7 Informalisation of Indian Workforce

Formal vs. Informal Sectors

Extent of Informalisation

Out of 535 million workers in India (2019-20), only about 59 million (11%) were in the formal sector, leaving 89% of the workforce in the informal sector. This high concentration of informal employment highlights the vulnerability of the Indian workforce.

6.8 Unemployment

Definition and Data Sources

Unemployment is a situation where individuals who are willing and able to work at prevailing wage rates cannot find employment. Key data sources include:

  • Reports of the Census of India
  • National Statistical Office (NSO) Reports
  • Annual Reports of the Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)
  • Directorate General of Employment and Training (DGET)

Types of Unemployment

  • Open Unemployment: A situation where workers are willing to work but are completely without jobs.
  • Disguised Unemployment: A state where more workers are employed than necessary, meaning their marginal productivity is zero (common in agriculture).
  • Seasonal Unemployment: Unemployment that occurs during specific periods of the year when agricultural or industrial activities cease.
6.9 Government and Employment Generation

Direct and Indirect Employment

  • Direct Employment: The government employs people directly in administrative departments, public enterprises, and services.
  • Indirect Employment: Government policies and investments stimulate private sector output, leading to secondary job creation.

Key Employment Schemes

The government implements various poverty alleviation and employment generation programmes, notably the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) 2005, which guarantees 100 days of unskilled manual work to rural households.
