Class 11 (Part 2) — Chapter 1: Introduction

Why Economics?

Economics is fundamentally the study of mankind in the ordinary business of life, a concept famously articulated by Alfred Marshall.

Basic Economic Roles

  • Consumer: An individual who buys goods and services to satisfy personal or family needs.
  • Seller: An individual who sells goods to make a profit.
  • Producer: An individual or business entity that produces goods or provides services to create value.
  • Employee: A person who works for another and receives wages or a salary in return.
  • Employer: A person or institution that employs others and pays them wages or salaries.

Economic Activity and Scarcity

An economic activity is any activity undertaken for monetary gain. The fundamental driver of all economic activities is the existence of scarcity.

  • The Problem of Scarcity: Our wants are unlimited, but the resources to satisfy them are limited. Scarcity is the root of all economic problems.
  • Alternative Uses: Resources are not only scarce but also have alternative uses (e.g., land can be used to grow food crops or non-food crops), giving rise to the problem of choice.
Core Divisions of Economics

The study of Economics is conventionally divided into three main parts:

Consumption, Production, and Distribution

  • Consumption: The study of how consumers decide, given their limited income and market prices, what goods and services to buy.
  • Production: The study of how producers choose what to produce, how to produce it, and at what cost for the market.
  • Distribution: The study of how the national income or Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is distributed among the factors of production in the form of wages, profits, rents, and interest.
Statistics in Economics

To understand and address economic problems, economists rely on factual information represented in numerical terms, known as economic data.

What is Statistics?

Statistics deals with the collection, analysis, interpretation, and presentation of numerical data. It utilizes two types of data:

  • Quantitative Data: Information that can be measured and expressed numerically (e.g., production of rice in tonnes).
  • Qualitative Data: Information describing attributes that cannot be measured quantitatively but can be ranked or classified (e.g., gender, skill levels, intelligence).

What Statistics Does

Statistics serves as an indispensable tool for economists by performing the following functions:

  1. Presents Facts Precisely: Expresses economic facts in definite, exact numerical terms rather than vague statements.
  2. Condenses Mass Data: Summarizes large volumes of data into simple, understandable numerical measures like mean, variance, and standard deviation.
  3. Finds Relationships: Helps identify and verify relationships between different economic factors (e.g., price and demand).
  4. Predicts Future Trends: Enables forecasting and predicting changes in economic variables to facilitate planning.
  5. Formulates Policies: Assists in designing and evaluating plans and policies to solve economic problems like poverty and unemployment.

Limitations of Statistics

Statistical methods are no substitute for common sense! Statistics deals with averages, which can be misleading if applied blindly without contextual understanding. For example, crossing a river based solely on its average depth can lead to disaster if the actual depth varies significantly.
