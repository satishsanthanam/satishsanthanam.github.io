Economics: Class 11 (Part 2) — Chapter 6: Correlation

1. Introduction to Correlation

In statistics, Correlation Analysis is a systematic method used to examine and measure the strength and direction of the relationship between two variables.

Covariation vs. Causation

It is critical to distinguish between association and cause-and-effect:

  • Covariation: Measures how variables change together.
  • Causation: Implies that change in one variable directly causes the change in the other. Correlation does not imply causation.
  • Relationships can sometimes be mere coincidences or driven by a common third variable (e.g., temperature influencing both ice-cream sales and swimming pool usage).
2. Types of Correlation

Correlation can be classified based on the direction of movement and the nature of the relationship:

Direction of Movement
  • Positive Correlation: Both variables move in the same direction. For example, when income rises, consumption also rises.
  • Negative Correlation: Variables move in opposite directions. For example, when the price of a commodity rises, its demand decreases.

Linear vs. Non-Linear Relationships
  • Linear Correlation: The relationship can be represented by a straight line on a graph, meaning the relative change between variables is constant.
  • Non-Linear Correlation: The relationship changes rate and cannot be represented by a straight line.
3. Techniques for Measuring Correlation

Three primary tools are used to study correlation: Scatter Diagrams, Karl Pearson's Coefficient, and Spearman's Rank Correlation.

A. Scatter Diagram

A visual method of plotting data points on a graph to observe the pattern of relationship without calculating numerical values.

  • Perfect Positive Correlation: All points lie exactly on an upward-sloping straight line ($r = 1$).
  • Perfect Negative Correlation: All points lie exactly on a downward-sloping straight line ($r = -1$).
  • Positive Correlation: Points are scattered around an upward-rising band ($0 < r < 1$).
  • Negative Correlation: Points are scattered around a downward-sloping band ($-1 < r < 0$).
  • No Correlation: Points are scattered in a chaotic, circular pattern with no discernible trend ($r = 0$).

B. Karl Pearson's Coefficient of Correlation

Also known as the Product Moment Correlation Coefficient ($r$), this index provides a precise numerical value of the degree of linear relationship between two variables $X$ and $Y$.

Mathematical Formulas:

Standard Covariance and Standard Deviation method:

$$r = \frac{\sum xy}{N \sigma_x \sigma_y}$$

Where deviations from arithmetic means are $x = X - \bar{X}$ and $y = Y - \bar{Y}$:

$$r = \frac{\sum (X - \bar{X})(Y - \bar{Y})}{\sqrt{\sum (X - \bar{X})^2} \sqrt{\sum (Y - \bar{Y})^2}}$$

Direct Method (using raw data):

$$r = \frac{N \sum XY - (\sum X)(\sum Y)}{\sqrt{N \sum X^2 - (\sum X)^2} \sqrt{N \sum Y^2 - (\sum Y)^2}}$$

Properties of $r$:

  • It is a pure number with no units of measurement.
  • Its value always lies between minus one and plus one: $-1 \le r \le 1$.
  • The magnitude of $r$ is unaffected by the change of origin and change of scale.
  • If $r = 0$, the variables are uncorrelated (no linear relationship).

Step Deviation Method

When values are large, calculations are simplified by transforming variables:

$$U = \frac{X - A}{B} \quad \text{and} \quad V = \frac{Y - C}{D}$$

Where $A$ and $C$ are assumed means, and $B$ and $D$ are common factors. Under this transformation:

$$r_{xy} = r_{uv}$$

C. Spearman's Rank Correlation Coefficient

Developed by C.E. Spearman, this method (Rank Correlation) measures the linear association between ranks assigned to individual items. It is ideal for qualitative attributes (e.g., beauty, intelligence) or when precise measurements are unavailable.

Formula for Non-Repeated Ranks:

$$r_s = 1 - \frac{6 \sum D^2}{n^3 - n}$$

Where $D$ is the difference between the ranks of the two variables for each observation, and $n$ is the number of observations.

Formula for Repeated Ranks:

When ranks are tied, a correction factor is added for each repetition:

$$r_s = 1 - \frac{6 \left[ \sum D^2 + \frac{(m_1^3 - m_1)}{12} + \frac{(m_2^3 - m_2)}{12} + \dots \right]}{n(n^2 - 1)}$$

Where $m_1, m_2, \dots$ represent the number of times a rank is repeated.
