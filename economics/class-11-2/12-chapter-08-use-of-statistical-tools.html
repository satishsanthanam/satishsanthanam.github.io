Economics: Class 11 (Part 2) — Chapter 8: Use of Statistical Tools

Introduction to Project Design

Developing a project by conducting a survey and preparing a report helps in understanding how statistical tools and methods are applied to analyze real-world economic problems. These tools are essential for analyzing economic activities such as production, consumption, distribution, banking, insurance, trade, and transport.

Steps Towards Making a Project

  1. Identifying a problem or an area of study: Clearly define the objective of the study (e.g., analyzing consumer awareness, product demand, or local utility issues).
  2. Choice of Target Group: Identify the specific population segment relevant to the study (e.g., targeting middle and high-income groups for car-related projects, or rural/urban households for consumer products).
  3. Collection of Data: Determine whether to use primary data (collected first-hand via questionnaires, interviews, or mail surveys) or secondary data (obtained from published sources like magazines, newspapers, and research reports).
  4. Organisation and Presentation of Data: Process, tabulate, and present the collected data using suitable diagrams such as bar diagrams, pie charts, and histograms.
  5. Analysis and Interpretation: Apply statistical measures such as Measures of Central Tendency (mean, median, mode), Measures of Dispersion (standard deviation), and Correlation to identify patterns and relationships.
  6. Conclusion: Draw meaningful conclusions, predict future prospects, and provide policy suggestions based on the analyzed data.
  7. Bibliography: Document all secondary sources used in the project.
Sample Project: Market Survey on Toothpaste

This sample project demonstrates how a young entrepreneur can plan and execute a market survey to assess consumer preferences, demand, and expenditure patterns before launching a new brand of toothpaste.

Project Objectives & Questionnaire Design

The primary objective is to study consumer tastes, monthly expenditures, brand loyalty, and media influences. A structured questionnaire is designed to collect primary data from a sample of 100 households.

  • Average monthly expenditure on toothpaste.
  • Popular brands currently in demand.
  • Consumer preferences regarding ingredients (e.g., gel, antiseptic, fluoride).
  • Influence of advertisements and media.

Key Survey Findings & Observations

  • Demographics: 67% of respondents belonged to urban areas, and the majority of surveyed individuals fell into the 20-50 age group.
  • Occupational Status: Service class (30%) and traders (40%) constituted the majority of the sample.
  • Brand Preferences: Pepsodent, Colgate, and Close-up were the most preferred brands.
  • Selection Criteria: Standardized markings (ISI/AGMARK), quality, price, and brand reputation were the primary drivers of consumer choice.
  • Media Influence: Television (47%) and newspapers (30%) were identified as the most effective advertising mediums.
Glossary of Key Statistical Terms

A summary of essential statistical terms used in project design and data analysis:

  • Census Method: A method of data collection where observations are gathered from every single individual in the target population.
  • Sample Survey Method: A method where observations are obtained from a representative subset (sample) of the population.
  • Primary Data: First-hand data collected directly from respondents using questionnaires or interviews.
  • Secondary Data: Data that has already been collected, processed, and published by other agencies.
  • Standard Deviation: A measure of the dispersion of a set of data from its mean, indicating variability.
  • Univariate Distribution: A frequency distribution that tracks only one variable.
  • Bivariate Distribution: A frequency distribution that tracks two variables simultaneously.
