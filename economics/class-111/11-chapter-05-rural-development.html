Class 111 — Chapter 5: Rural Development

Introduction and Concept of Rural Development

Agriculture is the primary source of livelihood in rural India, with more than two-thirds of the population depending on it. Real progress in India relies heavily on the development of its villages, as emphasized by Mahatma Gandhi.

What is Rural Development?

Rural Development is a comprehensive term focusing on action for the development of areas lagging behind in the overall village economy.

Key areas requiring fresh initiatives include:

  • Development of Human Resources: Literacy (specifically female literacy), education, skill development, and health (sanitation and public health).
  • Land Reforms: Elimination of exploitation and securing land rights for tillers.
  • Infrastructure Development: Electricity, irrigation, credit, marketing, transport facilities (roads, highways), and agricultural research.
  • Poverty Alleviation: Special measures to improve living conditions of weaker sections through productive employment.
Rural Credit and Banking System

Growth of the rural economy depends primarily on the timely infusion of capital to realize higher productivity in agriculture and non-agriculture sectors.

The Need for Rural Credit

Because the gestation period between crop sowing and realization of income is quite long, farmers borrow from various sources to meet initial investments on seeds, fertilizers, and family expenses.

Institutional Structure of Rural Banking

Post-1969, India adopted a social banking and multi-agency approach. The institutional structure today consists of:

  • NABARD: The National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development was set up in 1982 as an apex body to coordinate all rural financing institutions.
  • Commercial Banks, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), and Cooperatives: Expected to dispense adequate credit at cheaper rates.
  • Self-Help Groups (SHGs): Emerged to fill the gap in formal credit delivery. They promote thrift and provide micro-credit to needy members without collateral. An example is the Kudumbashree program in Kerala.

Critical Appraisal of Rural Banking

While rapid expansion of banking has led to food security and higher output, challenges remain:

  • High default rates on agricultural loans.
  • Inadequate deposit mobilization.
  • Decline in the expansion of rural banking post-reforms.
  • Jan-Dhan Yojana: Introduced to encourage bank account ownership, promoting thrift and financial inclusion with benefits like accidental insurance and overdraft facilities.
Agricultural Market System

Agricultural Marketing is a process that involves the assembling, storage, processing, transportation, packaging, grading, and distribution of different agricultural commodities across the country.

Government Measures to Improve Marketing

  1. Regulation of Markets: To create orderly and transparent marketing conditions.
  2. Provision of Physical Infrastructure: Roads, railways, warehouses, godowns, cold storages, and processing units.
  3. Cooperative Marketing: Realizing fair prices for farmers' products (e.g., milk cooperatives in Gujarat under Operation Flood).
  4. Policy Instruments: Assurance of Minimum Support Prices (MSP), maintenance of buffer stocks by the Food Corporation of India (FCI), and distribution of food grains through the Public Distribution System (PDS).

Emerging Alternate Marketing Channels

Direct sales by farmers to consumers increase incomes. Examples include:

  • Apni Mandi (Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan)
  • Hadaspar Mandi (Pune)
  • Rythu Bazars (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana)
  • Uzhavar Sandies (Tamil Nadu)
Diversification into Productive Activities

Diversification reduces the risk of exclusive dependence on farming and provides sustainable livelihood options during off-seasons.

Animal Husbandry

The farming community uses a mixed crop-livestock system. Livestock production provides stability in income, food security, and nutrition.

Fisheries

Water bodies are integral to the fishing community. Inland sources contribute about $75 ext{\%}$ to the total value of fish production, while the marine sector contributes $25 ext{\%}$. Total fish production accounts for about $1.5 ext{\%}$ of India's GDP.

Horticulture

Horticulture crops include fruits, vegetables, spices, and plantation crops. It contributes nearly one-third of the value of agricultural output and $6 ext{\%}$ of India's GDP.

Information Technology (IT)

IT acts as a tool for releasing creative potential, predicting food insecurity, and disseminating critical information regarding technologies, weather, and soil conditions.

Sustainable Development and Organic Farming

Conventional agriculture relies heavily on chemical fertilizers and toxic pesticides, which devastate ecosystems. Organic Farming is an eco-friendly technology that restores, maintains, and enhances ecological balance.

Benefits of Organic Farming

  • Substitutes costlier chemical inputs with cheaper, locally produced organic inputs.
  • Generates income through exports due to rising global demand.
  • Provides food with higher nutritional value.
  • Requires more labor input, making it highly suitable for labor-surplus countries like India.

Limitations of Organic Farming

  • Yields from organic farming are generally lower than modern agricultural farming in the initial years.
  • Organic produce has a shorter shelf life and may have more blemishes.
  • Limited choice in the production of off-season crops.
