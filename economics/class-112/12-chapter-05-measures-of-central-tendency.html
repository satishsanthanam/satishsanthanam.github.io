Class 112 — Chapter 5: Measures of Central Tendency

1. Introduction to Central Tendency

A measure of central tendency is a single summary value that represents an entire set of data by identifying its central position. It provides a quick, numerical method to explain data in brief.

The three most commonly used statistical averages are:

  • Arithmetic Mean
  • Median
  • Mode

Other less common averages include the Geometric Mean and Harmonic Mean, which are suitable in specific situations.

2. Arithmetic Mean

The Arithmetic Mean (or simply mean) is defined as the sum of the values of all observations divided by the total number of observations. It is denoted by $\bar{X}$.

Arithmetic Mean for Ungrouped (Individual) Data

There are three methods to calculate the mean for ungrouped data:

  • Direct Method:
    $$\bar{X} = \frac{\sum X}{N}$$ Where $\sum X$ is the sum of all observations, and $N$ is the total number of observations.
  • Assumed Mean Method: Used when the number of observations is large to simplify calculations.
    $$\bar{X} = A + \frac{\sum d}{N}$$ Where $A$ is the assumed mean, and $d = X - A$ represents the deviations of observations from the assumed mean.
  • Step Deviation Method: Further simplifies calculations by dividing deviations by a common factor $c$.
    $$\bar{X} = A + \frac{\sum d'}{N} \times c$$ Where $d' = \frac{X - A}{c}$ and $c$ is the common factor.

Arithmetic Mean for Grouped Data

Discrete Series

  • Direct Method:
    $$\bar{X} = \frac{\sum fX}{\sum f}$$ Where $f$ is the frequency of each observation $X$.
  • Assumed Mean Method:
    $$\bar{X} = A + \frac{\sum fd}{\sum f}$$ Where $d = X - A$.
  • Step Deviation Method:
    $$\bar{X} = A + \left(\frac{\sum fd'}{\sum f}\right) \times c$$ Where $d' = \frac{X - A}{c}$.

Continuous Series

In a continuous series, class intervals are given. The mid-points ($m$) of these class intervals are used as the representative values for calculation.

  • Direct Method:
    $$\bar{X} = \frac{\sum fm}{\sum f}$$
  • Step Deviation Method:
    $$\bar{X} = A + \left(\frac{\sum fd'}{\sum f}\right) \times c$$ Where $d' = \frac{m - A}{c}$ and $m$ is the mid-point of each class.

Properties of Arithmetic Mean
  1. The algebraic sum of deviations of items from their arithmetic mean is always zero: $\sum (X - \bar{X}) = 0$.
  2. The arithmetic mean is highly affected by extreme values (outliers).

Weighted Arithmetic Mean

When different items hold varying levels of importance, weights ($w$) are assigned to them. The weighted arithmetic mean is calculated as:

$$\bar{X}_w = \frac{\sum wX}{\sum w}$$
3. Median

The Median is the positional value of the variable that divides an ordered distribution into two equal parts. One half of the values are greater than or equal to the median, and the other half are less than or equal to it.

Computation of Median
  • Individual Series: Arrange the data in ascending or descending order.
    • If $N$ is odd, the median is the value of the $\left(\frac{N+1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}$ item.
    • If $N$ is even, the median is the arithmetic mean of the two middle values: $\frac{\text{Value of } (N/2)^{\text{th}} \text{ item} + \text{Value of } (N/2 + 1)^{\text{th}} \text{ item}}{2}$.
  • Discrete Series: Find the cumulative frequencies ($c.f.$). The median is the value of the variable corresponding to the cumulative frequency that contains the $\left(\frac{N+1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}$ item.
  • Continuous Series: Locate the median class where the cumulative frequency contains the $\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}$ item. The median is calculated using:
    $$\text{Median} = L + \frac{\frac{N}{2} - c.f.}{f} \times h$$ Where:
    • $L$ = lower limit of the median class
    • $c.f.$ = cumulative frequency of the class preceding the median class
    • $f$ = frequency of the median class
    • $h$ = magnitude/width of the median class interval

Quartiles and Percentiles

These partition values divide the distribution into more than two parts:

  • Quartiles divide the data into four equal parts ($Q_1$, $Q_2$, $Q_3$).
    • First Quartile ($Q_1$): Size of $\frac{N+1}{4}^{\text{th}}$ item.
    • Third Quartile ($Q_3$): Size of $\frac{3(N+1)}{4}^{\text{th}}$ item.
  • Percentiles divide the distribution into 100 equal parts ($P_1, P_2, \dots, P_{99}$). $P_{50}$ is equal to the median.
4. Mode

The Mode ($M_o$) is the value that occurs most frequently in a distribution. It represents the point of maximum concentration of frequencies.

Computation of Mode
  • Discrete Series: Identified by inspection as the value with the highest frequency.
  • Continuous Series: Locate the modal class (class with the highest frequency). The mode is calculated using:
    $$M_o = L + \frac{D_1}{D_1 + D_2} \times h$$ Where:
    • $L$ = lower limit of the modal class
    • $D_1 = f_1 - f_0$ (difference between frequency of modal class and preceding class)
    • $D_2 = f_1 - f_2$ (difference between frequency of modal class and succeeding class)
    • $h$ = class interval width
5. Relative Position of Mean, Median, and Mode

The relative magnitude of the three measures of central tendency generally follows a specific order depending on the symmetry of the distribution:

  • In a symmetric distribution, Mean = Median = Mode.
  • In an asymmetric (skewed) distribution, the median always lies between the mean and the mode: $$M_e > M_i > M_o \quad \text{or} \quad M_e < M_i < M_o$$ (where $M_e$ is Mean, $M_i$ is Median, and $M_o$ is Mode).
