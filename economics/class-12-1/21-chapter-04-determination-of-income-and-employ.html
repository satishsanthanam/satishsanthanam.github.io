Class 12 (Part 1) — Chapter 4: Determination of Income and Employment

Aggregate Demand and its Components

In macroeconomics, terms like consumption and investment have dual connotations depending on whether they refer to planned or actual values.

Ex-Ante vs. Ex-Post Measures

  • Ex-Ante Measures: Refer to the planned or intended values of variables (consumption, investment, or output) in an economy.
  • Ex-Post Measures: Refer to the actual or realized values of these variables as measured at the end of the year.

The Consumption Function

The relationship between consumption and income is described by the consumption function. The simplest linear consumption function is represented as:

$$C = \bar{C} + cY$$

Where:

  • $C$ is the total consumption expenditure.
  • $\bar{C}$ is Autonomous Consumption, which is the consumption level when income is zero.
  • $cY$ is Induced Consumption, which depends directly on the level of income.
  • $c$ is the Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC), representing the rate of change of consumption as income changes.

Key Propensity Metrics

MetricDefinitionFormula
Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)Change in consumption per unit change in income.$$MPC = c = \frac{\Delta C}{\Delta Y}$$ (where $0 \le MPC \le 1$)
Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS)Change in savings per unit change in income.$$MPS = s = \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta Y} = 1 - c$$
Average Propensity to Consume (APC)Consumption per unit of income.$$APC = \frac{C}{Y}$$
Average Propensity to Save (APS)Savings per unit of income.$$APS = \frac{S}{Y}$$

The Investment Function

Investment is defined as addition to the stock of physical capital. For simplicity, we assume autonomous investment:

$$I = \bar{I}$$

Where $\bar{I}$ is a positive constant representing autonomous investment, independent of the level of income.

Determination of Income in a Two-Sector Model

In a two-sector economy (without government), the ex-ante aggregate demand ($AD$) is the sum of ex-ante consumption and ex-ante investment:

$$AD = C + I = \bar{C} + \bar{I} + cY$$

By defining total autonomous expenditure as $\bar{A} = \bar{C} + \bar{I}$, we simplify the aggregate demand function to:

$$AD = \bar{A} + cY$$

Equilibrium Condition

The final goods market is in equilibrium when planned supply (output $Y$) equals planned demand ($AD$):

$$Y = \bar{A} + cY$$

Inventory Investment

Inventories are stocks of unsold finished goods. Change in inventory is called Inventory Investment.

  • Planned Inventory Investment: The stock changes a firm deliberately plans to make.
  • Unplanned Inventory Investment: Occurs when actual sales differ from planned sales, leading to unexpected accumulation or depletion of stocks.
Determination of Equilibrium Income in the Short Run

Graphical Method

Equilibrium is analyzed graphically using two main components:

  • Aggregate Demand Curve: An upward-sloping line with intercept $\bar{A}$ and slope $c$.
  • Aggregate Supply Curve: Represented by a $45^\circ$ line from the origin, where every point has equal horizontal and vertical coordinates ($Y = AD$).
  • The intersection of the $AD$ curve and the $45^\circ$ line determines the equilibrium level of income ($E$).

Algebraic Method

From the equilibrium condition $Y = \bar{C} + \bar{I} + cY$, we solve for $Y$:

$$Y(1 - c) = \bar{C} + \bar{I} \implies Y = \frac{\bar{C} + \bar{I}}{1 - c}$$
The Multiplier Mechanism and Paradox of Thrift

The Multiplier Mechanism

An initial change in autonomous investment leads to a larger change in final output. This is called the Investment Multiplier ($k$):

$$k = \frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta \bar{A}} = \frac{1}{1 - c} = \frac{1}{s}$$

The multiplier process operates through successive rounds of consumption spending triggered by the initial injection of investment.

The Paradox of Thrift

The Paradox of Thrift states that if all people in an economy increase their propensity to save (MPS increases, MPC decreases), the total value of savings in the economy will either decline or remain unchanged, while the equilibrium level of income will decline.

Key Employment and Demand Concepts

Full Employment and Equilibrium

Equilibrium income does not guarantee full employment of resources.

  • Full Employment Level of Income: The level of income where all factors of production are fully employed.
  • Deficient Demand: Occurs when equilibrium demand is less than the full employment level of output, leading to involuntary unemployment and downward pressure on prices.
  • Excess Demand: Occurs when equilibrium demand exceeds the full employment level of output, leading to inflationary pressures.
