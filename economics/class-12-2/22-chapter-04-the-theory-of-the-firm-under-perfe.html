Economics: Class 12 (Part 2) — Chapter 4: The Theory of the Firm under Perfect Competition

4.1 Perfect Competition: Defining Features

A perfectly competitive market is characterized by a specific set of market conditions that lead to price-taking behavior for both buyers and sellers.

Defining Features of Perfect Competition

  • Large Number of Buyers and Sellers: No single buyer or seller is large enough to influence the market price. Each agent is a price-taker.
  • Homogenous Product: The products of all firms are identical in terms of quality, size, and features. Buyers are indifferent about which firm they purchase from.
  • Free Entry and Exit: Firms can easily enter or leave the industry in the long run without facing legal or financial barriers.
  • Perfect Information: All buyers and sellers have complete and instantaneous knowledge of prices, product quality, and market conditions.

Price-Taking Behaviour

Because of these features, a firm must accept the prevailing market price:

  • If a firm sets a price above the market price, it loses all its buyers to competitors.
  • There is no incentive to set a price below the market price because the firm can sell any quantity it wants at the market price.
4.2 Revenue Concepts

A firm earns revenue by selling its output in the market. Under perfect competition, price remains constant regardless of the quantity sold.

Total Revenue (TR)

Total Revenue is the total money receipt of a firm from the sale of its output. It is calculated as:

$$TR = p \times q$$

Where $p$ is the market price and $q$ is the quantity sold. Since $p$ is constant, the TR curve is an upward-sloping straight line passing through the origin.

Average Revenue (AR) and Marginal Revenue (MR)

  • Average Revenue (AR): Revenue earned per unit of output. Under perfect competition, AR equals the market price:
    $$AR = \frac{TR}{q} = \frac{p \times q}{q} = p$$
  • Marginal Revenue (MR): The change in total revenue resulting from selling one additional unit of output:
    $$MR = \frac{\Delta TR}{\Delta q} = p$$

Thus, for a perfectly competitive firm, MR = AR = p. The demand curve facing the firm is a horizontal straight line at the market price, representing a perfectly elastic demand.

4.3 Profit Maximisation

A firm's objective is to maximize its profit ($\pi$), defined as the difference between total revenue ($TR$) and total cost ($TC$):

$$\pi = TR - TC$$

Three Conditions for Profit Maximisation

For a firm to maximize profits at an output level $q_0$, three conditions must be satisfied:

  1. Condition 1: The market price $p$ must equal the marginal cost ($MC$):
    $$p = MC$$
  2. Condition 2: Marginal cost must be non-decreasing (i.e., the MC curve must be upward-sloping) at $q_0$.
  3. Condition 3:
    • In the short run, price must be greater than or equal to the average variable cost ($p \ge AVC$).
    • In the long run, price must be greater than or equal to the average cost ($p \ge AC$).
4.4 Supply Curve of a Firm

A firm's supply curve shows the levels of output it chooses to produce at different market prices.

Short Run Supply Curve

The short run supply curve of a firm is divided into two parts:

  • For prices equal to or greater than the minimum of $AVC$, the supply curve is the rising part of the Short-run Marginal Cost ($SMC$) curve.
  • For prices strictly less than the minimum of $AVC$, the firm produces zero output.

Long Run Supply Curve

The long run supply curve of a firm is divided into two parts:

  • For prices equal to or greater than the minimum of $LRAC$, the supply curve is the rising part of the Long-run Marginal Cost ($LRMC$) curve.
  • For prices strictly less than the minimum of $LRAC$, the firm produces zero output.

Shut Down Point and Break-Even Point

  • Shut Down Point: The minimum point of the $AVC$ curve in the short run (or $LRAC$ in the long run) below which the firm stops production.
  • Normal Profit: The minimum level of profit required to keep a firm in its current business. It is treated as an opportunity cost and is included in total cost.
  • Super-normal Profit: Profit earned over and above normal profit.
  • Break-even Point: The point on the supply curve where the firm earns only normal profits (where $p = \text{minimum } AC$).
4.5 Determinants of a Firm's Supply Curve

Any factor that shifts a firm's marginal cost curve will also shift its supply curve.

Key Determinants

  • Technological Progress: Organizational or technological innovations lower marginal costs, shifting the $MC$ curve rightward (downward). This shifts the supply curve to the right, meaning more output is supplied at any given price.
  • Input Prices: An increase in input prices (e.g., wages, raw materials) raises production costs, shifting the $MC$ curve leftward (upward). This shifts the supply curve to the left.
  • Unit Tax: A tax imposed per unit of output sold increases the firm's marginal and average costs by the tax amount $t$. This shifts the supply curve to the left.
4.6 Market Supply Curve

The market supply curve shows the aggregate output produced by all firms in the market at different prices.

Derivation of Market Supply

It is obtained by the horizontal summation of the individual supply curves of all firms in the market.

For a market with $n$ firms, the market supply $S_m(p)$ at price $p$ is:

$$S_m(p) = S_1(p) + S_2(p) + \dots + S_n(p)$$
4.7 Price Elasticity of Supply

The price elasticity of supply ($e_s$) measures the responsiveness of the quantity supplied of a good to changes in its market price.

Formula and Measurement

$$e_s = \frac{\text{Percentage change in quantity supplied}}{\text{Percentage change in price}} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}$$

Where $\Delta Q$ is the change in quantity supplied, and $\Delta P$ is the change in price.

Geometric Method

The elasticity of a straight-line supply curve depends on where it intersects the axes:

  • If the supply curve cuts the price-axis (Y-axis) in its positive range, $e_s > 1$.
  • If the supply curve passes through the origin, $e_s = 1$.
  • If the supply curve cuts the quantity-axis (X-axis) in its positive range, $e_s < 1$.
