English: Class 11 (Book 1) — Chapter 11: Letter-writing

Introduction to Letter-Writing

Letter-writing serves as a vital channel of communication, bridging geographical distances between individuals. Historically, before the advent of modern telecommunications like telephones and e-mails, letters were the primary medium for personal and official correspondence. It is a structured skill that requires deliberate development and adaptation based on the target audience.

Classification of Letters
  • Formal Letters : Written to conduct official business, convey information, or interface with public bodies, civic agencies, and corporate entities. Examples include applications, official inquiries, and complaints.
  • Informal Letters : Personal communications sent to friends, family, and close acquaintances, characterized by a flexible structure and a conversational tone.
Formal Letters: Structure, Steps, and Format

Writing an effective formal letter requires adherence to a systematic structure and a professional tone. The process involves specific steps and a standardized layout known as the Full-Block style .

Key Steps in Writing Formal Letters
  1. Introduction: Introduce oneself if writing for the first time, or refer to previous correspondence if responding to an earlier communication.
  2. Statement of Purpose: Clearly state the primary objective or reason for writing the letter.
  3. Action/Information Exchange: Specify the action or information required from the addressee, or explain actions already taken and supply necessary details.
  4. Conclusion: Urge necessary action to be taken or offer future assistance politely.
The Six Core Principles of Content
  • Purpose: The letter must have a clear, well-defined objective.
  • Audience: The content must be tailored specifically to the person to whom it is addressed.
  • Tone: The writer must adopt an appropriate, polite, and professional tone.
  • Completeness: The message must contain all necessary details to prevent ambiguity.
  • Action Required: The desired outcome or next steps must be explicitly stated.
  • Conciseness: The expression must be brief, direct, and free of unnecessary jargon.
The 7-Point Standard Format
  1. Name and Address of Sender: Placed at the top. Corporate entities often use printed letterheads containing company details and executive designations.
  2. Name and Address of Addressee: Clearly identifies the recipient.
  3. Date: Essential for record-keeping and chronological tracking.
  4. Mode of Address (Salutation): Tailored to the relationship:
    • Dear Sir/Madam: Used for total strangers.
    • Dear Mr/Ms/Dr/Professor + Surname: Used for formal relationships where the recipient is known by name but not personally.
    • Dear [First Name]: Used for semi-formal relationships.
  5. Reference/Subject Line: A concise subject line placed above the salutation for quick reference.
  6. Content of Letter: Organized logically into two or three distinct paragraphs.
  7. Complimentary Close and Signature: Polite sign-off followed by 'Yours sincerely' or 'Yours truly' and the writer's signature.
The Full-Block Style Layout Rules

Modern correspondence utilizes the Full-Block style , which mandates specific formatting choices:

  • All elements of the letter (sender address, date, recipient address, subject, body, and closing) are strictly aligned to the left margin.
  • Commas are omitted at the end of each line in the address blocks.
  • Hanging participles such as 'Thanking you' or 'Awaiting your reply' must be avoided. Instead, complete sentences like 'Thank you' or 'I look forward to your reply' should be used.

"Sub: Your application No. F323 for a new gas connection... Dear Mr Thomas... With regards, Yours sincerely, Ritu Patel"

Literary Interpretation & Contextual Significance: This structural excerpt illustrates the clean, left-aligned layout of the Full-Block style . It demonstrates how modern administrative correspondence prioritizes readability and rapid processing by removing unnecessary punctuation from addresses and using a direct, bolded subject line.

The textbook highlights the importance of replacing archaic, hackneyed phrases with personalized, modern expressions:

Informal Letters and Semi-Formal Inquiries

Informal letters are highly flexible and personal. However, their structural principles can also be adapted to seek information from official bodies in a semi-formal manner.

Informal Letter Characteristics
  • The format is highly flexible; often, only the sender's city and date are placed at the top.
  • The salutation is personal (e.g., 'Dear Sujata'), and the closing is affectionate (e.g., 'Yours affectionately', 'With love', 'Take care').
  • The tone is conversational, warm, and subjective.
Semi-Formal Inquiry Example: Vacation Planning

When writing to an organization like a tourism office, a flexible yet polite format is used to request information:

  • Sender Address: Can be placed in the top right-hand corner or on the left.
  • Recipient Address: Placed on the left (e.g., 'The Manager, Himachal Tourism, Mall Road, Shimla').
  • Salutation: 'Dear Sir' or 'Dear Madam'.
  • Body: Clear paragraphs detailing travel plans (e.g., visiting Dharamsala during Dussehra) and requesting specific resources like lodging details, city maps, and brochures.
  • Closing Rules: Use 'Yours sincerely' if the recipient is addressed by name; use 'Yours faithfully' if the letter began with 'Dear Sir' or 'Madam'.
Job Applications and Curriculum Vitae (CV)

Applying for employment is a critical real-world application of formal letter-writing. It consists of two essential components: a covering letter and a structured resume.

Literary Profile: The Professional Job Applicant

  • Traits & Temperament: Sincere, honest, hardworking, friendly, outgoing, and possessing excellent spoken and written communication skills.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: Typically a recent graduate or diploma holder entering the professional workforce, responding to public advertisements (e.g., in newspapers like The Hindu ).
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Represents the transition from academic life to professional responsibility; symbolizes self-presentation, structured credentials, and civic integration.
Structure of a Curriculum Vitae (CV) / Resume

A CV, resume, or biodata is a structured summary of personal, academic, and professional particulars:

  • Personal Details: Name, Address, Telephone Number, E-mail ID, and Date of Birth.
  • Academic Qualifications: Presented in a tabular format detailing the Examination (S.S.C, Diploma, Degree), Board/University, Subjects, Year of Passing, and Division/Grade.
  • Professional Experience: Chronological listing of employment, beginning with the most recent position.
  • Skills & Languages: Technical competencies and languages known.
  • Personal Attributes: Hobbies, interests, and notable achievements.
  • References: Names and contact details of credible individuals (such as a school Principal) who can certify the applicant's character and conduct.
The Covering Letter

The covering letter introduces the applicant, references the job advertisement, highlights key qualifications, and requests an interview. It must be concise, professional, and aligned to the left margin in the Full-Block style .

Practical Writing Activities

The chapter concludes with five practical writing prompts designed to test the student's mastery of formal and informal letter-writing formats:

  1. Academic Inquiry: A formal letter to the Registrar, Examination Branch, CBSE, requesting a missing Class XII Roll Number card.
  2. Civic Advocacy: A formal letter to the President of the Residents' Welfare Association (RWA) suggesting measures to solve water scarcity and conserve water.
  3. Editorial Correspondence (Public Issues): A letter to the editor of a newspaper expressing views on the deteriorating law and order situation in the city.
  4. Personal Narrative: An informal letter to a friend narrating personal experiences during a rescue operation.
  5. Cultural Review: A letter to the Editor of a magazine describing a recently visited art gallery or a dance performance.
