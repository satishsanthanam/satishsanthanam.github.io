English: Class 11 (Book 3) — Chapter 1: The Peacock

Introduction to Poetry

The word poetry originates from a Greek word meaning 'to make'. A poet is thus a maker and the poem is something that is made or created. While no single definition of poetry is possible, it is characterized by several distinct features:

  • Musical Quality : Poetry utilizes rhythm, pitch, and metre to create a melodic flow.
  • Figures of Speech : It frequently employs devices such as simile and metaphor to enrich meaning.
  • Structural Diversity : Includes traditional forms as well as modern poems free from formal restrictions, alongside playful forms like haiku and limerick .
About the Poet

Literary Profile: Sujata Bhatt

  • Traits & Temperament: Renowned for her vivid imagery, multicultural perspective, and seamless blending of languages and cultural motifs.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: Born in 1956, educated in the USA, and currently resides in Germany. She won the prestigious Commonwealth Poetry Prize (Asia section) for her debut collection Brunizem (1988).
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Explores themes of exile, memory, nature's elusive beauty, and linguistic identity. She has also translated Gujarati poetry into English.
Poem Analysis: The Peacock
Stanza 1: The Elusive Presence

His loud sharp call / seems to come from nowhere. / Then, a flash of turquoise / in the pipal tree...

Literary Interpretation & Contextual Significance: The poem opens with an auditory cue—the peacock's "loud sharp call"—which is disorienting yet striking, seeming to emerge from "nowhere". This immediately establishes the bird's elusive, almost mystical nature. The visual transition is sudden: a "flash of turquoise" against the sacred pipal tree . The description focuses on fragments—the arched slender neck, a descending motion, and a fleeting glimpse of the tail—emphasizing that the peacock is rarely seen fully or statically, but rather in dynamic, transient moments.

Stanza 2: The Ritual of Concentration

I was told / that you have to sit in the veranda / And read a book... The moment you begin to live / inside the book / A blue shadow will fall over you.

Literary Interpretation & Contextual Significance: The poet introduces a specific ritual required to witness the peacock. It demands deep concentration, specifically losing oneself "inside" a favorite book on the veranda. This paradox suggests that only when one ceases to actively search for beauty (by immersing oneself in literature) does nature reveal itself. The "blue shadow" represents the physical and spiritual presence of the peacock, casting a quiet, transformative spell over the observer's environment.

Stanza 3: The Sensory Shift and Climactic Glimpse

The wind will change direction, / The steady hum of bees... will stop... It is the tail that has to blink / For eyes that are always open.

Literary Interpretation & Contextual Significance: The arrival of the peacock alters the entire sensory landscape: the wind shifts, the bees fall silent, and the domestic cat awakens. This dramatic pause in nature signals a sacred moment. When the reader looks up, they catch the peacock gathering its tail. The closing lines present a brilliant inversion: the "eyes" on the peacock's tail feathers "blink" (close) as the tail is gathered, acting as surrogates for the real, ever-watchful eyes of the bird. The contrast of "violet fringed with golden amber" highlights the rich, royal palette of the bird's plumage.

Literary Devices & Vocabulary Matrix
Literary Device / Vocabulary Exact Textual Example Contextual Meaning & Subtext Pedagogical / Stylistic Purpose
Auditory Imagery "His loud sharp call" The piercing sound of the peacock that disrupts the silence. Establishes the bird's presence before it is physically seen, building anticipation.
Visual Imagery (Color) "flash of turquoise", "blue shadow", "violet fringed with golden amber" The brilliant, royal colors of the peacock's feathers and body. Creates a vivid, highly aestheticized portrait of the bird, contrasting with the mundane veranda.
Synecdoche / Inversion "It is the tail that has to blink / For eyes that are always open." The ocelli (eye-like spots) on the tail feathers are described as blinking when the tail folds. Creates a surreal, mythic quality, suggesting nature is constantly observing us even when we think we are observing it.
Kinetic Imagery "slender neck arched", "descends", "darts away" The rapid, graceful movements of the peacock. Conveys the elusive, fleeting nature of wild beauty.
Vocabulary: Turquoise "a flash of turquoise" A greenish-blue semi-precious stone color. Denotes exotic, precious, and vibrant natural beauty.
Vocabulary: Darts "as he darts away" Moves suddenly and rapidly. Emphasizes the quickness and shy nature of the bird.
Study Guide & Comprehension
Understanding the Poem: Key Questions
  1. Visualizing the Peacock: The lines describing the "flash of turquoise", "slender neck arched", and "violet fringed with golden amber" build a rich, multi-hued visual image of the bird.
  2. Cues of Presence: The auditory "loud sharp call", the sudden "blue shadow", the shift in wind direction, the silencing of the bees' hum, and the cat stretching are all environmental cues signaling the peacock's arrival.
  3. The Tail and the Eyes: The connection between the tail's ocelli and real eyes suggests a mystical vigilance. The tail "blinks" as it folds, protecting the "eyes that are always open" (the eternal watchfulness of nature).
  4. Capturing Elusiveness: The poem captures elusiveness by showing that the peacock is never fully stationary; it is always "descending", "darting away", or "turning away as he gathers his tail". It requires absolute, indirect concentration to be seen.
Try This Out & Cultural Context
  • The Peacock in Literature: While Western literature often associates the peacock with vanity ("as proud as a peacock"), Indian literature associates it with grace, rain, romance, and divine vehicles (such as Kartikeya's vahana).
  • National Bird of India: Chosen for its rich religious and cultural involvement in Indian heritage, its widespread distribution across the subcontinent, and its majestic, unmistakable beauty.
