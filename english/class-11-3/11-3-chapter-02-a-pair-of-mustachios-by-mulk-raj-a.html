English: Class 11 (Book 3) — Chapter 2: A Pair of Mustachios

Chapter Overview & Author Profile

Literary Profile: Mulk Raj Anand

  • Traits & Temperament: Celebrated pioneer of Indo-Anglian fiction, known for his deep empathy for the marginalized, sharp social satire, and realistic portrayal of Indian society.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: Born in Peshawar (1905–2004); educated at Lahore, London, and Cambridge. His works reflect a keen eye for the pretensions of the upper classes and a warm compassion for the poor.
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Social realism, anti-feudalism, critique of the caste system, and the clash between traditional conventions and modern economic realities.

The short story A Pair of Mustachios is a brilliant, light-hearted satire that exposes the absurdity of the rigid class structures and feudal pride in colonial India. Through the medium of facial hair, Anand illustrates how symbolic status is fiercely guarded even when the material basis for that status has completely eroded.

The Taxonomy of Mustachios (Social Stratification)

In the story's setting, mustachios serve as permanent, visible boundary-lines that demarcate social classes, far more rigidly than European clothing conventions. Anand presents a detailed classification of these styles:

Moustache Style Associated Class / Group Visual Description Symbolic Meaning & Subtext
Lion Moustache Rajas, Maharajas, Nawabs, and English Army Generals Fearsome and upstanding Devotion to the King Emperor and supreme feudal authority.
Tiger Moustache Feudal Gentry (decaying aristocracy) Uncanny, several-pointed Unbending, unchanging pride in past glory and ancestral lineage.
Goat Moustache Nouveau Riche, Commercial Bourgeoisie, and Shopkeepers Thin, indifferent line with adjustable tips Opportunism; tips can be turned up for power or down for humility.
Charlie Chaplin Moustache Lower Middle Class, Clerks, and Professionals Half-and-half compromise Modernity vs. tradition; mimicking the British Curzon cut.
Sheep Moustache Coolies and lower orders Drooping and simple Subservience and lack of social power.
Mouse Moustache Peasants Small and insignificant Complete subjugation and vulnerability to exploitation.
Character Profiles & Socio-Economic Dynamics

Literary Profile: Khan Azam Khan

  • Traits & Temperament: Tall, middle-aged, handsome, and dignified. He is inordinately proud, headstrong, foolish, and fiercely protective of his ancestral honor.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: Claims descent from an ancient Afghan family of Moghul court noblemen. He lives in a dilapidated Moghul-style house and wears a faded gold-brocaded waistcoat, but is completely impoverished.
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Represents the decaying, obsolete feudal order clinging to hollow symbols of status while losing all material substance.
