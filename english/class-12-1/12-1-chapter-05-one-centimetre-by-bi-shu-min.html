English: Class 12 (Book 1) — Chapter 5: One Centimetre

Author & Character Profiles

Literary Profile: Bi Shu-min (Author)

  • Traits & Temperament: Compassionate, observant, and deeply analytical. Her background as a physician infuses her writing with psychological precision and a keen understanding of human vulnerability.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: Born in 1952; served as a doctor in the Chinese military for over twenty years before earning a Master's degree in literature from Beijing Teacher's College. She is celebrated as one of China's foremost contemporary writers.
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Focuses on the moral dilemmas of ordinary citizens, the psychological development of children, and the quiet heroism found in maternal love and everyday struggles.

Literary Profile: Tao Ying (Protagonist)

  • Traits & Temperament: Frugal, practical, yet fiercely protective of her dignity and her son's moral upbringing. She is highly self-aware and constantly strives to project an image of refinement and integrity.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: A working-class mother employed as a cook in a factory canteen, baking wheat cakes over an open fire. She lives a modest life where every cent counts.
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Represents the struggle of the working class to maintain self-respect and raise "cultured" children in a highly bureaucratic and cynical society.

Literary Profile: Xiao Ye

  • Traits & Temperament: Innocent, observant, and highly sensitive to rituals and parental promises. He possesses a strong sense of childhood pride and justice.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: Tao Ying's five-year-old son, growing up in a loving but financially constrained household.
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Symbolizes pure, uncorrupted childhood innocence and the psychological vulnerability of a child whose trust in his mother is shaken by adult cynicism.
Detailed Narrative Progression
1. The Bus Journey & The Ticket Ritual

The story opens by establishing Tao Ying 's pragmatic approach to daily expenses. When traveling alone, she often avoids buying a bus ticket, viewing it as a harmless saving and a minor punishment for careless conductors. However, when accompanied by her son Xiao Ye , her behavior changes completely. She insists on buying two tickets, even though the conductor points out that Xiao Ye is one centimetre short of the height requirement. For Tao Ying, the twenty cents spent is an investment in her son's self-esteem and a lesson in honesty, demonstrating her commitment to being an exemplary mother.

2. The Temple Incident & The Accusation

Tao Ying takes Xiao Ye on a rare outing to a grand temple using a complimentary ticket gifted by Lao Chiang . At the temple gates, a rude young guard in a red uniform stops them, claiming Xiao Ye is over the 110-centimetre free-entry limit. He accuses Tao Ying of trying to sneak her son in for free. When measured against an inaccurate red line on the wall (described metaphorically as an "earthworm"), Xiao Ye indeed appears to be over the limit. In a moment of panic and frustration, Tao Ying slaps her son's head to smooth down his fluffy hair, shocking the crowd and deeply hurting Xiao Ye's feelings.

3. The Aftermath: Distrust & The Flattering Scales

Deeply humiliated, Tao Ying leaves the temple without entering. The incident severely damages the trust between mother and son. Xiao Ye becomes sullen and suspicious, believing his mother hit him and refused to buy a ticket out of greed. This distrust is compounded when Xiao Ye is measured by an old woman's "flattering scales" outside, which falsely show him to be 1.11 metres tall. Back home, Tao Ying measures her sleeping son with her own tape and confirms he is actually 1.09 metres. She realizes she must vindicate herself to restore her son's faith in her integrity.

4. The Quest for Vindication: Writing the Letter

Determined to clear her name, Tao Ying decides to write a letter to the temple administration. Lacking formal writing skills, she seeks the help of "The Writer," a factory colleague. He advises her to make the letter emotionally moving and dramatic. Tao Ying sends the letter and waits anxiously for weeks, scanning newspapers and listening to the radio, hoping for a resolution that will melt the "ice" in her son's eyes.

5. The Resolution: Truth Restored

Two elderly officials from the temple administration visit Tao Ying's home to investigate. They measure Xiao Ye with a precise metallic tape and find him to be exactly 1.10 metres. Recognizing that their gate mark was inaccurate and accounting for a month's growth, they admit their mistake and offer a five-dollar refund or free tickets. Tao Ying refuses the monetary compensation. Instead, she asks the officials to explain the truth directly to Xiao Ye, ensuring he knows his mother did nothing wrong. Her dignity and her son's trust are successfully restored.

Thematic Analysis
The Burden of Exemplary Motherhood

The narrative explores the immense psychological pressure on working-class parents to raise "cultured" children. Tao Ying views herself as the center of her son's universe and constantly monitors her behavior—from how she eats watermelons to how she purchases bus tickets—to set a perfect example. This self-imposed standard makes the guard's accusation of dishonesty particularly devastating, as it threatens to destroy her standing as a moral role model in her son's eyes.

Arbitrary Rules vs. Human Dignity

The story highlights how rigid, bureaucratic rules (symbolized by the 110-centimetre height limit) can be weaponized by petty officials to humiliate ordinary citizens. The "one centimetre" difference becomes a battleground for human dignity. While the system views the measurement as a financial boundary, Tao Ying and Xiao Ye experience it as a measure of their honesty, self-worth, and social standing.

The Value of Truth and Trust

At its core, the story is about the preservation of maternal trust. Tao Ying's refusal of the five-dollar compensation underscores that truth and respect cannot be bought. Her ultimate victory is not financial or institutional; it is the restoration of her son's belief in her integrity, proving that a mother's moral standing is worth more than any material compensation.

Key Excerpts & Interpretations

"To be able to purchase self-esteem with twenty cents is something that can only happen in childhood and certainly no mother can resist an opportunity to make her son happy."

Literary Interpretation & Contextual Significance: This passage highlights the contrast between the adult world of financial calculation and the child's world of emotional value. For Tao Ying, the twenty cents spent on an unnecessary ticket is not a waste, but a small price to pay to preserve her son's pride and belief in his own growth. It underscores her deep maternal love and her understanding of a child's psychology.

"Gentlemen, would you be so kind as to explain to my son exactly what happened on that day. Please tell him that his mother has not done anything wrong..."

Literary Interpretation & Contextual Significance: This is the emotional climax of the story. By rejecting the monetary compensation and instead requesting a moral explanation for her son, Tao Ying redefines the terms of justice. She demonstrates that her struggle was never about the five dollars or the free entry, but about preserving her integrity and her sacred bond of trust with her child.
