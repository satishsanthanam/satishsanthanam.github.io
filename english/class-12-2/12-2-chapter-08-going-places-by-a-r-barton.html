English: Class 12 (Book 2) — Chapter 8: Going Places

Chapter Overview & Author Profile

Literary Profile: A. R. Barton

  • Traits & Temperament: Modern writer based in Zurich; writes with deep psychological insight into the minds of adolescents.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: Explores the post-war European working-class urban landscape, focusing on the stark contrast between industrial labor and the glamorous world of sports and fashion.
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Specializes in depicting adolescent fantasising , escapism, and the psychological phenomenon of hero worship .

The story Going Places explores the inner life of a working-class teenage girl named Sophie, who escapes her drab, suffocating reality through wild, romanticized fantasies. The narrative highlights the clash between youthful dreams and the harsh, predetermined socio-economic realities of the working class.

Character Profiles & Analytical Matrix

Literary Profile: Sophie

  • Traits & Temperament: Highly imaginative, unrealistic, escapist, and deeply romantic. She constantly seeks to distance herself from her working-class background.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: Daughter of a working-class family, destined to work in a local biscuit factory upon leaving school.
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Represents the vulnerability of youth to media-driven glamour, embodying the psychological defense mechanism of daydreaming to cope with poverty.

Literary Profile: Jansie

  • Traits & Temperament: Realistic, practical, down-to-earth, and gossipy. She accepts her social reality without bitterness.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: Sophie's classmate and neighbor; shares the same working-class destiny but remains grounded in her expectations.
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Serves as a direct foil to Sophie, representing common sense, social realism, and the acceptance of one's class limitations.

Literary Profile: Geoff

  • Traits & Temperament: Quiet, introspective, reticent, and deeply loyal to his family. He speaks very little, making him an enigmatic figure to Sophie.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: Sophie's older brother; an apprentice mechanic who travels to the far side of the city for work.
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Represents a gateway to the vast, unknown outer world for Sophie. His silence is romanticized by Sophie as a repository of exotic secrets.

Literary Profile: Danny Casey

  • Traits & Temperament: Shy, brilliant, youthful, and exceptionally talented.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: A young Irish football prodigy playing for the local club, United.
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: The ultimate object of Sophie's hero worship . He is not a real character in Sophie's personal life, but a blank canvas onto which she projects her romantic and escapist desires.
Detailed Narrative Arc & Plot Breakdown
1. The Walk Home: Dreams vs. Reality
  • Sophie declares her ambition to open a high-end boutique after school, comparing herself to the fashion icon Mary Quant .
  • Jansie reminds her of their financial reality, pointing out that opening a boutique requires capital they do not possess.
  • Sophie shifts her fantasy to becoming a manager, an actress, or a fashion designer to raise the money, refusing to accept her assignment to the biscuit factory .
2. The Domestic Environment
  • Sophie enters her small, cluttered, and steam-filled home. Her father is eating shepherd's pie, grimy and sweaty from a day of hard labor.
  • Her mother is stooped over the sink, working endlessly. Sophie feels a physical tightening in her throat , representing the suffocating nature of her domestic reality.
  • Little Derek cheekily remarks that Sophie thinks money grows on trees, highlighting the family's awareness of her unrealistic nature.
3. The Fabricated Meeting with Danny Casey
  • Sophie visits Geoff in his room, where he is working on his motorcycle. She envies his silence, imagining he travels to exotic places.
  • To gain Geoff's attention and enter his world, Sophie invents a story: she claims to have met the football star Danny Casey at the arcade outside Royce's window.
  • She describes Casey's physical appearance in detail: green, gentle eyes, not very tall, and a soft Irish accent. She claims they planned to meet again next week for an autograph.
4. The Weekly Pilgrimage to the Football Match
  • On Saturday, the family makes their customary, almost religious journey to watch United play.
  • Danny Casey scores a brilliant second goal, showcasing his genius by dodging defenders and beating the goalkeeper.
  • The family celebrates, with the father going to the pub and little Derek declaring that Ireland will win the World Cup. Sophie glows with intense pride.
5. The Threat of Exposure
  • The following week, Jansie confronts Sophie about the meeting with Danny Casey, having heard about it from her brother Frank, who was told by Geoff.
  • Sophie panics, fearing her father will find out and cause a major row. She is relieved to discover that Geoff did not reveal the detail about her planned "date" with Casey.
  • Sophie covers up by claiming the meeting was brief and that they couldn't get an autograph because neither had a pen.
6. The Canal and the Crushing Weight of Reality
  • After dark, Sophie walks to a secluded spot by the canal, sitting on a wooden bench beneath a solitary elm to wait for Danny Casey.
  • She actively constructs the fantasy of his arrival, imagining his footsteps and her own excitement.
  • As time passes, the harsh truth sets in. She experiences the pangs of doubt and realizes he will not come.
  • She contemplates the sadness of her reality and the disappointment of her brother Geoff when she returns with no proof. She is trapped in a cycle of self-inflicted melancholy.
Literary Devices, Colloquialisms & Vocabulary Matrix
Literary Device / Colloquial Phrase Exact Textual Example / Quote Contextual Meaning & Subtext Pedagogical / Stylistic Purpose
Simile "Words had to be prized out of him like stones out of a ground." Geoff is extremely reticent and rarely speaks; extracting information from him requires immense effort. Emphasizes Geoff's quiet, reserved nature and Sophie's frustration with his silence.
Metaphor / Physical Manifestation "Sophie felt a tightening in her throat." A physical sensation of suffocation caused by the steam, clutter, and poverty of her home. Conveys the oppressive, claustrophobic atmosphere of her working-class domestic life.
Metaphor "On Saturday they made their weekly pilgrimage to watch United." The family's weekly trip to the football stadium is treated with the devotion and reverence of a religious ritual. Highlights the cultural importance of football as an escapist outlet for the working class.
Metaphor "...saw him ghost past the lumbering defenders..." Danny Casey moves silently, effortlessly, and deceptively past the opposing team's defense. Emphasizes Casey's extraordinary skill, agility, and almost supernatural presence on the field.
Colloquialism "chuffed as anything" Extremely pleased, delighted, or proud. Establishes the authentic working-class British/Irish setting and dialogue of the characters.
Colloquialism "nosey" Inquisitive, prying, or overly interested in other people's business. Highlights Jansie's character trait of wanting to know and spread neighborhood gossip.
Textual Excerpts & Analytical Commentaries

"Sophie watched her back stooped over the sink and wondered at the incongruity of the delicate bow which fastened her apron strings. The delicate-seeming bow and the crooked back."

Literary Interpretation & Contextual Significance: This passage highlights the central theme of the story: the stark contrast between beauty/aspiration and harsh reality. The "crooked back" represents the physical toll of endless domestic labor endured by working-class women, while the "delicate bow" represents a futile, fragile attempt at grace and beauty. Sophie recognizes this incongruity , which mirrors her own struggle to reconcile her glamorous dreams with her drab reality.

"Here I sit, she said to herself, wishing Danny would come, wishing he would come and sensing the time passing. I feel the pangs of doubt stirring inside me... It is a hard thing, this sadness."

Literary Interpretation & Contextual Significance: This internal monologue marks the climax of Sophie's psychological journey. For the first time, her fantasy world is forced to confront the unyielding nature of reality. The "pangs of doubt" represent her growing self-awareness that her meeting with Danny Casey was entirely self-fabricated. The "sadness" she feels is not just for a missed date, but the crushing realization that she is trapped in her socio-economic reality, unable to bridge the gap between her dreams and her life.

Textbook Questions & Model Solutions
Think as you read (Page 77)
  1. Where was it most likely that the two girls would find work after school?
    It was highly likely that both Sophie and Jansie would find work in the local biscuit factory , as they were both "earmarked" for it due to their working-class backgrounds and lack of financial resources.
  2. What were the options that Sophie was dreaming of? Why does Jansie discourage her from having such dreams?
    Sophie dreamed of opening a boutique, becoming a manager, an actress, or a fashion designer. Jansie discourages her because she is realistic and knows that these options require significant money and social standing, neither of which Sophie's family possesses. Jansie wants Sophie to be sensible and accept their reality.
Think as you read (Page 79)
  1. Why did Sophie wriggle when Geoff told her father that she had met Danny Casey?
    Sophie wriggled because she knew her father would be highly skeptical, angry, and dismissive of her "wild stories." She also feared that her lie would be exposed under his aggressive questioning.
  2. Does Geoff believe what Sophie says about her meeting with Danny Casey?
    No, Geoff does not truly believe her. He calls it "the unlikeliest thing I ever heard" and repeatedly tells her that Casey will never show up. However, he harbors a quiet wish that it were true, which is why he listens to her details.
  3. Does her father believe her story?
    No, her father completely disbelieves her. He looks at her with disdain, mutters about it being another of her "wild stories," and warns her that she is going to talk herself into a load of trouble.
  4. How does Sophie include her brother Geoff in her fantasy of her future?
    Sophie imagines Geoff riding his motorcycle, wearing new, shining black leathers, with her sitting behind him in a yellow dress with a flying cape. She views him as a gateway to a vast, glamorous world that awaits their arrival, where the world rises to applaud them.
  5. Which country did Danny Casey play for?
    Danny Casey played for Ireland .
Think as you read (Page 83)
  1. Why didn’t Sophie want Jansie to know about her story with Danny?
    Sophie knew that Jansie was "nosey" and a gossip. If Jansie found out, she would tell the whole neighborhood, leading to crowds of people asking her father about it, which would result in a massive family row and expose her lie.
  2. Did Sophie really meet Danny Casey?
    No, Sophie did not meet Danny Casey. The entire encounter was a product of her active imagination, born out of her intense hero worship and desire to escape her mundane life.
  3. Which was the only occasion when she got to see Danny Casey in person?
    The only occasion she saw him in person was from the spectator stands on Saturday, when she, her father, and her brothers went to watch United play their weekly football match.
