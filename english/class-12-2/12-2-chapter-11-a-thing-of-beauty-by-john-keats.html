English: Class 12 (Book 2) — Chapter 11: My Mother at Sixty-six

About the Poet & Context

Literary Profile: Kamala Das

  • Traits & Temperament: Sensitive, deeply observant, expressive of raw human emotions, capturing the complex subtleties of human relationships in lyrical idiom.
  • Socio-Economic & Contextual Background: Born in Malabar, Kerala (1934-2009). Recognized as one of India's foremost poets, writing in both English and Malayalam (under the pen name Madhavikutty ). Her works are celebrated for their originality, versatility, and indigenous flavor of the soil.
  • Thematic & Symbolic Motifs: Explores themes of aging, maternal bonds, separation anxiety, mortality, and the silent pain of filial helplessness.

The poem My Mother at Sixty-six serves as a poignant exploration of the universal theme of ageing and the inevitable fear of losing one's parents. It captures a highly personal yet universally relatable moment of transition and emotional vulnerability.

Thematic & Narrative Breakdown
The Journey and the Realization of Decay

The poem opens with a physical journey that mirrors an emotional transition. The poet is driving from her parent's home to the Cochin airport on a Friday morning, with her elderly mother sitting beside her.

"I saw my mother, beside me, doze, open mouthed, her face ashen like that of a corpse and realised with pain that she was as old as she looked..."

Literary Interpretation & Contextual Significance: The physical description of the mother—dozing, open-mouthed, with a face "ashen like that of a corpse"—vividly conveys her advanced age and proximity to death. The word ashen suggests a loss of color, vitality, and life, resembling ashes. This sudden realization strikes the poet with sharp emotional pain, forcing her to confront the reality of her mother's mortality.

The Contrast of Youth and Vitality

To escape the painful realization of her mother's impending demise, the poet diverts her attention outside the car window, encountering a starkly contrasting world of energy and life.

"...put that thought away, and looked out at Young Trees sprinting, the merry children spilling out of their homes..."

Literary Interpretation & Contextual Significance: The external world is filled with movement and joy, serving as a direct contrast to the passive, decaying state of the mother inside the car. The Young Trees sprinting symbolize the rapid passage of time and youth, while the merry children spilling represent the abundance of life, energy, and unblemished joy. This juxtaposition highlights the cycle of life, where youth and old age coexist in stark contrast.

The Airport Farewell and the Mask of Optimism

At the airport, after the security check, the physical distance between the poet and her mother increases, mirroring the emotional and existential distance brought by aging.

"...I looked again at her, wan, pale as a late winter’s moon and felt that old familiar ache, my childhood’s fear, but all I said was, see you soon, Amma, all I did was smile and smile and smile......"

Literary Interpretation & Contextual Significance: The mother is compared to a late winter’s moon , which lacks brightness and strength, obscured by haze and mist, symbolizing the final stage of life. The poet experiences her "old familiar ache"—the primal childhood fear of separation from her mother. Despite this overwhelming grief, she conceals her pain behind a repetitive, reassuring smile and optimistic parting words, attempting to comfort both her mother and herself.

Literary Devices & Poetic Syntax

The poem is structurally unique, written as a single continuous sentence punctuated only by commas. This stylistic choice represents a continuous, uninterrupted stream of consciousness, reflecting how thoughts and emotions flow seamlessly in the poet's mind during this emotional journey.

Literary Device Exact Textual Example Contextual Meaning & Subtext Pedagogical / Stylistic Purpose
Simile "face ashen like that of a corpse" Compares the mother's pale, lifeless face to a dead body. Emphasizes the stark reality of aging and the physical proximity to death.
Simile "pale as a late winter’s moon" Compares the mother's faded vitality to the dim, hazy winter moon. Symbolizes the loss of strength, radiance, and the approach of the end of life's cycle.
Personification "Young Trees sprinting" Attributes the human action of running to the trees outside the moving car. Creates a dynamic contrast with the static, dozing mother, symbolizing fast-moving time.
Metaphor "merry children spilling out of their homes" Compares the uncontrolled rush of children to liquid overflowing. Highlights spontaneous energy, vitality, and the vibrant beginning of life.
Repetition "smile and smile and smile......" The repeated use of the word "smile" at the end of the poem. Reflects the poet's desperate attempt to hide her intense grief, anxiety, and fear behind a brave facade.
Study Guide & Comprehension (Think It Out)
  1. What is the kind of pain and ache that the poet feels?

    The poet feels the sharp pain of witnessing her mother's rapid physical decline and aging. This triggers her childhood fear of losing her mother and being permanently separated from her by death.

  2. Why are the young trees described as 'sprinting'?

    The young trees are described as 'sprinting' because, from the perspective of the fast-moving car, they appear to be running past. Symbolically, this represents the rapid, unstoppable passage of time and the fleeting nature of youth.

  3. Why has the poet brought in the image of the merry children 'spilling out of their homes'?

    The image of the merry children spilling out of their homes is introduced to create a stark contrast with the lifelessness, silence, and decay inside the car. It juxtaposes the vibrant, energetic beginning of life with its frail, quiet end.

  4. Why has the mother been compared to the 'late winter’s moon'?

    Winter is the final season of the year, symbolizing old age and decay. A late winter's moon lacks brightness, warmth, and clarity due to mist and fog. Similarly, the mother has lost her youthful radiance, strength, and vitality, looking pale and wan.

  5. What do the parting words of the poet and her smile signify?

    The parting words "see you soon, Amma" and her persistent smile signify hope, reassurance, and a deliberate attempt to mask her deep-seated anxiety and grief. It is a brave front put up to comfort her fragile mother and to console her own aching heart.
