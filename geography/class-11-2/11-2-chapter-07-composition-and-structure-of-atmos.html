Class 11 (Part 2) — Chapter 7: Composition and Structure of Atmosphere

Composition of the Atmosphere

The atmosphere is a mixture of different gases, water vapour, and dust particles that envelopes the earth. It contains life-giving gases like oxygen and carbon dioxide. Notably, 99% of the total mass of the atmosphere is confined to a height of 32 km from the earth's surface.

Gases

The atmosphere is composed of various gases with changing proportions in higher layers:

  • Carbon dioxide: Meteorologically crucial as it is transparent to incoming solar radiation but opaque to outgoing terrestrial radiation. It is largely responsible for the green house effect.
  • Ozone: Found between 10 and 50 km above the earth's surface. It acts as a filter, absorbing harmful ultra-violet rays from the sun.
  • Oxygen becomes negligible at a height of 120 km, while carbon dioxide and water vapour are found only up to 90 km.

Water Vapour

A variable gas that decreases with altitude and from the equator towards the poles.

  • In warm, wet tropics, it may account for 4% of the air by volume, whereas in dry/cold deserts and polar regions, it is less than 1%.
  • It absorbs parts of insolation and preserves terrestrial radiation, acting like a blanket.

Dust Particles

Concentrated in the lower layers, originating from sea salts, fine soil, smoke-soot, ash, and pollen.

  • They act as hygroscopic nuclei around which water vapour condenses to produce clouds.
Structure of the Atmosphere

The atmosphere is divided into five distinct layers based on temperature conditions:

LayerAverage Height RangeKey Characteristics
Troposphere0 to 13 km (8 km at poles, 18 km at equator)Lowermost layer; contains dust and water vapour; all weather changes occur here. Temperature decreases with height.
Stratosphere13 to 50 kmContains the ozone layer which absorbs UV radiation. Temperature increases or remains constant.
Mesosphere50 to 80 kmTemperature decreases with height, reaching minus 100°C at 80 km.
Ionosphere80 to 400 kmContains electrically charged particles (ions); reflects radio waves back to Earth.
ExosphereAbove 400 kmHighest, extremely rarefied layer; gradually merges with outer space.

Troposphere and Tropopause

The troposphere is the most important layer for biological activity. The temperature decreases at a specific rate:

$$\text{Normal Lapse Rate} = \frac{1^\circ\text{C}}{165\text{m}\text{ of height}}$$

The boundary separating the troposphere and stratosphere is the tropopause, where air temperature is about minus 80°C over the equator and minus 45°C over the poles.

Other Atmospheric Boundaries

  • The stratopause lies at the top of the stratosphere.
  • The mesopause is the upper limit of the mesosphere at 80 km.
Elements of Weather and Climate

The main elements of the atmosphere that influence human life on earth include:

  • Temperature
  • Pressure
  • Winds
  • Humidity
  • Clouds
  • Precipitation
