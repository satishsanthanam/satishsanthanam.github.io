Class 12 (Part 1) — Chapter 6: Tertiary and Quaternary Activities

Introduction to Tertiary Activities

Tertiary activities are related to the service sector. Unlike primary and secondary activities, they do not directly involve the processing of physical raw materials or the production of tangible goods. Instead, they rely on specialized skills, experience, and knowledge of the workers.

Key Characteristics

  • Manpower is a critical component, as services are performed by skilled labor, professionally trained experts, and consultants.
  • In developed economies, the majority of workers are employed in the tertiary sector.
  • The output is measured indirectly in terms of wages and salaries.
Types of Tertiary Activities

Tertiary activities are broadly classified into trade, transport, communication, and services.

Trade and Commerce

Trade is the buying and selling of items produced elsewhere, specifically intended for profit. The towns and cities where these activities take place are called trading centres.

  • Rural Marketing Centres: Quasi-urban centres that serve nearby settlements with rudimentary services and local wholesale markets (mandis).
  • Periodic Markets: Weekly or bi-weekly markets in rural areas where there are no regular shops.
  • Urban Marketing Centres: Highly specialized centres offering ordinary as well as specialized goods and professional services.
  • Retail Trading: Direct sale of goods to consumers, occurring in fixed establishments or through non-store channels (e.g., street peddling, mail-order, internet).
  • Wholesale Trading: Bulk business conducted through intermediary merchants and supply houses.

Transport

Transport is a service or facility by which people, materials, and manufactured goods are physically carried from one location to another.

  • Distance metrics: km distance (actual route length), time distance (time taken), and cost distance (expense incurred).
  • Isochrone lines: Lines drawn on a map joining places equal in terms of the time taken to reach them.
  • Network: A system of nodes (meeting points of routes) and links (routes joining two nodes).
  • Factors affecting transport: Demand (influenced by population size) and Routes (influenced by physical landscape, climate, and economic links).

Communication

Communication services involve the transmission of words, messages, facts, and ideas.

  • Telecommunications: Rapid transmission of messages using modern technology, reducing time from weeks to minutes.
  • Mass Media: Radio, television, and newspapers that reach vast global audiences.

Services

Services operate at various levels, from low-order everyday services to high-order professional services.

  • Low-order services: Common and widespread services like grocery shopping, laundry, and hairdressing.
  • High-order services: Specialized services like those of physicians, lawyers, and accountants.
  • Personal services: Unorganized services to facilitate daily life, such as domestic help or Mumbai's famous dabbawala service.
Tourism and Medical Services

Tourism

Tourism is travel undertaken for recreation rather than business. It is the world's single largest tertiary activity in terms of jobs and revenue.

  • Factors attracting tourists: Climate (e.g., sunny Mediterranean beaches), Landscape (mountains, lakes), History and Art (monuments, heritage sites), and Culture and Economy (ethnic customs, affordable home-stays).

Medical Tourism

Medical tourism occurs when medical treatment is combined with international tourism. India, Thailand, Singapore, and Malaysia are leading destinations due to world-class hospitals and lower costs.

Quaternary and Quinary Activities

Quaternary Activities

The Quaternary sector is a knowledge-oriented segment of the service sector focusing on the collection, production, and dissemination of information, as well as research and development.

Quinary Activities

The Quinary sector involves the highest level of decision-makers and policy-makers, often referred to as gold collar professions.

  • Outsourcing: Contracting work to outside agencies to improve efficiency and reduce costs.
  • KPO (Knowledge Process Outsourcing): Information-driven outsourcing involving highly skilled workers (e.g., research, e-learning, intellectual property research).
The Digital Divide

The Digital Divide refers to the uneven distribution of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) opportunities and access across different countries and within regions of the same country.
