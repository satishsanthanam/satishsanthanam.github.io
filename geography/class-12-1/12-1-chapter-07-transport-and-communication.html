Geography: Class 12 (Part 1) — Chapter 7: Transport and Communication

Introduction to Transport and Communication

Transport is a service or facility for the carriage of persons and goods from one place to another using humans, animals, and different kinds of vehicles. Communication and transport establish links between producing centres and consuming centres, supporting mass production and exchange.

Modes of Transportation

The principal modes of world transportation are land, water, air, and pipelines. The significance of a mode depends on the type of goods, costs, and availability.

  • Land Transport: Includes roads, railways, and pipelines.
  • Water Transport: Divided into sea routes and inland waterways.
  • Air Transport: Fastest but most expensive mode.
  • Pipelines: Used for transporting liquids and gases like water, petroleum, and natural gas.

Pack Animals

Historically, humans and animals served as the primary carriers. Different animals are used as beasts of burden depending on the climate and terrain:

  • Horses: Used as draught animals in Western countries.
  • Dogs and Reindeer: Used in North America, Northern Europe, and Siberia to draw sledges over snow.
  • Mules: Preferred in mountainous regions.
  • Camels: Used for caravan movement in deserts.
  • Bullocks: Used for pulling carts in India.
Road Transport and Highways

Road transport is the most economical mode for short distances, offering door-to-door service. However, unmetalled roads are not effective during rainy seasons, and traffic congestion remains a major issue in urban areas.

Highways

Highways are metalled roads connecting distant places, designed for unobstructed vehicular movement. They are typically 80 m wide with separate traffic lanes, bridges, and flyovers.

  • North America: High highway density of about $0.65\text{ km per sq km}$. Every place is within 20 km of a highway.
  • Trans-Canadian Highway: Links Vancouver (west coast) to St. John's City (east coast).
  • Alaskan Highway: Links Edmonton (Canada) to Anchorage (Alaska).
  • Pan-American Highway: Connects South America, Central America, and USA-Canada.
  • Golden Quadrilateral: Connects India's major metropolitan cities (New Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, and Kolkata).

Border Roads

Border Roads are roads laid along international boundaries. They play an important role in integrating remote border areas with major cities and providing defense logistics.

Railways

Railways are highly suited for moving large volumes of bulky materials and passengers over long distances. There are about 13 lakh km of railways open for traffic globally.

Trans-Continental Railways

These railways run across continents to link their two ends, constructed for economic and political reasons:

  • Trans-Siberian Railway: The longest trans-continental railway in the world (9,332 km), running from St. Petersburg in the west to Vladivostok in the east.
  • Trans-Canadian Railways: A 7,050 km line linking Halifax in the east to Vancouver in the west.
  • The Union and Pacific Railway: Connects New York on the Atlantic coast to San Francisco on the Pacific coast.
  • The Australian Trans-Continental Railway: Runs west-east across the southern part of Australia from Perth to Sydney.
  • The Orient Express: Runs from Paris to Istanbul, reducing travel time significantly compared to sea routes.
Water Transport

Water transport is the cheapest mode of transport because it requires no route construction and experiences minimal friction compared to land transport.

Important Sea Routes

  • The Northern Atlantic Sea Route: Links North-eastern U.S.A. and North-western Europe. It is the busiest route in the world, carrying one-fourth of the world's foreign trade, and is known as the Big Trunk Route.
  • The Mediterranean-Indian Ocean Sea Route: Passes through the heart of the Old World, serving more countries and people than any other route.
  • The Cape of Good Hope Sea Route: Connects Western Europe with West Africa, South Africa, East Africa, and Australia.

Inland Waterways

The development of inland waterways depends on the navigability, width, depth, and water flow of rivers and canals:

  • The Rhine Waterways: Flows through Germany and the Netherlands, connecting the industrial Ruhr region to the North Atlantic Sea Route. It is the world's most heavily used waterway.
  • The Danube Waterway: Serves Eastern Europe, rising in the Black Forest and flowing eastwards.
  • The Volga Waterway: Russia's most important navigable waterway, draining into the Caspian Sea.
  • The Great Lakes - St. Lawrence Seaway: Connects the Great Lakes of North America to the Atlantic Ocean, forming a unique commercial waterway.
Air Transport and Pipelines

Air Transport

Air Transport is the fastest and most expensive mode of transportation. It has revolutionized global connectivity, making no place in the world more than 35 hours away. The U.S.A. alone accounts for 60 percent of the world's airways.

Pipelines

Pipelines are used extensively to transport liquids and gases (water, petroleum, natural gas, and liquefied coal) for an uninterrupted flow.

  • Big Inch: A famous pipeline in the U.S.A. carrying petroleum from the Gulf of Mexico to the North-eastern States.
  • Iran-India Pipeline: A proposed international oil and natural gas pipeline via Pakistan, projected to be the longest in the world.
Communications

Long-distance communication has evolved from the telegraph and telephone to advanced digital networks.

Satellite Communication

Satellite Communication emerged in the 1970s, drastically reducing the unit cost and time of communication over long distances. India has made significant strides with satellites like Aryabhatt, Bhaskar-I, Rohini, and the INSAT series.

Cyber Space - Internet

Cyber Space is the electronic digital world for communicating or accessing information over computer networks without physical movement. The rapid expansion of the internet has transformed the world into a global village, enabling e-mail, e-commerce, e-learning, and e-governance.
