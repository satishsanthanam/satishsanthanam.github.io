Geography: Class 12 (Part 2) — Chapter 3: Land Resources and Agriculture

Land Use Categories and Records

Land-use records in India are maintained by the Land Revenue Department. This is distinct from the geographical area measured by the Survey of India. The land revenue records measure the reporting area, which fluctuates slightly based on administrative estimates, whereas the geographical area remains fixed.

The Nine Land-Use Categories

The Land Revenue Records classify land into nine distinct categories:

  • Forests: Area demarcated by the government for forest growth, which differs from actual forest cover.
  • Barren and Wastelands: Land that cannot be brought under cultivation with current technology (e.g., deserts, hilly terrains).
  • Land put to Non-agricultural Uses: Land occupied by settlements, infrastructure, industries, and shops.
  • Area under Permanent Pastures and Grazing Lands: Mostly owned by the village Panchayat or government, classified under Common Property Resources.
  • Area under Miscellaneous Tree Crops and Groves: Land under orchards and fruit trees, not included in the net sown area.
  • Culturable Wasteland: Land left uncultivated for more than five years, which can be reclaimed for farming.
  • Current Fallow: Land left uncultivated for one or less than one agricultural year to recoup fertility naturally.
  • Fallow other than Current Fallow: Cultivable land left uncultivated for more than one year but less than five years.
  • Net Area Sown: The physical extent of land on which crops are sown and harvested.
Land-use Changes in India

Land-use patterns are highly influenced by economic development. Three major economic changes affect land-use:

  1. Size of the Economy: Growth in population and income increases the pressure on land.
  2. Composition of the Economy: Growth in secondary and tertiary sectors leads to a gradual shift of agricultural land to non-agricultural uses.
  3. Pressure on Agricultural Land: Even as agriculture's share in GDP declines, the absolute population dependent on it continues to grow, maintaining high pressure on land resources.

Common Property Resources (CPRs)

Land ownership is broadly divided into private land and Common Property Resources (CPRs). CPRs are owned by the state or community and are accessible to all members. They are crucial for providing fodder, fuel, and minor forest products, particularly supporting the livelihoods of landless, marginal farmers, and rural women.

Agricultural Land Use and Cropping Intensity

Agriculture is a land-based activity where land quality and ownership directly impact productivity and social status. The total stock of agricultural land resources is calculated as:

$$\text{Total Cultivable Land} = \text{Net Sown Area} + \text{All Fallow Lands} + \text{Culturable Wasteland}$$

To maximize output from limited land resources, farmers increase cropping intensity, which is calculated using the following formula:

$$\text{Cropping Intensity (CI)} = \frac{\text{Gross Cropped Area (GCA)}}{\text{Net Sown Area (NSA)}} \times 100$$

Types of Farming

  • Irrigated Farming: Divided into protective irrigation (to defend crops from soil moisture deficiency) and productive irrigation (to provide high moisture for high yields).
  • Rainfed Farming: Classified into dryland farming (regions with rainfall < 75 cm, growing drought-resistant crops like bajra and ragi) and wetland farming (regions with excess rainfall, growing water-intensive crops like rice and sugarcane).
Major Crops of India

Foodgrains

Foodgrains occupy about two-thirds of the total cropped area in India. They are classified into:

  • Cereals: Divided into fine grains (Rice, Wheat) and coarse grains (Jowar, Bajra, Maize). Rice is a tropical humid crop, while wheat is primarily a temperate rabi crop.
  • Pulses: Rich sources of proteins and legume crops that aid in nitrogen fixation (e.g., Gram, Tur/Arhar).

Oilseeds and Fibre Crops

  • Oilseeds: Grown for extracting edible oils. Main crops include Groundnut, Rapeseed, Mustard, Soyabean, and Sunflower.
  • Fibre Crops: Provide raw materials for textiles and packaging. Cotton is a kharif crop grown in semi-arid areas, while Jute is a cash crop concentrated in West Bengal and adjoining eastern states.

Beverage and Cash Crops

  • Sugarcane: A tropical, irrigated crop concentrated in the Indo-Gangetic plain and western India.
  • Tea: A plantation crop grown on undulating topography with well-drained soils in humid climates (e.g., Assam, West Bengal, Nilgiri hills).
  • Coffee: Grown in the highlands of the Western Ghats (Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu), with the high-quality Arabica variety being highly demanded.
Agricultural Development and Challenges

Historical Development

Before independence, Indian agriculture was largely subsistence-based. Post-independence, strategies shifted from expanding cultivated land to intensifying production. The mid-1960s witnessed the introduction of the Green Revolution, utilizing High Yielding Varieties (HYVs) of seeds, chemical fertilizers, and irrigation, which made India self-reliant in foodgrains.

Major Problems of Indian Agriculture

  • Dependence on Erratic Monsoon: Only about one-third of the cultivated area is irrigated, leaving the rest vulnerable to rainfall fluctuations.
  • Low Productivity: Crop yields per hectare are significantly lower than international standards.
  • Small Farm Size and Fragmentation: Shrinking landholding sizes make modern farming uneconomic.
  • Degradation of Cultivable Land: Faulty irrigation practices lead to alkalisation, salinisation, and waterlogging, depleting soil fertility.
