Introduction to Health and its Dimensions

Health has evolved from being understood merely as the absence of disease to a comprehensive state of complete well-being. In 1948, the World Health Organization (WHO) defined health as: "a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity." Modern health is multidimensional and dynamic, representing the ability to adapt and manage life's challenges.

The Six Dimensions of Health

  • Physical Dimension: The ability of the human body to function properly. It involves regular exercise, healthy habits, a balanced diet, bone health, and maintaining an optimal Body Mass Index (BMI).
    $$\text{BMI} = \frac{\text{Weight in kg}}{(\text{Height in meters})^2}$$
  • Mental Dimension: Refers to cognitive aspects of health including thinking, reasoning, remembering, imagining, and learning. It is the capacity to process information, master new skills, and maintain self-esteem.
  • Social Dimension: The ability to interact effectively with others in a socio-cultural environment, maintain healthy relationships, and communicate effectively.
  • Emotional Dimension: The ability to cope, adjust, and adapt to the environment, manage stress, and maintain a positive mindset.
  • Spiritual Dimension: Finding meaning, value, belief, and a sense of overall purpose in life, which strongly influences mental and emotional resilience.
  • Environmental Dimension: Living in harmony with nature, protecting the environment, and understanding how external surroundings (habitat, pollution, occupation) affect personal wellness.
Health Needs of Children, Adolescents, and Differently Abled

Different demographic groups require targeted health interventions based on their developmental stages and unique challenges.

Health Needs of Children

Childhood lays the foundation for a healthy life. Key needs include:

  • Adequate food, nutrition, and immunity.
  • Protection against vaccine-preventable diseases (e.g., measles).
  • Prevention and immediate treatment of common childhood illnesses like diarrhoea, pneumonia, and fevers.

Health Needs of Adolescents

Adolescence (ages 10–19) is a critical transition period marked by rapid physical, physiological, and emotional changes.

  • Key Challenges: Nutritional disorders (malnutrition and obesity), substance abuse, stress, mental disorders, injuries, and internet/social media addiction.
  • Support Systems: Access to counseling, healthcare advice, and peer-led support systems.

Health Needs of the Differently Abled

According to the WHO, disability covers impairments, activity limitations, and participation restrictions. Key frameworks include:

  • National Policy for Persons with Disabilities (2006): Promotes equal opportunities, protection of rights, and full participation in society.
  • 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development: Emphasizes inclusive development and welfare schemes to support physical, social, and emotional well-being.

Addressing Health Needs: Roles of Stakeholders

Improving community and individual health requires active participation from multiple institutions:

Role of Schools and Teachers

  • Schools promote healthy behaviors, physical education, and structured sports.
  • Teachers identify diverse student needs, offer counseling, and implement health promotion programs.

Role of Adolescent-friendly Health Services

Providing accessible, confidential, and non-judgmental healthcare services specifically tailored to adolescent concerns.

Role of Peer Educators / Facilitators

Trained senior students act as peer facilitators to help adolescents overcome shyness and discuss sensitive health issues.

International Efforts: MDGs and SDGs

Global frameworks established to address health and development challenges:

  • Millennium Development Goals (MDGs): Eight target-based, time-bound goals, three of which were directly related to health.
  • Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs): Also known as 'Transforming our world: the 2030 Agenda'. Consists of 17 Global Goals with 169 targets.
  • SDG 3: Focuses on "ensuring healthy lives and promoting well-being for all at all ages" through universal health coverage.
WHO Physical Activity Recommendations

Physical activity is a key determinant of energy balance, weight control, and prevention of non-communicable diseases.

Age GroupRecommended Physical Activity Level
Children & Adolescents (5–17 years)At least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous intensity physical activity daily. Should include bone and muscle-strengthening activities at least thrice a week.
Adults (18–64 years)At least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity physical activity throughout the week, along with muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days.
