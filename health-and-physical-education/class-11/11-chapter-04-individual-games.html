Athletics

Athletics consists of track and field events, representing the oldest and most natural forms of human physical expression: running, jumping, and throwing.

History of Athletics

The first recorded organized track and field events occurred at the Ancient Olympic Games in 776 B.C. in Olympia, Greece, featuring a single event called the stadion footrace. Later, the pentathlon was introduced, consisting of five events: discus throw, long jump, javelin throw, stadion footrace, and wrestling.

In modern history, the Amateur Athletic Association (AAA) was established in England in 1880, and the International Amateur Athletic Federation (IAAF) was founded in 1912. In India, the Amateur Athletics Federation of India (AAFI) was established in 1946 to manage and promote the sport nationally.

Classification of Athletics Events

Athletics events are classified into four primary categories:

  • Track Events: All running events.
  • Field Events: Jumping and throwing events.
  • Combined Events: Multi-discipline events such as the Decathlon (men, 10 events over 2 days) and Heptathlon (women, 7 events over 2 days).
  • Events Conducted Outside the Stadium: Road races and walks, such as the Marathon (42.195 km) and 20 km/50 km race walks.

Rules, Techniques, and Specifications

Starting the Race: Races are started with a starting gun. The two main types of starts are:

  • Crouch Start: Essential for sprint races. Divided into Bunch/Bullet Start, Medium Start, and Elongated Start.
  • Standing Start: Used for middle and long-distance races.

Finishing Techniques: The three primary methods to finish a race are Lunging forward, Shoulder shrug, and Run through.

Relay Races: Team events consisting of four runners. The baton must be exchanged within a designated 20 m exchange zone. Methods of baton exchange include Downward pass, Upward pass, and Push pass.

National Sports Awards

The Government of India honors outstanding coaches and sportspersons with prestigious national awards:

  • Dronacharya Award: Instituted in 1985 to recognize outstanding coaches. It carries a cash prize of ₹5 lakhs, a bronze statue of Guru Dronacharya, a scroll, and ceremonial attire.
  • Arjuna Award: Instituted in 1961 to recognize outstanding sports achievements at the international level. It carries a cash prize of ₹5 lakhs, a bronze statuette of Arjuna, and a scroll.
  • Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna Award: India's highest sporting honor, instituted in 1991–92. It carries a medal, a scroll of honor, and a cash prize of ₹7.5 lakhs. The first recipient was Chess Grandmaster Viswanathan Anand.
  • Dhyan Chand Award: Awarded for lifetime contribution and achievement in sports.
Badminton

Badminton is a fast-paced racket sport played indoors. It was historically known as Poona or Poonah in India during the 1860s.

Court Measurements and Equipment
  • Court Dimensions: Rectangular, 13.40 m × 6.10 m (for doubles) and 13.40 m × 5.18 m (for singles).
  • Net Height: 1.55 m at the posts and 1.524 m at the center.
  • Shuttlecock: Must have 16 feathers fixed in the base, weighing between 4.74 g and 5.50 g.

Scoring System and Strokes

A match consists of the best of three games of 21 points. Points are scored on every serve (rally scoring). If the score reaches 20-all, the side gaining a 2-point lead first wins. If it reaches 29-all, the side scoring the 30th point wins.

Fundamental Strokes:

  • Services: High Serve, Backhand Low Serve, Backhand Flick Serve, Forehand Low Serve, Forehand Flick Serve.
  • Net/Forecourt Strokes: Net Shot, Net Lift, Net Kill.
  • Midcourt Strokes: Block, Drive.
  • Rear Court Strokes: Clear, Smash, Drop Shot.
Gymnastics

Gymnastics originated in ancient Greece and is regulated globally by the Federation of International Gymnastics (FIG), founded in 1881. The Gymnastics Federation of India (GFI) was formed in 1951.

Apparatus Specifications

Gymnastics routines are performed on specific apparatuses categorized by gender:

  • Women's Apparatus: Vaulting Table, Balancing Beam, Floor Exercise, Uneven Bars.
  • Men's Apparatus: Vaulting Table, Horizontal Bar, Floor Exercise, Parallel Bars, Roman Rings, Pommel Horse.

Judo

Judo is a modern martial art and self-defense system created in Japan by Jigoro Kano in 1882. The Judo Federation of India (JFI) was established in 1965.

Basic Rules and Scoring

An international match lasts four minutes. The primary objective is to score an Ippon (immediate victory), which can be achieved by throwing an opponent flat on their back, or by holding them in an armlock/stranglehold or pinning them for 20 seconds. The next best score is Waza-ari.

Penalties are awarded as Shido (minor infractions). Accumulating three Shidos results in a Hansoku-make (disqualification and loss of match).

Contest Area and Uniform

The contest area is covered with Tatamis (pressed foam mats) measuring 14 m × 14 m or 16 m × 16 m, with a central combat zone of 9 m × 9 m or 10 m × 10 m. Players wear a traditional uniform called a Judogi secured by an Obi (belt) indicating their rank.

Swimming

Swimming emerged as a competitive sport in England in the early 1800s. The world governing body, FINA, was formed in 1908, and the Swimming Federation of India (SFI) was established in 1948.

Pool Dimensions
  • Length: 50 m
  • Width: 25 m
  • Depth: Minimum 2.0 m (recommended 3.0 m)
  • Number of Lanes: 10 lanes, each 2.5 m wide
  • Water Temperature: 25°C to 28°C

Strokes and Techniques

Competitive swimming features four primary strokes:

  1. Front Crawl (Freestyle): The fastest stroke, utilizing alternating arm pulls and a continuous flutter kick.
  2. Back Crawl (Backstroke): Swum on the back, utilizing alternating arm actions and a flutter kick.
  3. Breaststroke: A highly technical stroke requiring simultaneous arm recovery and a whip kick.
  4. Butterfly: A powerful stroke utilizing a simultaneous over-water arm recovery and a dolphin kick.
Table Tennis

Table Tennis (historically known as Ping-Pong) is a popular indoor racket sport. The International Table Tennis Federation (ITTF) was founded in 1926.

Equipment Specifications
  • Table Dimensions: Rectangular, 2.74 m long, 1.525 m wide, and 76 cm above the floor.
  • Net Height: 15.25 cm above the playing surface.
  • Ball: Spherical, 40 mm diameter, weighing 2.7 g, made of celluloid or similar plastic.
  • Racket Grips: The two primary grips are the Penhold grip and the Shakehand grip.

Scoring and Rules

A game is won by the player or pair first scoring 11 points. If both sides reach 10 points, the game is won by the first side to gain a 2-point lead. Service alternates every 2 points. The expedite system is introduced if a game is not completed after 10 minutes of play (unless 18 or more points have been scored).

Wrestling

Wrestling is one of the oldest combat sports. Globally, it is governed by United World Wrestling (UWW), and nationally by the Wrestling Federation of India (WFI).

Types of Wrestling
  • Freestyle: Allows holds on any part of the opponent's body, including the legs.
  • Greco-Roman: Strictly forbids holds below the waist; active leg-tripping or holding the legs is prohibited.

Wrestling Arena and Scoring

The wrestling mat is a 12 m × 12 m square containing:

  • A Central Wrestling Area of 7 m diameter.
  • A 1 m wide orange Passivity Zone.
  • A 1.5 m wide outer Protection Area.

Points Classification:

  • Takedown: Up to 2 points.
  • Escape: 1 point.
  • Reversal: 2 points.
  • Near Fall: 2 points (if the fall lasts 2 seconds) or 3 points (if it lasts 5 seconds).
