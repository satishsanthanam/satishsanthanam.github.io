Safety Measures in Playgrounds, Gymnasium, and Swimming Pool

Sports Grounds and Fields

Proper preparation and maintenance of sports grounds and fields are critical to preventing accidental injuries.

  • Sports Ground: Must be free from hazards like high tension electric lines, barbed wires, wild grass, and sharp stones. Hard surfaces for basketball, badminton, and tennis must be properly constructed. Fencing and spectator seating should be at least 6 meters away from the end lines.
  • Field: Separate sitting areas for extra players and spectators must be provided. The playing area must be clean, even, and free of broken glass or sharp stones.

Sports Instruments and Uniforms

Modern sports equipment and uniforms must prioritize the safety of the sportsperson.

  • Sports Instruments: Manufacturers must integrate safety concepts. Players should never use damaged equipment.
  • Sports Uniforms: Poor fabric and unsuitable shoes can cause pain and injury. Proper shoes provide comfortable feeling, proper ankle support, proper balance, and prevent leg injuries. T-shirts should be light-colored and made of sweat-wicking fabric to prevent skin allergies.

Gymnasium Safety

Injuries in the gymnasium often occur due to unsafe practices or poor maintenance. Key risk factors include:

  • Unsafe methods of keeping equipment and old/damaged equipment.
  • Lack of proper exercise space and warm-up areas.
  • Unsafe electrical fittings, slippery or uncleaned surfaces, and poor lighting.
  • Lack of knowledge regarding weightlifting safety (e.g., not using spotters/helpers).

Swimming Pool Safety

Operating a swimming pool requires strict adherence to safety and hygiene protocols:

  • Separate pools for beginners and advanced swimmers.
  • Trained lifeguards and instructors equipped with life-saving devices (ropes, life jackets, etc.).
  • Mandatory use of proper swimming uniforms (costume, cap, eye protectors).
  • Continuous operation of water treatment plants and proper chlorination to remove algae.
  • Regular cleaning of the pool surface and installation of safety stairs, hangers, and non-slip matting in washrooms.
Safety Measures to Prevent Injuries

Why Sports Injuries Occur

Injuries are often caused by a careless attitude towards fitness and training. Key factors include:

  • Poor equipment, surface, or flooring.
  • Lack of knowledge of game rules, technical skills, or tactical execution.
  • Inadequate physical fitness and conditioning.
  • Failure to observe safety rules in high-difficulty sports like gymnastics, diving, judo, and wrestling.

Prevention Strategies

Preventive measures can be categorized into facility management, competition rules, and technical execution:

  • Prevention in Competition: Proper warming up to enhance reflex actions, using appropriate safety gears, adhering to rules, and pre-game evaluation of the arena by officials.
  • Technical Measures: Enhancing physical fitness to avoid fatigue, practicing high-difficulty movements before active participation, and receiving regular safety guidance from coaches.
Dealing with Alcohol, Substance Abuse, and Common Medicines

Tobacco and Substance Abuse

Adolescence is a highly vulnerable period where peer pressure, curiosity, or rebellion can lead to substance abuse.

  • Tobacco: Used in smoking (cigarettes, beedi, hookah) and smokeless forms (pan masala, gutkha, khaini). It contains highly addictive nicotine, which raises blood pressure.
  • Cigarette Smoke Constituents: Contains over 4000 chemicals, including highly toxic and carcinogenic substances.

Safe Use of Common Medicines

Self-medication and over-the-counter abuse for weight loss, weight gain, or cosmetic enhancement pose severe health risks.

  • Medicines must only be consumed under a doctor's prescription and dosage guidelines.
  • Medicines should be stored in cool, dry places and taken with water rather than tea, milk, or juice.
Doping in Sports

Concept and Classification of Doping

Doping is the use of banned performance-enhancing substances or methods to gain an unfair advantage in sports.

  • Defined by the International Olympic Committee (IOC) as any method or substance that might harm the athlete in a quest to gain an unfair advantage.
  • Regulated globally by the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA).

Classifications of Doping:

  1. Dietary Supplements: Highly refined products with no positive nutritional value.
  2. Injectable Supplements: Hormones (like testosterone) and drugs.

Basic Doping Drugs and Methods

  • Narcotics: Used to reduce, eliminate, or hide pain (e.g., morphine, methadone). Side effects include decreased heart rate, nausea, and vomiting.
  • Steroids: Anabolic-androgenic steroids related to testosterone. Used to build muscle mass. Side effects include heart attacks, strokes, liver tumors, kidney failure, and psychiatric issues.
  • Gene Doping: Injection of genes to prevent muscle wasting or enhance growth. Extremely dangerous, leading to abnormal growth and cancer risks.
  • Blood Doping: Increasing red blood cell mass to deliver more oxygen to muscles, enhancing endurance. Misuse of recombinant human EPO (Erythropoietin) thickens blood, increasing risks of heart disease, stroke, and pulmonary embolism.

Doping Control and Testing

Testing is conducted to maintain the integrity of sports and protect athlete health.

  • In-Competition Testing: Conducted at the competition venue.
  • Out-of-Competition (OOC) Testing: Conducted with no advance notice at any location and time.
