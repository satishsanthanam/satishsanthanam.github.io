History: Class 12 (Book 1) — Chapter 1: Bricks, Beads and Bones

The Harappan Civilisation

Introduction and Terminologies

The Harappan seal is arguably the most distinct artefact of the Indus Valley Civilisation. Sculpted out of a soft stone called steatite, these seals contain animal representations and symbolic scripts that remain undeciphered.

Time Periods and Scope Overview
  • The Core Nomenclature: Named after Harappa, the standard contextual type-site where its components were first unearthed.
  • Formative Stage (Early Harappan): Flourished smoothly from roughly 6000 BCE to 2600 BCE.
  • Urban Stage (Mature Harappan): The peak prosperous configuration, mapping across 2600 BCE to 1900 BCE.
  • Decadent Stage (Late Harappan): Marked by systemic regional dispersion between 1300 BCE and 1900 BCE.
  • Acronym Standardisation: BP denotes Before Present; BCE means Before Common Era; CE maps Common Era; c. references circa (approximate).
1. Beginnings and Settlements

Distinct archaeological entities featuring unique ceramic styles, metal crafts, and pastoral setups preceded the Mature urban phase.

Spatial Distribution Matrix
  • Over 2000 contextual sites have been documented across the Indian subcontinent, sitting predominantly between the Indus and the dry Saraswati basins.
  • Nearly two-thirds of discovered nodes sit along the Saraswati channels, confirming massive resource dependence on this waterway network.
  • The 5 premier urban megacities are catalogued as: Rakhigarhi, Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Dholavira, and Ganweriwala.
2. Subsistence Strategies and Agrarian Systems
Dietary Patterns and Archaeo-Botanical Profiles
  • Grain Flora Assemblies: Wheat, barley, sesame, chickpea, and lentils are regular; millets are mapped predominantly across coastal Gujarat, while rice grains are rare.
  • Faunal Domesticates: Skeletal fragments confirm domestication lines for cattle, sheep, goats, buffalo, and pigs, analyzed by archaeo-zoologists.
Agricultural Engineering and Irrigation Channels
  • Terracotta plough miniatures have been systematically salvaged from Cholistan and Banawali (Haryana).
  • A dual-axis ploughed field profile has been unearthed at Kalibangan (Rajasthan), containing right-angle intersecting furrows indicating two concurrent crops.
  • Canal irrigation traces are explicit at Shortughai (Afghanistan). In southern deserts, deep stone masonry check-dams and reservoirs were built at Dholavira to store runoff.

Saddle Querns Find: "Saddle querns are found in considerable numbers... made of hard, gritty igneous rock or sandstone... Two types are found: those on which a smaller stone was rolled to and fro, and others used as a pounder..." — Ernest Mackay, 1937

Analytical Historical Context & Interpretation: Proves that grain processing required highly specialized abrasive tools. The massive presence of these stone devices indicates centralized, domestic scale food preparation throughout urban sectors.

3. Mohenjodaro: Architectural Spatial Planning

Mohenjodaro exhibits highly standardized layouts, divided cleanly into two walled settlement structures.

The Citadel versus Lower Town Matrix
  • The Citadel: Small footprint, built on elevated mud platforms, walled completely to protect ritual administrative spaces.
  • The Lower Town: Expansive layout, acting as residential spaces. Structures sat on platforms that defined strict layout plots.
  • Brick Standardisation: Uniform 4:2:1 mathematical proportions ($Length:Breadth:Height$) apply across all brick classes, whether sun-dried or fire-baked.
  • The Looting of Harappa: Harappa was heavily damaged by 19th-century railway robbers. Alexander Cunningham noted in 1875 that brick thievery stripped enough materials to pave 100 miles of track line between Lahore and Multan.
Drainage Networks and Domestic Courtyards
  • Grid Network: Intersecting grid paths running at right angles required streets and central waste lines to be laid out first, followed by residential construction.
  • Private Structural Layouts: Ground walls had no windows, preventing outside lines of sight directly into inner courtyards.
  • Sanitation Access: Homes included paved brick floors with waste lines running through outer walls into street canals; Mohenjodaro featured an estimated 700 masonry wells.
  • Public Administrative Compounds: The Citadel preserves the Warehouse with deep brick foundations and the Great Bath. The Great Bath features a watertight lining made of edge-laid bricks and gypsum mortar.
4. Tracking Social Stratification and Luxuries
Mortuary Archaeology and Material Classification
  • Grave Offerings: Pit burials include pottery and personal gear (jasper beads, shell rings); however, Harappans generally avoided burying bulk luxury wealth with the dead.
  • Utilitarian Class: Everyday functional objects produced from easily accessible regional supplies (querns, pottery, needles, sandstone rubbers).
  • Luxury Class: Scarce commodities involving sophisticated manufacturing pipelines or non-local minerals, like miniature faience cosmetic containers.
5. Craft Production Systems and Bead Technology

Chanhudaro acted as a specialized industrial settlement of under 7 hectares, dedicated to multi-craft bead making, shell-cutting, and seal workshops.

Mineral Processing Protocols
  • Material Matrix: Carnelian, jasper, crystal, quartz, steatite, alongside copper, bronze, and gold alloys.
  • Pyrotechnic Color Engineering: Yellowish raw pebbles were fired to change their color into the deep red of carnelian beads.
  • Coastal Manufacturing Nodes: Nageshwar and Balakot served as dedicated maritime centers for processing marine shells into bangles, ladles, and decorative inlay work.
6. Procurement Strategies and Distance Commerce
Raw Material Logistics Network
  • Shortughai (Afghanistan): Strategically situated near deep mountain lines to extract premium blue lapis lazuli.
  • Lothal (Gujarat): Acted as a major trading port and workshop hub for carnelian extraction.
  • Khetri Zone (Rajasthan): Extracted regional copper inputs, showing interactions with the local hunter-gatherer Ganeshwar-Jodhpura culture.
  • International Maritime Trading: Cuneiform tablets from Mesopotamia reference interactions with a seafaring plain called Meluhha (the Indus region), alongside intermediate ports like Dilmun (Bahrain) and Magan (Oman).
7. Seals, Script, Metrics, and Centralized Authority
Administrative Controls and Metrology
  • Secured Logistics: Wet clay sealings pressed over cord knots verified shipment authenticity; uncompromised impressions guaranteed that goods had not been tampered with.
  • Metrology Metrics: Regulated by cubical, unmarked chert weight systems. Lower measures followed strict binary steps (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 up to 12,800), while higher weights integrated standard decimal tiers.
  • The Enigmatic Script: Written from right to left, utilizing between 375 and 400 unique symbolic characters without an alphabetical key.
  • Political Theories: Highly planned towns and uniform brick sizing suggest complex decision-making. Interpretations range from a completely egalitarian society to a single state layout managed by a centralized priest-king.
8. Systemic De-Urbanisation and the Late Phase

By c. 1800 BCE, primary mature urban cities in Cholistan were abandoned, accompanied by migrations into Gujarat and western Uttar Pradesh.

The Collapse Catalyst Debate
  • Environmental indicators suggest a combination of climate desertification, deforestation, river drying, and soil exhaustion.
  • Late Harappan layers are marked by the complete collapse of standardized weight controls, long-distance trade, writing blocks, and civic hygiene.

The Aryan Invasion Myth: Wheeler hypothesized that skeletal clusters in Mohenjodaro's "Deadman Lane" verified an attack by Vedic tribes. However, George Dales (1960s) disproved this, demonstrating that the skeletons belonged to distinct chronological layers with no military damage, confirming the "massacre" was mythical.

Analytical Historical Context & Interpretation: Replaces catastrophic warfare theories with evidence of gradual environmental decline, showing that urban centers dissolved due to resource strain rather than external invasions.

Recent Archaeogenetic Discoveries (Rakhigarhi)

DNA sequencing of skeletal material excavated from Rakhigarhi (Haryana) offers new data on population history:

  • Establishes that the Harappan citizens represent an indigenous lineage with genetic tracks extending backward to c. 10,000 BCE.
  • Rules out large-scale genetic replacement by western nomadic step migrations, confirming over 5000 years of biological continuity within the region.
9. Historiography: Discovery and Epistemology
Evolution of Excavation Methods
  • Cunningham's Blindspot: Alexander Cunningham missed Harappa's antiquity because he mistakenly believed Indian urbanism began in the Ganga valley, trying to fit artefacts into early historical Chinese Buddhist itineraries.
  • John Marshall's Announcement (1924): Formally announced the discovery of a new civilization to the world. However, his method of digging along uniform horizontal trenches mixed up distinct stratigraphic layers, destroying valuable data.
  • R.E.M. Wheeler's Stratigraphy (1944): Fixed Marshall's error by introducing stratigraphic field archaeology, aligning excavations with natural earth layers.
  • Classification Constraints: Since script markers are missing, interpretations remain speculative. Heavily ornamented terracotta figures are labeled mother goddesses, yogic seals are termed proto-Shiva, and conical stones are classified as lingas based on modern cultural analogies.
