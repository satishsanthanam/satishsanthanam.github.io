History: Class 12 (Book 1) — Chapter 4: Thinkers, Beliefs and Buildings: Cultural Developments (c. 600 BCE - 600 CE)

1. A Glimpse of Sanchi

The monumental stupa complex at Sanchi stands as one of the most magnificent and well-preserved archaeological sites in India, offering deep insights into early Buddhism.

Sanchi in the Nineteenth Century
  • Bhopal State : Sanchi was located in the princely state of Bhopal. Its preservation was heavily supported by the ruling Nawabs.
  • Shahjehan Begum and Sultan Jehan Begum : Provided crucial funding for the preservation of the ancient site, the construction of a museum, and a guesthouse where scholars like John Marshall lived and worked.
  • European Interest: French and British antiquarians sought to carry away the eastern gateway to display in European museums. They were ultimately satisfied with precise plaster-cast copies, leaving the original in situ.

"The most wonderful ancient buildings in the state of Bhopal are at Sanchi Kanakhera... We inspected the stone sculptures and statues of the Buddha and an ancient gateway..."

Analytical Historical Context & Interpretation: This excerpt from the Taj-ul Iqbal Tarikh Bhopal highlights the early colonial fascination with Sanchi and the proactive role of the Bhopal state in safeguarding its heritage, preventing the wholesale looting suffered by other sites like Amaravati.

2. The Background: Sacrifices and Debates

The mid-first millennium BCE is widely regarded as a turning point in world history, marked by intense intellectual exploration and the emergence of influential thinkers globally.

The Sacrificial Tradition
  • Rigveda : Compiled between c. 1500 and 1000 BCE, consisting of hymns in praise of deities like Agni , Indra , and Soma .
  • Sacrificial Evolution: Early sacrifices were performed collectively. Later (c. 1000 BCE - 500 BCE onwards), domestic sacrifices were performed by heads of households, while elaborate rituals like rajasuya and ashvamedha were conducted by chiefs and kings with the help of Brahmana priests.

"Bring, O strong one, this sacrifice of ours to the gods, O wise one, as a liberal giver. Bestow on us, O priest, abundant food. Agni, obtain, by sacrificing, mighty wealth for us..."

Analytical Historical Context & Interpretation: This Rigvedic prayer to Agni demonstrates that early Vedic rituals were deeply tied to material desires, such as cattle, sons, health, and wealth, mediated through the sacred fire.

New Questions and Debates
  • Upanishads : Composed from c. sixth century BCE onwards, showing curiosity about the meaning of life, the possibility of life after death, and the nature of ultimate reality.
  • Kutagarashala : Literally, a hut with a pointed roof, where travelling philosophers and teachers gathered to debate their ideas.
  • Heterodox Challenge: Thinkers like Mahavira and Gautama Buddha questioned the authority of the Vedas and emphasized individual agency, suggesting that anyone could strive for liberation regardless of caste or gender.
3. Beyond Worldly Pleasures: The Message of Mahavira

Jainism introduced a deeply ethical and ascetic philosophy that left an enduring mark on Indian thought.

Core Philosophy of Jainism
  • Tirthankaras : Meaning "those who guide men and women across the river of existence." Mahavira was preceded by 23 other tirthankaras.
  • Animated World: The belief that the entire world is animated, and that even stones, rocks, and water possess life.
  • Ahimsa : Extreme non-injury to living beings, especially humans, animals, plants, and insects.
  • Karma and Asceticism: The cycle of birth and rebirth is shaped by karma. Escaping this cycle requires severe penance and asceticism, which can only be achieved through monastic life.
  • Five Monastic Vows: To abstain from killing, stealing, and lying; to observe celibacy; and to abstain from possessing property.
4. The Buddha and the Quest for Enlightenment

The teachings of Buddhism emerged from the personal spiritual quest of Siddhartha, who challenged contemporary religious orthodoxies.

Siddhartha's Renunciation
  • Early Life: Siddhartha was born into the Sakya clan and lived a sheltered life of luxury within his palace.
  • The Four Sights: On a journey outside the palace, he witnessed an old man, a sick man, a corpse, and a calm, homeless mendicant, which forced him to confront the inevitability of decay and suffering.
  • Enlightenment: Siddhartha abandoned his palace, explored extreme bodily mortification, and finally attained enlightenment through meditation under a Bodhi tree, becoming the Buddha.
5. The Teachings of the Buddha

The Buddha's teachings, preserved in the Sutta Pitaka, focused on practical ethics and the nature of existence.

Key Buddhist Doctrines
  • Anicca : The belief that the world is transient and constantly changing.
  • Anatta : The belief that the world is soulless, with nothing permanent or eternal.
  • Dukkha : The understanding that sorrow is intrinsic to human existence.
  • The Middle Path: The path of moderation between extreme self-indulgence and severe penance.
  • Nibbana : The extinguishing of the ego and desire, which ends the cycle of suffering.
6. Followers of the Buddha

The Buddha established a democratic and egalitarian community of disciples that rapidly expanded across social boundaries.

The Sangha and Social Equality
  • Sangha : An organization of monks ( bhikkhus ) who lived simply on alms and taught the dhamma .
  • Admission of Women: Mediated by Ananda , the Buddha's foster mother Mahapajapati Gotami became the first woman ordained as a bhikkhuni . Many women became theris (respected women who attained liberation).
  • Social Inclusivity: The Sangha welcomed kings, wealthy men, gahapatis, workers, slaves, and craftspeople, all of whom shed their previous social identities upon entry.
7. Stupas

Stupas evolved from simple burial mounds into elaborate architectural symbols of the Buddha and his teachings.

Sacred Geography and Architecture
  • Chaityas : Sacred places associated with special trees, rocks, or natural beauty.
  • Four Sacred Sites: Lumbini (birth), Bodh Gaya (enlightenment), Sarnath (first sermon), and Kusinagara (nibbana).
  • Relics and Stupas: Stupas were built over the physical remains or objects used by the Buddha. The Ashokavadana records that Emperor Ashoka distributed the Buddha's relics to major towns and ordered stupas to be built over them.

8. "Discovering" Stupas: The Fate of Amaravati and Sanchi

The contrasting fates of the stupas at Amaravati and Sanchi highlight the evolution of archaeological preservation practices in India.

The Destruction of Amaravati vs. the Survival of Sanchi
  • Amaravati's Plunder: Discovered in 1796, its stones were looted by a local raja. Later, British officials like Walter Elliot carried away its finest sculptures (the "Elliot marbles") to Madras, Calcutta, and London, leaving the site denuded.
  • Sanchi's Preservation: Discovered in 1818, Sanchi survived because of early advocacy for in situ preservation by archaeologists like H.H. Cole , who argued against looting original works of art.
9. Sculpture

Sculptures at Sanchi and other early Buddhist sites depict stories, symbols of worship, and popular non-Buddhist traditions.

Symbols and Motifs
  • Aniconic Representation: Early artists did not depict the Buddha in human form. Instead, they used symbols: the empty seat (meditation), the stupa (mahaparinibbana), the wheel (first sermon), and the tree (enlightenment).
  • Shalabhanjika : A popular motif of a woman swinging from a tree, whose touch caused it to flower, integrated as an auspicious symbol.
  • Gajalakshmi : A goddess of good fortune associated with elephants, identified by some as Maya, the mother of the Buddha.
10. New Religious Traditions

The turn of the common era witnessed major transformations in both Buddhism and Hinduism, marked by the rise of devotionalism.

Mahayana Buddhism and Puranic Hinduism
  • Mahayana Buddhism : The "Great Vehicle" introduced the concept of a saviour Buddha and compassionate Bodhisattas , alongside the worship of physical images.
  • Hinayana / Theravada : The older, conservative tradition emphasizing individual effort to achieve nibbana.
  • Vaishnavism and Shaivism : Devotional Hindu traditions centered on Vishnu (and his ten avatars ) and Shiva (represented by the linga ).
  • Temple Architecture: Early temples evolved from a simple square room ( garbhagriha ) to elaborate structures with tall spires ( shikhara ) and rock-cut cave temples like the Kailashnatha Temple at Ellora.
