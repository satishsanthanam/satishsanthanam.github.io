Introduction and Significance of Fashion Merchandising

The garment and textile industry in India has thrived for ages. The recent boom in fashion designing has created new prospects in garment and accessory design, satisfying both creative urges and materialistic needs.

Historical Evolution of Trading to Merchandising

  • Barter System: The origin of trading practices where products and crafts were directly exchanged.
  • What was available was saleable: Early industrial stage with simple distribution systems and no marketing complications.
  • Ready-to-Wear (1920): The birth of standardized, pre-manufactured garments which made fashion apparel the most important merchandise in department stores.
  • Fashion Merchandising: A specialization born out of the economic opportunities of fashion, encompassing 'planning, buying, and selling'.

Significance of the Field

  • Provides a comprehensive understanding of how the fashion business works, from raw material production to retail sales.
  • Integrates knowledge of textiles (fabrics and fibers) with business operations.
  • Equips professionals to analyze what, why, and when a style becomes fashion, determining its retail suitability and lifespan.
Basic Concepts and Fashion Terminology

To understand the fashion industry, one must master its foundational vocabulary and concepts.

Core Fashion Terms

  • Fashion: The style or styles most popular at a given time.
  • Style: Any particular look or characteristic in apparel or accessories. A style may come and go in fashion, but its specific identity remains.
  • Fads: Short-lived fashions that come and go in a single season; they lack the design strength to hold consumer attention long (e.g., hot pants, baggy pants, unmatched buttons).
  • Classic: Styles that never become completely obsolete, remaining accepted for extended periods. Characterized by simplicity of design (e.g., blazer jackets, polo shirts, Chanel suits).
Fashion Development and Historical Milestones

The history of fashion helps designers and merchandisers make informed decisions for present and future trends by reinterpreting past ideas.

France: The Centre of Fashion

  • Dominance began in the early 18th century.
  • Prior to the Industrial Revolution, only the wealthy could afford fashionable clothes.
  • King Louis XIV's court members became trendsetters, making Paris the fashion capital of Europe.
  • Couture: The art of dressmaking. A male designer is a couturier, and a female designer is a couturiere. All clothes were custom-made and hand-sewn to fit exact measurements.

The Industrial Revolution and Technological Advances

  • Marked the beginning of technological advances in textile and apparel production, enabling more fabrics to be produced in less time.
  • Inventions of the spinning jenny and power looms accelerated American and European textile industries.
  • Created a middle class with disposable income to spend on better clothing.
  • Sewing Machine (1859): Isaac Singer developed the foot treadle, freeing hands to guide fabric and democratizing fashion.
  • Denims (1849): Levi Strauss used fabric intended for tents to make durable pants with tool pockets for laborers. This apparel has remained virtually unchanged for nearly 150 years.
  • Separates (1880s): Women started wearing separate skirts and blouses, a major step toward ready-to-wear, allowing working-class women to mix and match wardrobes.
  • Prêt-à-porter: Ready-to-wear factory-made clothing sold in finished conditions and standardized sizes. Yves Saint Laurent (YSL) was the first French haute couturier to launch a full prêt-à-porter line in the 1960s.
Fashion Evolution: The Fashion Cycle

Fashion moves in a continuous cycle represented by a bell-shaped curve of time and sales volume.

Stage of CycleCharacteristics and Market Dynamics
1. Introduction of a StyleDesigners interpret research and creative ideas into apparel and offer new styles to the public. Created by changing elements like line, color, shape, or fabric.
2. Increase in PopularityAs the new fashion is purchased, worn, and seen by many, it rises in popularity and attracts media attention.
3. Peak of PopularityThe fashion is at its height of popularity. Manufacturers copy or produce adaptations at various price levels to meet high demand.
4. Decline in PopularityMass production leads to saturation. Fashion-conscious consumers tire of the style and seek new looks. Declining styles are moved to clearance racks.
5. Rejection or ObsolescenceThe final phase where consumers have completely turned to new looks, beginning a new cycle.
Fashion Merchandising: Roles and Levels

Fashion merchandising is the planning required to have the right merchandise, at the right time, at the right place, at the right price, and with the right sales promotion.

The Role of a Fashion Merchandiser

  • In Manufacturing: Provides input on fabric selection based on historical and socio-cultural understanding. Translates a designer's vision into a cost-effective, manufacturable product suitable for the target market.
  • In Buying: Analyzes trends and forecasts to order accurate quantities of fashion items for retail stores.
  • In Promoting: Uses visual merchandising, fashion shows, and targeted marketing to capture buyer attention.
  • In Selling: Recommends display strategies and coordinates with retail stores to present fashion items effectively.

Three Levels of Merchandising

  1. Retail Organisation Merchandising: Specialized management that moves fashion from designers' showrooms to retail sales floors. Ensures adequate inventory is sold at profitable, consumer-acceptable prices.
  2. Buying Agency Merchandising: Acts as a procurement office for buyers. Helps exporters save cost and time by identifying vendors, negotiating costs, checking in-process quality, and performing pre-shipment inspections.
  3. Export House Merchandising: Divided into buyer merchandisers (who act as a link between buyer and manufacturer, handling sourcing and sampling) and production merchandisers (who link production with buyer merchants to ensure schedules are met).
Target Market and Market Segmentation

To interpret consumer demand, merchandisers must segment the market to focus sales efforts on those most likely to purchase.

The Six Merchandising Rights

  • Right Merchandise: Stocking products that the customer actively wants.
  • Right Place: Ensuring convenient location and accessibility of merchandise.
  • Right Time: Aligning inventory with seasonal demands.
  • Right Quantity: Maintaining a profitable balance between sales volume and inventory.
  • Right Price: Setting prices high enough for profit, yet low enough to meet competition and customer expectations.
  • Right Promotion: Balancing investment with promotional appeal to drive sales.
Fashion Retail Organisations and Divisions

Retail structures vary based on merchandise type, store size, and target customers.

Types of Retail Businesses

  • Small Single-unit Store: Neighborhood stores, typically owner- and family-operated.
  • Department Stores: Large stores consisting of separate sections (departments) such as clothing, sporting goods, and electronics.
  • Chain Stores: Retail outlets sharing a brand, central management, and standardized business methods.

Major Divisions in Retail Organisations

  • Merchandising Division: Handles buying, merchandise planning, inventory control, selling, and fashion coordination.
  • Sales and Promotion Division: Manages advertising, visual merchandising, special events, publicity, and public relations.
  • Finance and Control Division: Manages credit, accounts payable, and inventory control.
  • Operational Division: Maintains facilities, handles store security, customer service, and receiving/marking merchandise.
  • Personnel and Branch Store Division: Manages human resources and coordinates branch operations in large retail systems.
Preparing for a Career and Scope

A successful career in fashion requires a combination of style, business acumen, and specialized education.

Three Primary Skills Required

  • Forecasting Ability: Predicting future fashion trends by analyzing past and current industry shifts.
  • Analytical Ability: Understanding the financial aspects, analyzing the economy, and managing budgets to ensure profitability.
  • Communication Ability: Negotiating prices with manufacturers, writing advertising copy, press releases, and communicating with consumers.

Educational Pathways

  • Certificate or Diploma (6 months to 1 year): Focuses directly on the practical job aspects of fashion merchandising; ideal for quick entry into the field.
  • Associate or Master's Degrees (2 years): Combines liberal arts or general education with specialized fashion and business courses.
  • Bachelor's Degrees (4 years): Comprehensive programs combining liberal arts, fashion design, and business courses; suitable for long-term career growth and promotional opportunities.

Career Options and Scope

  • Visual Merchandise Designers: Design window displays, arrange store merchandise, style mannequins, and lead marketing campaigns.
  • Fashion Designers: Create designs for clothing and apparel, working for established labels or managing independent brands.
  • Set Designers: Conceptualize and create designs for movies, television, theatre productions, trade shows, and museums.
  • Interior Designers: Combine form and function to design safe, beautiful, and functional spaces in retail, residential, and commercial settings.
Practical 1: Developing a Female Fashion Figure

Learning the proportions and sketching techniques of a fashion figure (croquis) to communicate design ideas.

Croquis Proportions and Guidelines

  • A fashion figure is constructed using the head as the unit of measurement. Full size ranges from $8\frac{1}{2}$ to $10$ heads.
  • Width Proportions: Shoulders are the widest point; waist is the smallest; hips are in between.
  • Vertical Proportions: The figure is approximately $\frac{1}{2}$ from the top of the head to the bottom of the torso, and $\frac{1}{2}$ from the end of the torso to the ankle.
  • Horizontal Guidelines: Shoulderline, Bustline, Waistline, Hip plane, End of torso.
  • Vertical Guidelines: Centre front, Armhole, Princess line.

Step-by-Step Sketching Guide (10-Head Figure)

  1. Draw a central line measuring 10 heads (where 1 head = 1 inch). This is the Centre Front line.
  2. Divide the line into 10 equal parts.
  3. Mark the following levels:
    • Eye line: $\frac{1}{2}$ head
    • Shoulder line: $1\frac{1}{2}$ heads
    • Bust line: Just below 2
    • Waist line: Just below 3
    • Hip plane: $3\frac{1}{2}$ heads
    • End of torso: Bit above $4\frac{1}{2}$ heads
    • Knees: $6\frac{1}{2}$ heads
    • Ankles: Above or at 9
  4. Join the levels with a pencil to develop the human form.
  5. Practice at least 5 croquis with different head measurements to build proficiency.
