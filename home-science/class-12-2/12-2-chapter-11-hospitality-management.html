Unit V: Resource Management - Introduction

Resource Management focuses on the allocation and utilisation of human and non-human resources to achieve individual, family, and organisational goals. It integrates consumer finance, ergonomics, interior/exterior space design, hospitality, and event management.

  • Ergonomics: Study of efficiency in working environments; applied to homes, public spaces, and industries to enhance productivity and safety.
  • Consumer Guidance: Professional assistance and counselling centers to help consumers resolve product and service quality issues.
  • Event Management: A specialty profession managing diverse events such as product launches, exhibitions, weddings, and conferences.
  • Interior & Landscape Design: Planning and managing indoor and outdoor spaces to create attractive, user-friendly, and sustainable environments.
Chapter 11: Hospitality Management

Learning Objectives

  • Explain the importance of hospitality management.
  • Explain the functioning of food and beverage departments of the hospitality industry.
  • Describe the functioning of the housekeeping department.
  • Discuss the functioning of the front office in the hospitality industry.
  • Identify various career opportunities available in the hospitality field.

Introduction & Significance

In Indian culture, guests are regarded as God-like, encapsulated in the traditional ethos of Athithi Devo Bhava, implying that every guest must be received with warmth, care, and excellent service. Historically, travellers stayed with relatives, friends, or in traditional dharmashalas. Modern trade, commerce, and globalisation have necessitated paid hospitality services.

  • Globalisation: Has transformed the world into a small place, increasing travel for business, marketing, education, pilgrimages, medical tourism, leisure, and cultural festivals.
  • A Home Away From Home: The core philosophy of the hospitality industry, ensuring tourists receive comforts, safety, and hygiene comparable to their homes.

Basic Concepts & Types of Establishments

Hospitality is the relationship between the guest and the host, involving the friendly, generous reception, entertainment, and provision of services with warmth and courtesy.

  • Hotel: A commercial establishment offering lodging, meals, and other guest services.
  • Motel: Provides services similar to a hotel, featuring parking facilities near the rooms or room doors opening directly onto the parking lot.
  • Lodge: Offers rented accommodation primarily for sleeping, often without food and other services.
  • Resort: Known for leisure attractions, offering a broad range of amenities, sports facilities, and activities for a complete vacation experience.
  • Furnished Apartments: Provide all essential household amenities needed for short or long-term guest stays.
  • Furnished Camps: Provide hospitality services to people engaged in hiking, adventure sports, and outdoor activities.

The hospitality industry is divided into five major sectors: Tourism, Food & Beverage Service, Accommodation (Front Office), Meetings & Events, and Entertainment & Recreation.

The Guest Cycle

The Guest Cycle represents the systematic stages of interaction between a guest and a hospitality establishment, starting before physical arrival and extending beyond departure.

  1. Pre-arrival Stage: Activities include quoting room rates and reserving rooms through a central reservation system.
  2. Arrival Stage: The guest physically arrives, registers, and checks-in.
  3. Occupancy Stage: Provision of various services, coordination of guest requirements, and ensuring security to build customer loyalty.
  4. Departure Stage: The final stage where the guest checks out, settles bills, and the hotel updates the guest history record along with feedback.

Front Office Department

The Front Office is the focal point of any hotel, serving as the primary contact point where guests interact with staff. It coordinates with other departments like transport, accounts, billing, room service, restaurants, engineering, and housekeeping.

Housekeeping Department

The Housekeeping Department is primarily responsible for providing a clean, hygienic, safe, and aesthetically pleasing environment throughout the establishment.

  • Key Functions: Cleaning public areas and guestrooms; supply, upkeep, and laundering of linen and uniforms; internal flower arrangements; pest control; and coordination with other departments.
  • Eco-Friendly Practices: Using 100% organic cotton sheets, chemical-free laundry cycles, non-toxic biodegradable cleaning agents, and energy-efficient lighting.

Sections of the Housekeeping Department

  1. Housekeeping Control Desk: The central communication hub passing information to staff across the hotel.
  2. Housekeeping Management: Plans, organises, and controls all department activities.
  3. Guestroom Brigade: Upkeeps and cleans guestrooms.
  4. Public Area Brigade: Maintains cleanliness of foyers, lobbies, offices, and common areas.
  5. Linen and Uniform Room: Coordinates with laundry; provides clean linen and uniforms.
  6. Horticulture and Flower Arrangement Team: Maintains landscapes, gardens, and indoor floral displays.
  7. Lost and Found Section: Safely deposits and records misplaced guest luggage and items.

Housekeeping Personnel Responsibilities

  • Executive Housekeeper: Manages the department through judicious use of manpower, materials, money, and time.
  • Assistant Housekeeper: Prepares duty schedules and monitors cleanliness across shifts.
  • Desk Control Supervisor: Coordinates with the front office regarding room occupancy and departures.
  • Floor Supervisor: Responsible for the cleanliness of guest floors, corridors, staircases, and pantries.
  • Room Attendant: Performs actual cleaning of guest rooms and bathrooms.
  • Public Area Supervisor: Maintains cleanliness of main entrances, offices, banquet halls, and restaurants.
  • Florist / Gardener: Maintains gardens and creates floral arrangements.
  • Linen Room / Uniform Room Supervisor: Responsible for storage, issue, and cleanliness of linens and uniforms.

Food and Beverages Department

The Food and Beverages (F&B) Department is responsible for the preparation and sale of food and beverages across various venues.

  • Kitchen: The food preparation area. Large hotels feature specialized sections: butchery, bakery, confectionery, vegetable prep, soup section, pantry, and hot range. Led by the Executive Chef (Chef-de-cuisine), assisted by the deputy chef, Chef-de-parties (section supervisors), and cooks.
  • Kitchen Stewarding: Responsible for the storage, maintenance, cleanliness, and issue of cutlery, crockery, and glassware. Led by the executive kitchen steward, managing dishwashers, pot washers, and utility workers.
  • Restaurant: A commercial facility providing food and beverages. Managed by a Restaurant Manager, senior restaurant supervisors, Head Waiters, waiters/stewards, and assistant waiters.

Support Departments & Management Functions

Behind-the-scenes operations are managed by Support Service Departments (Back Office):

  • Finance and Accounts: Manages financial transactions, billing, and budgeting.
  • Engineering: Maintains physical infrastructure, utilities, and machinery.
  • Human Resources (HR): Handles recruitment, training, and staff welfare.
  • Sales and Marketing: Drives business, promotions, and public relations.

The core Management Functions governing these operations include: Planning, Organising, Co-ordinating, Staffing, Directing, Controlling, and Evaluating resources.

Scope & Career Opportunities

The hospitality industry is fast-growing and prefers a youthful workforce due to high energy demands, adaptability to new technologies, and long working hours. Essential competencies include professional grooming, high hygiene standards, orderliness, courtesy, physical fitness, and multilingual communication skills.

  • Career Paths:
    • Positions in housekeeping departments at various hierarchical levels.
    • Entrepreneurial opportunities in commercial housekeeping and cleaning services.
    • Front office, reception, and control desk operations in hotels and corporate offices.
    • Entrepreneurial ventures in floral design and landscape maintenance.
    • Specialisation in theme-based event management within hospitality organisations.
