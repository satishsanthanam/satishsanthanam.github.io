Introduction and Significance

All humans are natural consumers, purchasing a variety of goods and services to satisfy their needs and desires. Consumer Education and Protection teaches individuals to become efficient, alert, and well-informed consumers, ensuring they get maximum satisfaction from their financial resources.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the importance and role of Consumer Education and Protection.
  • Explain basic concepts, consumer rights, and responsibilities.
  • Analyse the knowledge and skills needed for a career in this field.
  • Understand the scope and diverse career options available.

Significance of Consumer Education and Protection

  • Market Expansion: The steady increase of manufactured products in both urban and rural markets requires consumers to make informed choices.
  • Globalisation: Living in a "global village" exposes consumers to global markets, multinational products, and complex technologies, making selection more challenging.
  • Consumer Redressal: Manufacturers can no longer take consumers for granted; company reputation and profits are directly determined by consumer opinion and satisfaction.
  • Protection against Malpractices: Empowers consumers against exploitation, misleading advertisements, unsafe products, and unscrupulous sellers.
Basic Concepts and Consumer Expectations

Understanding key terms is essential for navigating the consumer market effectively.

Key Definitions

  • Consumer: The final buyer of goods and services for the satisfaction of personal needs and wants.
  • Consumer Product: Any article produced or distributed for sale to a consumer for personal, family, household, institutional, or business use.
  • Consumer Behaviour: The decision-making process through which buyers purchase goods and services.
  • Consumer Forum: A place or organisation where consumers discuss products/services, advantages, disadvantages, and seek protection against exploitation.
  • Consumer Footfalls: The number of customers or consumers who visit a given retail space, store, or mall.

Major Problems Faced by Consumers

Consumers regularly encounter various unfair trade practices and market challenges:

  1. Substandard/Poor Quality Goods: Variations in materials and manufacturing standards make it difficult for consumers to identify poor quality, especially when ignorant of quality standards.
  2. Adulteration: Intentional or unintentional addition or removal of substances, altering the composition and quality, which can cause severe harm to health and safety.
  3. High Prices: Overcharging by suppliers who exploit a consumer's lack of information, even though price does not always correlate with quality.
  4. Lack of Consumer Information: Ignorance of consumer rights, responsibilities, and protective legislative provisions.
  5. Inadequate or Erroneous Information: Deceptive labels, uninformative advertisements, lack of buying guides, and misleading oversized packaging ("new pack" gimmicks).
  6. Incorrect Weights and Measures: Retailers using altered weights, non-standard measures, or unsealed scales, leading to less quantity than paid for.
  7. Spurious/Duplicate/Imitation Products: Counterfeit goods with similar packaging, colour schemes, and sounding brand names that are unsafe and of poor quality.
  8. Deceptive Sales Promotion Schemes: Gimmicks like fake exchange offers, bonuses, and lucky draws designed to entice and deceive consumers.
  9. Deficient Services: Poor after-sales service and failure of public utilities (water, electricity, banking, insurance) to deliver promised contractual standards.
Consumer Protection Act and Consumer Rights

The Government of India has established robust legal frameworks to protect consumer interests.

The Consumer Protection Act (CPA)

  • CPA 1986: Landmark legislation providing simple, speedy, and inexpensive redressal of consumer grievances. It allows consumers to file complaints and claim compensation for negligence.
  • CPA 2019: Replaced the three-decade-old CPA 1986. It introduced a central regulator, strict penalties for misleading advertisements, and comprehensive guidelines for e-commerce and electronic service providers.

The Six Consumer Rights

  1. Right to Safety: Protection against products, production processes, and services that are hazardous to health or life.
  2. Right to be Informed: Access to accurate information about quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard, and price to prevent unfair trade practices.
  3. Right to Choose: Assured access to a variety of products and services at competitive prices, matching individual needs.
  4. Right to be Heard: Assurance that consumer interests will receive due consideration at appropriate forums, including government and voluntary bodies.
  5. Right to Seek Redressal: Right to seek legal remedies against unfair trade practices or exploitation, including fair settlement of genuine claims and compensation for faulty goods.
  6. Right to Consumer Education: Right to acquire the knowledge and skills necessary to be an informed consumer throughout life.

Consumer Responsibilities

To exercise their rights effectively, consumers must act responsibly by adhering to the following guidelines:

  1. Update Knowledge: Regularly update knowledge regarding consumer protection laws, legislative provisions, and amendments.
  2. Honest Dealings: Be honest in all transactions and ensure payment is made for all purchases.
  3. Market Survey: Conduct a comparative market survey of different brands, features, and prices before making a purchase.
  4. Inspect Labels: Carefully read all information on product labels, brochures, and instruction manuals.
  5. Choose Quality: Buy products carrying standardisation marks (ISI, AGMARK, FSSAI, etc.) to ensure safety and quality.
  6. Keep Proof of Purchase: Retain cash memos, receipts, warranty cards, and invoices as legal proof of purchase for filing complaints.
  7. Understand Service Terms: Read and clarify all terms, conditions, liabilities, and service charges before buying financial or utility services (insurance, credit cards, bank deposits).
  8. Support Consumer Organisations: Develop awareness of national and international consumer organisations and support their activities.
Career Opportunities and Scope

The field of consumer studies offers diverse professional pathways across public, private, and non-profit sectors.

Required Skills

  • Thorough knowledge of quality standards, adulteration detection, and consumer protection laws.
  • Soft skills: Good communication, interpersonal skills, empathetic attitude, and active listening.
  • Creative skills: Developing consumer awareness programmes, advertisements, and educational materials.
  • Writing skills: Drafting educational leaflets, reporting consumer tests, and writing articles.

Educational Qualifications

  • Undergraduate/Postgraduate degrees in B.Sc. Home Science, Family Resource Management, or Business Administration (B.B.A. / B.B.S.).
  • Specialised courses: PG Diploma in Consumer Education, PG Diploma in Management of Voluntary Organisations, or PG Diploma in Consumer Services.

Professional Scope

  • Government Organisations: Managerial and technical positions in the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS), Directorate of Marketing and Inspection, and Ministry of Consumer Affairs.
  • Voluntary Consumer Organisations (NGOs): Product testing, advocacy, publishing consumer magazines (e.g., Consumer Voice, Insight), and running awareness campaigns.
  • Corporate Sector: Working in Consumer Divisions, Customer Relationship Management (CRM), and customer facilitation cells.
  • Market Research: Analysing consumer behaviour, product reach, feedback, and market acceptance of new products.
  • National Consumer Helpline: Consumer counselling and helping individuals seek legal redressal.
  • Educational Institutions: Managing consumer clubs in schools/colleges, teaching, and developing educational content.
  • Testing Laboratories: Working as analysts in consumer testing laboratories for comparative product evaluation.
