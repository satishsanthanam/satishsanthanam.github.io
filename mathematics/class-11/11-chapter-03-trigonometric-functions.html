Mathematics: Class 11 — Chapter 3: Trigonometric Functions

Introduction & Angle Measurement

Trigonometry, derived from the Greek words trigon (triangle) and metron (measure), originally focused on measuring the sides and angles of a triangle. Modern trigonometry has expanded into a foundational branch of mathematics with applications in seismology, electrical circuit design, atomic state description, oceanography, and acoustics.

Angles and Direction of Rotation

An angle is a measure of rotation of a given ray about its initial point. The original ray is called the initial side , and the final position of the ray after rotation is called the terminal side . The point of rotation is the vertex .

  • Positive Angle: Generated if the direction of rotation is anticlockwise.
  • Negative Angle: Generated if the direction of rotation is clockwise.
Units of Angle Measurement

Angles are primarily measured in two systems: degree measure and radian measure .

  • Degree Measure: If a rotation from the initial side to the terminal side is $\\frac{1}{360}$ of a revolution, the angle is said to have a measure of one degree ($1^\\\circ$).
    • A degree is divided into 60 minutes: $1^\\\circ = 60'$
    • A minute is divided into 60 seconds: $1' = 60''$
  • Radian Measure: The angle subtended at the centre of a unit circle (radius 1 unit) by \tan arc of length 1 unit is defined as 1 radian.
    • In a circle of radius $r$, \tan arc of length $l$ subtends \tan angle $\\theta$ radians at the centre, given by:$$\\theta = \\frac{l}{r} \\\quad \\\text{or} \\\quad l = r\\theta$$
Relation Between Radian, Real Numbers, and Degrees

Radian measures and real numbers can be considered as one and the same. By placing a real number line tangent to a unit circle at point $A(1,0)$, wrapping the positive line anticlockwise and the negative line clockwise associates every real number with a unique radian angle.

Since a complete revolution of a circle subtends $2\\pi$ radians at the centre, we establish the fundamental equivalence:

$$2\\pi \\\text{ radians} = 360^\\\circ \\\implies \\pi \\\text{ radians} = 180^\\\circ$$

This yields the following conversion formulas:

  • Radian to Degree: $1 \\\text{ radian} = \\frac{180^\\\circ}{\\pi} \\\approx 57^\\\circ 16'$
  • Degree to Radian: $1^\\\circ = \\frac{\\pi}{180} \\\text{ radians} \\\approx 0.01746 \\\text{ radians}$

Radian Measure $\\frac{\\pi}{6}$ $\\frac{\\pi}{4}$ $\\frac{\\pi}{3}$ $\\frac{\\pi}{2}$ $\\pi$ $\\frac{3\\pi}{2}$ $2\\pi$
Trigonometric Functions & Quadrant Behavior

Trigonometric ratios are generalized to trigonometric functions for any real angle using a unit circle coordinate system.

Unit Circle Definition

Let $P(a, b)$ be any point on a unit circle centered at the origin $O(0,0)$ such that the angle $AOP = x$ radians. The arc length $AP = x$. We define:

$$\\cos x = a \\\quad \\\text{and} \\\quad \\sin x = b$$

Since $P(a,b)$ lies on the unit circle $a^2 + b^2 = 1$, we obtain the fundamental identity:

$$\\cos^2 x + \\sin^2 x = 1$$

Angles that are integral multiples of $\\frac{\\pi}{2}$ are called quadrantal angles . Their coordinates yield:

  • $\\cos 0 = 1, \\sin 0 = 0$
  • $\\cos \\frac{\\pi}{2} = 0, \\sin \\frac{\\pi}{2} = 1$
  • $\\cos \\pi = -1, \\sin \\pi = 0$
  • $\\cos \\frac{3\\pi}{2} = 0, \\sin \\frac{3\\pi}{2} = -1$
  • $\\cos 2\\pi = 1, \\sin 2\\pi = 0$

Since a complete revolution returns to the same point, trigonometric functions are periodic with period $2\\pi$:

$$\\sin(2n\\pi + x) = \\sin x, \\\quad \\cos(2n\\pi + x) = \\cos x \\\quad (n \\in \\\mathbb{Z})$$

Trigonometric Identities & Compound Angle Formulas

Trigonometric identities establish relationships between functions for sums, differences, and multiples of angles.

Fundamental Pythagorean Identities
  • $\\cos^2 x + \\sin^2 x = 1$
  • $1 + \\tan^2 x = \\sec^2 x$
  • $1 + \\cot^2 x = \\csc^2 x$
Sum and Difference of Two Angles (Compound Angles)
  • $\\cos(x + y) = \\cos x \\cos y - \\sin x \\sin y$
  • $\\cos(x - y) = \\cos x \\cos y + \\sin x \\sin y$
  • $\\sin(x + y) = \\sin x \\cos y + \\cos x \\sin y$
  • $\\sin(x - y) = \\sin x \\cos y - \\cos x \\sin y$
  • $\\tan(x + y) = \\frac{\\tan x + \\tan y}{1 - \\tan x \\tan y}$
  • $\\tan(x - y) = \\frac{\\tan x - \\tan y}{1 + \\tan x \\tan y}$
  • $\\cot(x + y) = \\frac{\\cot x \\cot y - 1}{\\cot y + \\cot x}$
  • $\\cot(x - y) = \\frac{\\cot x \\cot y + 1}{\\cot y - \\cot x}$
Multiple Angle Formulas

Double Angle Formulas:

  • $\\sin 2x = 2\\sin x \\cos x = \\frac{2\\tan x}{1 + \\tan^2 x}$
  • $\\cos 2x = \\cos^2 x - \\sin^2 x = 2\\cos^2 x - 1 = 1 - 2\\sin^2 x = \\frac{1 - \\tan^2 x}{1 + \\tan^2 x}$
  • $\\tan 2x = \\frac{2\\tan x}{1 - \\tan^2 x}$

Triple Angle Formulas:

  • $\\sin 3x = 3\\sin x - 4\\sin^3 x$
  • $\\cos 3x = 4\\cos^3 x - 3\\cos x$
  • $\\tan 3x = \\frac{3\\tan x - \\tan^3 x}{1 - 3\\tan^2 x}$
Product-to-Sum and Sum-to-Product Formulas

Sum-to-Product Formulas:

  • $\\sin x + \\sin y = 2\\sin\\frac{x+y}{2}\\cos\\frac{x-y}{2}$
  • $\\sin x - \\sin y = 2\\cos\\frac{x+y}{2}\\sin\\frac{x-y}{2}$
  • $\\cos x + \\cos y = 2\\cos\\frac{x+y}{2}\\cos\\frac{x-y}{2}$
  • $\\cos x - \\cos y = -2\\sin\\frac{x+y}{2}\\sin\\frac{x-y}{2}$

Product-to-Sum Formulas:

  • $2\\sin x \\cos y = \\sin(x + y) + \\sin(x - y)$
  • $2\\cos x \\sin y = \\sin(x + y) - \\sin(x - y)$
  • $2\\cos x \\cos y = \\cos(x + y) + \\cos(x - y)$
  • $-2\\sin x \\sin y = \\cos(x + y) - \\cos(x - y)$
Algorithmic Problem Solving & Solved Examples
Angle Conversion and Arc Length Problems

Mathematical Problem Statement: Convert $40^\\\circ 20'$ into radian measure.

Step-by-Step Algorithmic Solution Trace:

  1. Convert minutes to degrees: $20' = \\frac{20}{60}^\\\circ = \\frac{1}{3}^\\\circ$.
  2. Express total angle in degrees: $40^\\\circ 20' = 40\\frac{1}{3}^\\\circ = \\frac{121}{3}^\\\circ$.
  3. Apply conversion factor (multiply by $\\frac{\\pi}{180}$):$$\\\text{Radian measure} = \\frac{121}{3} \\times \\frac{\\pi}{180} = \\frac{121\\pi}{540}$$

Final Evaluation Output: $\\frac{121\\pi}{540} \\\text{ radians}$

Mathematical Problem Statement: Find the distance moved by the tip of a $1.5\\\text{ cm}$ long minute hand of a watch in $40\\\text{ minutes}$ (Use $\\pi = 3.14$).

Step-by-Step Algorithmic Solution Trace:

  1. Determine the fraction of a full revolution in 40 minutes: $\\frac{40}{60} = \\frac{2}{3}$ of a revolution.
  2. Convert the revolution to radians: $\\theta = \\frac{2}{3} \\times 2\\pi = \\frac{4\\pi}{3}$ radians.
  3. Use the arc length formula $l = r\\theta$ with $r = 1.5\\\text{ cm}$:$$l = 1.5 \\times \\frac{4\\pi}{3} = 2\\pi \\\text{ cm}$$
  4. Substitute $\\pi \\\approx 3.14$: $l = 2 \\times 3.14 = 6.28\\\text{ cm}$.

Final Evaluation Output: $6.28\\\text{ cm}$

Quadrant-Constrained Function Evaluation

Mathematical Problem Statement: If $\\cos x = -\\frac{3}{5}$ and $x$ lies in the third quadrant, find the values of the other five trigonometric functions.

Step-by-Step Algorithmic Solution Trace:

  1. Find $\\sec x$ as the reciprocal of $\\cos x$: $\\sec x = -\\frac{5}{3}$.
  2. Use the identity $\\sin^2 x = 1 - \\cos^2 x$ to find $\\sin x$:$$\\sin^2 x = 1 - \\left(-\\frac{3}{5}\\right)^2 = 1 - \\frac{9}{25} = \\frac{16}{25} \\\implies \\sin x = \\\pm \\frac{4}{5}$$
  3. Since $x$ lies in the third quadrant, sine is negative: $\\sin x = -\\frac{4}{5}$.
  4. Find $\\csc x$ as the reciprocal of $\\sin x$: $\\csc x = -\\frac{5}{4}$.
  5. Calculate $\\tan x = \\frac{\\sin x}{\\cos x} = \\frac{-4/5}{-3/5} = \\frac{4}{3}$.
  6. Calculate $\\cot x = \\frac{1}{\\tan x} = \\frac{3}{4}$.

Final Evaluation Output: $\\sin x = -\\frac{4}{5}, \\csc x = -\\frac{5}{4}, \\tan x = \\frac{4}{3}, \\cot x = \\frac{3}{4}, \\sec x = -\\frac{5}{3}$

Compound and Multiple Angle Proofs

Mathematical Problem Statement: Prove that $\\frac{\\sin 5x - 2\\sin 3x + \\sin x}{\\cos 5x - \\cos x} = \\tan x$.

Step-by-Step Algorithmic Solution Trace:

  1. Rearrange the numerator: $(\\sin 5x + \\sin x) - 2\\sin 3x$.
  2. Apply the sum-to-product formula to the numerator group:$$\\sin 5x + \\sin x = 2\\sin\\frac{5x+x}{2}\\cos\\frac{5x-x}{2} = 2\\sin 3x \\cos 2x$$
  3. Substitute back into the numerator: $2\\sin 3x \\cos 2x - 2\\sin 3x = 2\\sin 3x(\\cos 2x - 1)$.
  4. Apply the sum-to-product formula to the denominator:$$\\cos 5x - \\cos x = -2\\sin\\frac{5x+x}{2}\\sin\\frac{5x-x}{2} = -2\\sin 3x \\sin 2x$$
  5. Form the ratio:$$\\frac{2\\sin 3x(\\cos 2x - 1)}{-2\\sin 3x \\sin 2x} = \\frac{\\cos 2x - 1}{-\\sin 2x} = \\frac{1 - \\cos 2x}{\\sin 2x}$$
  6. Use double angle identities ($1 - \\cos 2x = 2\\sin^2 x$ and $\\sin 2x = 2\\sin x \\cos x$):$$\\frac{2\\sin^2 x}{2\\sin x \\cos x} = \\frac{\\sin x}{\\cos x} = \\tan x$$

Final Evaluation Output: $\\\text{L.H.S.} = \\\text{R.H.S.} = \\tan x$ (Proved)

Historical Chronicles & Mathematical Evolution

Historical Chronicle: Indian Origins of Trigonometry

  • Historical Context & Milestones: Trigonometric studies originated in ancient India. Renowned mathematicians such as Aryabhata (476 CE), Brahmagupta (598 CE), Bhaskara I (600 CE), and Bhaskara II (1114 CE) formulated foundational principles. This knowledge was transmitted to the Middle East and subsequently to Europe.
  • Theoretical Foundations: The predecessor of the modern sine function was known as jya or ardha-jya (half-chord) in Sanskrit astronomical texts ( siddhantas ). Bhaskara I developed accurate approximation formulas for sine values of angles greater than $90^\circ$. The 16th-century Malayalam treatise Yuktibhasa contains early proofs for the expansion of $\\sin(A+B)$.

Historical Chronicle: Greek and Western Developments

  • Historical Context & Milestones: Early Greek mathematicians like Thales (circa 600 BCE) applied geometric similarity to solve height and distance problems, such as determining the height of Egyptian pyramids by comparing shadow lengths.
  • Theoretical Foundations: The modern inverse trigonometric notation (such as $\\sin^{-1} x$ and $\\cos^{-1} x$) was introduced much later by astronomer Sir John F.W. Herschel in 1813.
