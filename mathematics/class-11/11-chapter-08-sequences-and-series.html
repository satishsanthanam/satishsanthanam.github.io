Mathematics: Class 11 — Chapter 8: Sequences and Series

Introduction to Sequences and Series
Definitions and Basic Concepts

A sequence is an ordered collection of numbers arranged in a definite order according to a specific rule. Formally, a sequence can be regarded as a function whose domain is the set of natural numbers $\mathbb{N}$ or a subset of the form $\{1, 2, 3, \dots, k\}$.

  • Terms of a Sequence: The various numbers occurring in a sequence are called its terms, denoted by $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots, a_n, \dots$.
  • General Term: The term $a_n$ at the $n$-th position is called the general term of the sequence.
  • Finite Sequence: A sequence containing a finite number of terms (e.g., the number of ancestors over a fixed generation span).
  • Infinite Sequence: A sequence containing infinitely many terms (e.g., the successive quotients in the division of 10 by 3: $3, 3.3, 3.33, 3.333, \dots$).
Fibonacci Sequence & Recurrence Relations

Some sequences do not have an obvious explicit algebraic formula for their $n$-th term but are instead generated by a recurrence relation, where subsequent terms are defined using preceding terms.

The Fibonacci sequence is a classic example defined by the recurrence relation:

$$a_1 = a_2 = 1$$ $$a_n = a_{n-2} + a_{n-1} \text{ for } n > 2$$
Series and Sigma Notation

Let $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots, a_n$ be a given sequence. The algebraic expression representing the sum of these terms:

$$a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \dots + a_n + \dots$$

is called the series associated with the given sequence. A series is finite or infinite depending on whether the underlying sequence is finite or infinite.

Series are compactly represented using the Greek letter $\sum$ (sigma notation):

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k = a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \dots + a_n$$

Remark: The term "series" refers to the indicated sum expression, whereas the "sum of a series" refers to the single numerical value resulting from the addition of those terms.

Geometric Progression (G.P.)
Definition and General Form

A sequence $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots, a_n, \dots$ is called a geometric progression (or G.P.) if each term is non-zero and the ratio of any term to its immediately preceding term is a constant throughout the sequence.

Mathematically, for all $k \ge 1$:

$$\frac{a_{k+1}}{a_k} = r \text{ (constant)}$$

Where:

  • $a$ (or $a_1$) is the first term of the G.P.
  • $r$ is the common ratio of the G.P.

The general form of a G.P. is written as:

$$a, ar, ar^2, ar^3, \dots, ar^{n-1}, \dots$$
General Term (n-th Term) of a G.P.

The $n$-th term ($a_n$) of a geometric progression is given by the formula:

$$a_n = a r^{n-1}$$

This is also referred to as the general term of the G.P.

Sum to n Terms of a G.P.

Let $S_n$ denote the sum of the first $n$ terms of a G.P. with first term $a$ and common ratio $r$. The sum is evaluated under two distinct cases:

Condition on Common Ratio ($r$) Sum Formula ($S_n$) Derivation Methodology
$r = 1$ $S_n = na$ Direct summation of $n$ identical terms: $a + a + \dots + a$.
$r \neq 1$ $S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1} = \frac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r}$ Obtained by subtracting $r S_n$ from $S_n$ to eliminate intermediate terms.
Geometric Mean (G.M.) and Relationship with A.M.
Geometric Mean of Two Numbers

The geometric mean (G.M.) of two positive real numbers $a$ and $b$ is defined as the positive square root of their product:

$$G = \sqrt{ab}$$

If $G$ is the geometric mean of $a$ and $b$, then the sequence $a, G, b$ forms a geometric progression because:

$$\frac{G}{a} = \frac{b}{G} \implies G^2 = ab$$
Insertion of n Geometric Means

Given any two positive numbers $a$ and $b$, we can insert $n$ numbers $G_1, G_2, \dots, G_n$ between them such that the resulting sequence $a, G_1, G_2, \dots, G_n, b$ is a geometric progression.

Since $b$ is the $(n+2)$-th term of this G.P., we have:

$$b = a r^{n+1} \implies r = \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}$$

The inserted geometric means are calculated as:

  • $G_1 = ar = a \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}$
  • $G_2 = ar^2 = a \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{\frac{2}{n+1}}$
  • $G_n = ar^n = a \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}$
Relationship Between Arithmetic Mean (A.M.) and Geometric Mean (G.M.)

Let $A$ and $G$ be the Arithmetic Mean and Geometric Mean of two positive real numbers $a$ and $b$, respectively:

$$A = \frac{a + b}{2}, \quad G = \sqrt{ab}$$

Theorem: For any two positive real numbers $a$ and $b$, the Arithmetic Mean is always greater than or equal to the Geometric Mean:

$$A \ge G$$

Proof:

$$A - G = \frac{a + b}{2} - \sqrt{ab} = \frac{a + b - 2\sqrt{ab}}{2} = \frac{(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})^2}{2}$$

Since the square of any real number is non-negative, we have:

$$\frac{(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})^2}{2} \ge 0 \implies A - G \ge 0 \implies A \ge G$$

Equality holds if and only if $a = b$.

Algorithmic Problem Solving & Solved Examples
Example 9: Sum and Product of Three Terms of a G.P.

Mathematical Problem Statement: The sum of the first three terms of a G.P. is $\frac{13}{12}$ and their product is $-1$. Find the common ratio and the terms.

Step-by-Step Algorithmic Solution Trace:

  1. Let the first three terms of the G.P. be $\frac{a}{r}$, $a$, and $ar$. This choice simplifies the product calculation.
  2. Set up the product equation:$$\left(\frac{a}{r}\right)(a)(ar) = -1 \implies a^3 = -1$$Taking the real root, we find $a = -1$.
  3. Set up the sum equation:$$\frac{a}{r} + a + ar = \frac{13}{12}$$Substitute $a = -1$:$$-\frac{1}{r} - 1 - r = \frac{13}{12} \implies -\left(\frac{1 + r + r^2}{r}\right) = \frac{13}{12}$$
  4. Clear fractions to form a quadratic equation:$$-12(1 + r + r^2) = 13r \implies 12r^2 + 25r + 12 = 0$$
  5. Solve the quadratic equation using factorization:$$12r^2 + 16r + 9r + 12 = 0 \implies 4r(3r + 4) + 3(3r + 4) = 0 \implies (4r + 3)(3r + 4) = 0$$Thus, $r = -\frac{3}{4}$ or $r = -\frac{4}{3}$.
  6. Calculate the terms for each value of $r$:
    • For $r = -\frac{3}{4}$ and $a = -1$, the terms are: $\frac{4}{3}, -1, \frac{3}{4}$.
    • For $r = -\frac{4}{3}$ and $a = -1$, the terms are: $\frac{3}{4}, -1, \frac{4}{3}$.

Final Evaluation Output: The common ratio is $r = -\frac{3}{4}$ or $r = -\frac{4}{3}$, and the three terms are $\frac{4}{3}, -1, \frac{3}{4}$ (or vice versa).

Example 10: Summing the Non-G.P. Series $7, 77, 777, \dots$

Mathematical Problem Statement: Find the sum of the sequence $7, 77, 777, 7777, \dots$ to $n$ terms.

Step-by-Step Algorithmic Solution Trace:

  1. Write the sum expression:$$S_n = 7 + 77 + 777 + 7777 + \dots \text{ to } n \text{ terms}$$
  2. Factor out $7$ to obtain a series of $1$s:$$S_n = 7(1 + 11 + 111 + 1111 + \dots \text{ to } n \text{ terms})$$
  3. Multiply and divide by $9$ to introduce powers of $10$:$$S_n = \frac{7}{9}(9 + 99 + 999 + 9999 + \dots \text{ to } n \text{ terms})$$
  4. Express each term as a power of $10$ minus $1$:$$S_n = \frac{7}{9}[(10 - 1) + (10^2 - 1) + (10^3 - 1) + \dots + (10^n - 1)]$$
  5. Regroup the terms into a geometric progression and a constant sum:$$S_n = \frac{7}{9}[(10 + 10^2 + 10^3 + \dots + 10^n) - (1 + 1 + 1 + \dots + 1 \text{ up to } n \text{ terms})]$$
  6. Apply the G.P. sum formula to the first part ($a = 10, r = 10$):$$S_n = \frac{7}{9}\left[\frac{10(10^n - 1)}{10 - 1} - n\right] = \frac{7}{9}\left[\frac{10(10^n - 1)}{9} - n\right]$$

Final Evaluation Output: $S_n = \frac{70}{81}(10^n - 1) - \frac{7}{9}n$

Example 14: Advanced Algebraic Proof of G.P. Condition

Mathematical Problem Statement: If $a, b, c, d$ and $p$ are different real numbers such that $(a^2 + b^2 + c^2)p^2 - 2(ab + bc + cd)p + (b^2 + c^2 + d^2) \le 0$, show that $a, b, c, d$ are in G.P.

Step-by-Step Algorithmic Solution Trace:

  1. Expand and regroup the given inequality:$$(a^2p^2 - 2abp + b^2) + (b^2p^2 - 2bcp + c^2) + (c^2p^2 - 2cdp + d^2) \le 0$$
  2. Express each grouped trinomial as a perfect square:$$(ap - b)^2 + (bp - c)^2 + (cp - d)^2 \le 0$$
  3. Since the sum of squares of real numbers is always non-negative, the only way the sum can be less than or equal to $0$ is if each individual square is exactly $0$:$$(ap - b)^2 = 0, \quad (bp - c)^2 = 0, \quad (cp - d)^2 = 0$$
  4. Solve for $p$ in each equation:$$ap - b = 0 \implies p = \frac{b}{a}$$$$bp - c = 0 \implies p = \frac{c}{b}$$$$cp - d = 0 \implies p = \frac{d}{c}$$
  5. Equate the expressions for $p$:$$\frac{b}{a} = \frac{c}{b} = \frac{d}{c} = p$$

Final Evaluation Output: Since the consecutive ratios are equal ($\frac{b}{a} = \frac{c}{b} = \frac{d}{c}$), the numbers $a, b, c, d$ form a geometric progression (G.P.).

Historical Chronicle & Summary

Historical Chronicle: Evolution of Sequences and Series

  • Babylonian & Greek Foundations: Evidence shows that Babylonians ($\approx$ 4000 years ago) were familiar with arithmetic and geometric sequences. Early Greek writers also documented these progressions, as noted by Boethius (510 AD).
  • Indian Contributions: Aryabhata (476 AD) formulated the sum of squares and cubes of natural numbers in his seminal work Aryabhatiyam (499 AD), alongside formulas for arithmetic progressions starting from the $p$-th term. Brahmagupta (598 AD), Mahavira (850 AD), and Bhaskara (1114–1185 AD) expanded on these summations.
  • The Fibonacci Sequence: Leonardo Fibonacci (1170–1250 AD) introduced his famous sequence to model biological growth patterns.
  • Modern Rigor: In 1671, James Gregory introduced the term "infinite series". The rigorous formulation of sequences and series was achieved in the 19th century through set-theoretic and algebraic tools.
