Introduction & Concept of Dispersion

Statistics deals with the collection, analysis, interpretation, and presentation of numerical data. While measures of central tendency (mean, median, and mode) provide a representative value around which data points cluster, they are insufficient to describe the spread or variability of the observations.

Limitations of Central Tendency

Two datasets can have the exact same mean and median but completely different distributions. Consider the runs scored by two batsmen over ten matches:

  • Batsman A: $30, 91, 0, 64, 42, 80, 30, 5, 117, 71$ (Mean = $53$, Median = $53$)
  • Batsman B: $53, 46, 48, 50, 53, 53, 58, 60, 57, 52$ (Mean = $53$, Median = $53$)

Batsman B is highly consistent, with scores clustered tightly around the mean, whereas Batsman A is highly volatile, with scores widely scattered. To quantify this variability, we require measures of dispersion.

Range

The simplest measure of dispersion is the Range, defined as the difference between the maximum and minimum values in a dataset:

$$\text{Range} = \text{Maximum value} - \text{Minimum value}$$
  • Range of Batsman A: $117 - 0 = 117$
  • Range of Batsman B: $60 - 46 = 14$

While easy to calculate, the range is highly sensitive to outliers and does not reflect the dispersion of data points between the extremes.

Mean Deviation

The Mean Deviation is the arithmetic mean of the absolute values of the deviations of observations from a measure of central tendency (usually the mean or median).

Mean Deviation for Ungrouped Data

Let $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ be $n$ observations. The mean deviation about a central value $a$ is given by:

$$\text{M.D.}(a) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x_i - a|$$
  • Mean Deviation about Mean ($\bar{x}$): $\text{M.D.}(\bar{x}) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x_i - \bar{x}|$
  • Mean Deviation about Median ($M$): $\text{M.D.}(M) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x_i - M|$

Mathematical Problem Statement: Find the mean deviation about the mean for the data: $6, 7, 10, 12, 13, 4, 8, 12$.

Step-by-Step Algorithmic Solution Trace:

  1. Calculate the mean ($\bar{x}$): $$\bar{x} = \frac{6 + 7 + 10 + 12 + 13 + 4 + 8 + 12}{8} = \frac{72}{8} = 9$$
  2. Find the absolute deviations $|x_i - \bar{x}|$: $$|6-9|, |7-9|, |10-9|, |12-9|, |13-9|, |4-9|, |8-9|, |12-9| = 3, 2, 1, 3, 4, 5, 1, 3$$
  3. Sum the absolute deviations: $$\sum_{i=1}^{8} |x_i - \bar{x}| = 3 + 2 + 1 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 1 + 3 = 22$$
  4. Compute the Mean Deviation: $$\text{M.D.}(\bar{x}) = \frac{22}{8} = 2.75$$

Final Evaluation Output: $\text{M.D.}(\bar{x}) = 2.75$

Mean Deviation for Grouped Data

For grouped data, observations $x_i$ occur with corresponding frequencies $f_i$.

  • Discrete Frequency Distribution: $$\text{M.D.}(\bar{x}) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i |x_i - \bar{x}|, \quad \text{where } N = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i$$ $$\text{M.D.}(M) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i |x_i - M|$$
  • Continuous Frequency Distribution: Classes are converted to mid-points $x_i$, and the same discrete formulas are applied. The median $M$ is calculated using: $$M = l + \left(\frac{\frac{N}{2} - C}{f}\right) \times h$$ where $l$ is the lower limit of the median class, $C$ is the cumulative frequency of the preceding class, $f$ is the frequency of the median class, and $h$ is the class width.
Limitations of Mean Deviation
  • The absolute value mathematical operation $|x_i - a|$ is not easily subjected to further algebraic treatment.
  • If the degree of variability in a series is very high, the median may not remain a representative central tendency, making the mean deviation about the median unreliable.
Variance and Standard Deviation

To overcome the algebraic limitations of absolute values, we square the deviations. The mean of the squared deviations is called the Variance, and its positive square root is the Standard Deviation ($\sigma$).

Ungrouped Data Formulas
  • Variance ($\sigma^2$): $$\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \bar{x})^2$$
  • Standard Deviation ($\sigma$): $$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \bar{x})^2}$$
Grouped Data Formulas
  • Variance ($\sigma^2$): $$\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i (x_i - \bar{x})^2$$
  • Standard Deviation ($\sigma$): $$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i (x_i - \bar{x})^2}$$
  • Alternative Computational Formula: $$\sigma = \frac{1}{N} \sqrt{N \sum f_i x_i^2 - \left(\sum f_i x_i\right)^2}$$
Shortcut Method (Step-Deviation)

When mid-points $x_i$ or frequencies $f_i$ are large, we use step-deviations $y_i = \frac{x_i - A}{h}$, where $A$ is an assumed mean and $h$ is the class width.

  • Mean: $\bar{x} = A + h \bar{y}$
  • Variance ($\sigma_x^2$): $$\sigma_x^2 = \frac{h^2}{N^2} \left[ N \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i y_i^2 - \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i y_i\right)^2 \right]$$
  • Standard Deviation ($\sigma_x$): $$\sigma_x = \frac{h}{N} \sqrt{N \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i y_i^2 - \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i y_i\right)^2}$$

Historical Chronicle

Historical Chronicle: Evolution of Statistics

  • Etymology: Derived from the Latin word status, meaning a political state.
  • John Graunt (1620-1674): Known as the father of vital statistics for his systematic study of births and deaths in London.
  • Karl Pearson (1857-1936): Contributed significantly to mathematical statistics, introducing the Chi-square test and establishing the first university statistics department.
  • Sir Ronald A. Fisher (1890-1962): Known as the "Father of modern statistics," he applied statistical methods to genetics, agriculture, and experimental design.
